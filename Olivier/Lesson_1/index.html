02 · EXECUTIVE VERB COACHING

Learn the verb before you are asked to use it.

Powerful verbs only help if you understand what they mean, when they fit and what evidence supports them.

LED

lead

Meaning: direct people, activity or change.

Use when: you had responsibility and decision authority.

“I led the integration of two healthcare sites.”

DROVE

drive

Meaning: actively push progress or change.

Use when: you created momentum, not only supervised.

“I drove operational improvement across several facilities.”

DELIVERED

deliver

Meaning: achieve a concrete outcome.

Use when: the result matters more than the process.

“I delivered a successful quality-certification programme.”

IMPLEMENTED

implement

Meaning: put a plan, system or process into practice.

Use when: you moved from decision to real operation.

“I implemented new care and performance processes.”

SECURED

secure

Meaning: successfully obtain or protect something important.

Use when: accreditation, authorization, funding, agreement or continuity was obtained.

“I secured high-level quality certification.”

LAUNCHED

launch

Meaning: start a new service, project or activity.

Use when: the initiative was genuinely new.

“I launched new clinical services and care pathways.”

STREAMLINED

streamline

Meaning: simplify a process to make it more efficient.

Use when: you reduced friction, delay or unnecessary steps.

“I streamlined operational processes across sites.”

ALIGNED

align

Meaning: make teams, goals or processes work toward the same objective.

Use when: stakeholders had different priorities.

“I aligned medical, operational and financial priorities.”

04 · GRAMMAR WITH A PROFESSIONAL PURPOSE

Past evidence → present value → future contribution.

The grammar should help you tell a coherent professional story — not become a disconnected rule list.

PAST EVIDENCE

Past simple

Use for completed achievements.

I + past verb + result

“I led two hospital sites and secured quality certification.”

CAREER EXPERIENCE

Present perfect

Use for experience that still matters now.

I have + past participle

“I have managed complex healthcare transformations for more than twenty years.”

VALUE NOW

Present simple

Use for what your experience enables you to do.

My experience helps me + verb

“My experience helps me identify operational risks before implementation.”

FUTURE CONTRIBUTION

Would / could

Use to discuss value without overclaiming.

I could / would + base verb

“I could help a MedTech team translate clinical needs into an implementation plan.”

Build a 4-step executive answer

1 · Evidence→2 · Experience→3 · Transferable value→4 · Future contribution

“I led multi-site healthcare organizations in France and managed major transformation projects. I have worked extensively with quality, risk, medical recruitment and service development. This experience helps me understand what healthcare teams need when a new solution is introduced. In an Israeli or international MedTech environment, I could help connect strategic objectives with operational reality.”

Choose the best tense: “I ___ several hospital transformation projects during my career.”

Completed achievement in 2024–2025: “I ___ two sites and secured high-quality certification.”

Professional value now: “My operational background ___ me identify adoption risks.”

Writing transfer — 120–150 words

Prompt: A MedTech company asks you to explain what healthcare organizations should consider before implementing a new digital solution. Write a concise professional response.

Show writing plan
  1. Opening position
  2. Operational and clinical needs
  3. Stakeholder / workflow considerations
  4. Risk, quality and local requirements
  5. Implementation and adoption
  6. Concise conclusion
Show model answer

Before implementing a new digital-health solution, a healthcare organization should first confirm that the technology addresses a real clinical or operational need. The project team should identify the main stakeholders, understand existing workflows and define clear success criteria. It is also important to assess quality, risk, data and local regulatory requirements with the appropriate specialists. From an operational perspective, implementation should include communication, training, governance and follow-up. A technically strong solution can still fail if teams do not understand how it improves their work or patient care. In my experience, successful transformation requires both strategic direction and practical adoption. The objective is therefore not simply to install a solution, but to integrate it safely and effectively into everyday healthcare operations.
