01 · Before the first question

You are not proving that you know every answer.

You are showing how you think, support, prioritise, communicate and learn. The interviewers already have your CV. Your job is to make the evidence behind it easy to hear.

Monday objectiveMake them picture you in the role.
1

Answer first

Start with your position, not the backstory. “Yes — I have done this regularly…”

2

Choose one proof

Cartier onboarding, Chloé coordination, a complex diary, a seminar, a difficult follow-up.

3

Use 3 cue words

Do not write a script. Prepare three words: context · action · impact.

4

Keep it 45–75 sec

Enough detail to be credible; short enough to invite a follow-up.

5

Pause without apologising

“Let me think about the best example.” Silence is more professional than filling every second.

6

Finish with relevance

“That experience would help me support both directors with reliable visibility.”

7

Unknown word? Describe it

“I don’t know the exact technical term yet, but I mean the stage where…”

8

Stay yourself

The model answer is a reference after you speak — never the sentence you must reproduce.

02 · The motivation signal

Show that your interest has grown because you did the work.

Your research matters. The strongest version is not “I have read everything about Hermès.” It is: the more I learned, the clearer the fit became.

1 · Attraction

What genuinely draws you in?

Supporting two senior leaders, a demanding collection environment, transversal coordination, luxury standards.

2 · Evidence of curiosity

What did your research teach you?

Talk about the house’s emphasis on craftsmanship, transmission of savoir-faire, quality, long-term thinking and the importance of learning the métier.

3 · Contribution

Why does your background fit?

You already know how to create structure around senior leaders, coordinate stakeholders and anticipate needs in luxury environments.

Build your own version

Select ideas — then speak, do not read.

Astuce : dites que votre recherche a renforcé votre motivation, puis donnez un exemple précis de ce que vous avez compris. Ne cherchez pas à impressionner avec des faits appris par cœur.

Avoid

“I love Hermès and I know everything about the company.”

Better

“The more I have learned about the role and the way the métier operates, the more motivated I have become.”

Even better

“What attracts me is the combination of high standards, very concrete coordination and a culture where learning the métier matters.”

05 · Accuracy lab

Fix the errors most likely to appear under pressure.

Short rules, immediate feedback. Choices are mixed each time — the correct answer is not always in the same position.

01

Experience until now

have/has + past participle + for + duration

“I have worked in the luxury industry for more than ten years.”

for ten years ✓since 2015 ✓since ten years ✗
02

Dates & places

on Monday · in August · at 9 a.m.

Use at for a point/place: at Cartier; in for an industry/team when natural: in luxury, in the team.

03

Strong answer structure

Subject + verb + detail + reason/result

“I organised the onboarding schedule so that the new director could become operational quickly.”

04

Hypothetical pressure

If + past → would + base verb

“If both directors had urgent requests, I would clarify the business impact and propose an order.”

09 · The last five minutes

Leave them with motivation, judgement and a good question.

A strong finish does not repeat your whole interview. It gives one clear final signal and then turns the conversation back to them.

If they ask: “Is there anything you would like to add?”

A natural closing structure

  1. Thank them for the conversation.
  2. Confirm the fit you see between the role and your experience.
  3. Express motivation that has grown through your preparation.
  4. End forward: “I would be very pleased to bring that experience to the team.”

“Thank you. The conversation has reinforced my interest in the role. I can see a strong connection between what you need — coordination, anticipation and support for two senior leaders — and the way I have worked throughout my career. I have also really enjoyed learning more about the shoe métier during my preparation, and that has made me even more motivated to join the team and continue learning.”
