GRAMMAR STRATEGY LADDER

One interview answer can move through three time zones

Use tense as a storytelling tool: career overview → concrete evidence → professional value today.

01 · ESSENTIAL
NOW

Know the timeline

Present = how I work now.
Past = one finished experience.
Present perfect = career experience connected to now.

I manage priorities. · I worked at Chloé. · I have worked in executive support for more than ten years.
02 · INTERVIEW-READY
STORY

Choose the tense that proves your point

Use the past simple for a specific completed example, and the present perfect to connect your accumulated career experience to the position you want now.

I have managed complex priorities throughout my career. At Cartier, I coordinated the onboarding of a new director.
03 · STRONGER
SHIFT

Switch tenses deliberately

Move from present perfect for the career overview, to past simple for evidence, then to present simple for the habits and value you bring today.

I have supported senior leaders for more than ten years. At Cartier, I managed complex executive priorities. Today, I work proactively and anticipate needs.

07 · NEVER FREEZE · 5 MIN

Say it another way

05 min

Your interview problem is not always “I do not know.” Sometimes it is “I know, but I cannot find the exact word.” The solution is paraphrase, simplify, continue.

BUY TIME

That’s an interesting question. Let me think about the best example.

CHECK

If I understand correctly, you’re asking about…

PARAPHRASE

It’s an activity where… / It’s a person who… / It’s a document that…

RESTART

Let me put that another way.

THE “DUBBING” EXAMPLE — THREE WAYS
Safe & clear

We organised a team activity with a short cartoon. Each group created new words for the characters.

Interview-ready

During the seminar, we organised a team activity using a short extract from an animated film. Each team invented new dialogue for the characters and performed it.

Stronger professional

One of the activities was a creative dubbing exercise: teams worked from a short animated sequence, created their own dialogue and performed it together. The objective was collaboration and creativity rather than technical performance.

Self-review — choose evidence, not feelings

Safe

Content: I gave my years of experience. Language: use “have worked”. Delivery: speak more slowly.

Interview-ready

Content: I connected Cartier to the role. Language: use “for more than ten years”. Delivery: pause after my headline instead of filling the silence.

Stronger

Content: my evidence was relevant but my final connection to the role was weak. Language: replace repeated “organise” with “coordinate” once. Delivery: finish the answer decisively instead of adding an unnecessary final sentence.
