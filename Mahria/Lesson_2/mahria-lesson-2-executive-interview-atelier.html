01 · Personal error clinic

Make your English sound like you — not like translated French

These patterns come directly from your Lesson 1 work. The objective is automatic correction, not memorisation.

FR → EN

“It’s important to get to know them…”

→

“I make it a priority to understand each leader’s working style.”

Lead with I + action, not an abstract “It is important to…” opening.
FR → EN

“I adapt to them.”

→

“I adapt my support to each person’s style.”

Name what you adapt: your support, approach, communication or priorities.
Precision

→

“I onboarded…, organised…, coordinated…, and prepared…”

Spoken interview English prefers subject + verb, not administrative noun lists.

→

“Before my new director arrived, I had already structured her agenda and key meetings.”

Replace an adjective with an observable fact whenever possible.

02 · Grammar that changes how you sound

Three rules for executive interview precision

You already know much of this grammar. The difference today is learning to choose it quickly while speaking.

01

Past simple vs present perfect

Past simple = a finished action in a finished time or a specific story.

I organised · I coordinated · I prepared · I resolved

Present perfect = experience or development connected to now, with no finished time stated.

I have supported · I have worked · I have developed

✓ I organised two seminars during my Cartier assignment.✓ I have worked in demanding luxury environments.

Past simple = événement terminé et situé. Present perfect = expérience liée au présent, sans moment terminé précis.

02

Real method vs hypothetical response

Use the present simple for the way you genuinely work now.

I prioritise · I check · I clarify · I keep both leaders informed.

Use would + base verb when the interviewer gives you a hypothetical situation.

I would clarify · I would assess · I would propose

“How do you manage competing priorities?” → I start by clarifying urgency.“What would you do if both directors needed you?” → I would first clarify the true deadline.

Présent = votre méthode réelle. Would + verbe = ce que vous feriez dans une situation hypothétique.

03

Verb-led professional English

Strong spoken English makes you the actor.

I + action verb + object + purpose/result

anticipatedcoordinatedstreamlinedpreparedonboardedescalatedresolvedaligned

Upgrade: “Organisation of two seminars” → “I organised two seminars and coordinated the logistics from planning through delivery.”

À l’oral, évitez les listes nominales de type CV. Utilisez “I + verbe d’action”.

Duration refresher for + duration → for ten years / for six months since + starting point → since 2013 / since January ago + past simple → I joined the company six months ago.

03 · Evidence bank

Turn a quality into proof in 20 seconds

A recruiter cannot see “proactive” or “reliable.” Give them something they can picture.

QUALITY→ACTION→CONTEXT→RESULT / VALUE

Anticipation · Cartier

New director onboarding

Before the new director arrived, you had already structured key meetings, contacts, visits and onboarding steps.

“Anticipation is one of my strengths. For example, before my new director arrived at Cartier, I had already structured her first meetings, key contacts and onboarding schedule so that she could become operational quickly.”

Coordination · Chloé

Studio ↔ operational teams

You coordinated across different stakeholders to keep collection planning moving.

“I’m comfortable coordinating across different teams. At Chloé, I worked between the design studio and operational teams to support meetings, presentation materials and collection planning.”

Persistence + judgement · budget issue

Escalating at the right moment

A cost-allocation error remained unresolved for months until you prepared the facts and involved your director.

“I’m persistent, but I have also learned the importance of escalating at the right moment. In one budget issue, I followed up repeatedly, then prepared a precise summary for my director. Once she intervened, the issue was resolved immediately.”

Executive support · Cartier

Complex calendars + CODIR

Your experience already includes senior-level calendar coordination, travel, executive documents and committee interaction.

“I’m used to creating structure around senior leaders. At Cartier, I managed complex calendars, coordinated with Comité de Direction stakeholders and prepared executive documents while priorities were moving.”

04 · STAR without sounding scripted

Three stories you can reuse for ten different questions

You do not need ten stories. You need a small number of strong examples you can adapt to the question.

05 · Supporting two senior leaders

Prioritise without sounding passive or defensive

This role is not only about managing two calendars. It is about creating visibility, clarifying urgency and making trade-offs professionally.

1

Clarify

What is truly urgent? What is fixed? What can move?

2

Assess impact

Which deadline affects more people, business continuity or a key decision?

3

Propose

Bring a solution, not only a conflict.

4

Communicate

Keep both leaders informed so neither feels support is reactive.

Live scenario90 seconds

The Industrial Director asks for an urgent supplier meeting at 10:00. The Development Director has already asked you to protect that same slot for a collection review involving several teams.

What would you do?

Structure: clarify urgency → identify constraints → propose alternatives → communicate transparently.

“I would first clarify the real urgency and constraints behind both requests rather than deciding from the calendar alone. If the supplier issue genuinely required immediate attention, I would look for a way to protect the collection review — for example by moving only the director’s participation, adjusting the supplier time or finding a delegate where appropriate. I would then confirm the agreed solution with both directors so that the prioritisation is transparent.”

06 · When the word disappears

Do not stop. Change the route.

Your previous seminar example became difficult because one specific activity was hard to describe. In an interview, the goal is not to translate the perfect French word — it is to keep the meaning moving.

A

Use a simpler category

“It was a team-building activity…”

B

Describe what people did

“Participants worked in small groups and created a new dialogue…”

C

Explain the purpose

“The aim was to encourage creativity and teamwork.”

D

Move on

Do not keep searching once the interviewer understands.

The exact situation that blocked you before

Describe the dubbing activity without needing the word “dubbing”

“We organised a team-building activity using a short animated video. In small groups, participants created a new dialogue for the characters and performed it while the video played. The aim was to encourage creativity and teamwork.”

10 · The interview becomes a conversation

Questions you can ask the two directors

A strong question shows that you are already thinking about how the role works, not only whether you will get it.

Success

“What would make you say after the first three months that the person in this role has made a real difference?”

Two leaders

“How do you currently divide priorities and information between the Industrial and Development sides of the role?”

Coordination

“Where do you see the biggest coordination challenge between the teams in France and Italy?”

Collections

“During the four collection cycles, when does the assistant role become most critical for you?”

Follow-up habit: If their answer gives you a connection to your experience, react briefly before asking the next question: “That’s helpful — I had a similar coordination point at Chloé…”

11 · Final mission

12-minute interview sprint

Coach mode off. Notes closed. Models closed. Your job is not to be perfect — it is to stay clear, specific and in control.

  4. How would you manage conflicting priorities from two directors?
