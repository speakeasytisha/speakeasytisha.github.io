02 · FRANCE → LONDON

Book the family journey

Work like a traveller on a booking website or with a travel adviser. Choose the dates and passengers first, then compare the whole journey: getting to the departure point, crossing to Britain, arriving, and reaching your hotel.

TRIP PLANNER · TRAINING SIMULATORPlanning prices / current “from” fares — always check live before booking.
OPTION A · PASSENGER TRAIN

🚆 France → Paris → Eurostar → London St Pancras

How you book

1. Search your French rail journey to Paris.
2. Search Paris Gare du Nord → London St Pancras International on Eurostar.
3. Enter outward/return dates and every passenger.
4. Compare departure times and fare conditions.
5. Allow enough time in Paris for the international departure process.
6. Pay and keep the confirmation/tickets accessible.

What the journey is like

Travel to Paris, reach Gare du Nord, complete international departure formalities, board Eurostar and travel directly into central London. Paris–London itself is about 2h17. This option brings you directly into central London without your car.

Planning cost

Eurostar currently advertises Paris–London from about €44 one way per person. This is a “from” fare, not a guaranteed family total. Add the journey to Paris and London local transport. Popular dates may cost more.

You end up: London St Pancras International → choose Tube, taxi/black cab, bus or another local option to the training hotel near Covent Garden.
Booking English: outward journey · return journey · one-way/single · return/round trip · passenger · adult · child · teenager · departure · arrival · connection · direct train · flexible fare · non-refundable · seat · luggage
OPTION B · CAR + VEHICLE SHUTTLE

🚗 France → Calais → LeShuttle → Folkestone → London

How you book

Select Calais → Folkestone, dates/times, vehicle details and passengers on LeShuttle, then choose the fare type. Keep the booking reference ready for check-in.

What happens

Drive to Calais, check in and complete border controls, drive onto the shuttle train, remain with your vehicle, then drive off in Folkestone. The crossing itself is about 35 minutes. Continue by road towards London.

Planning cost

Fares vary by dates, duration and ticket. Current short-trip promotions can start around €71 per vehicle each way under specific conditions. Check the actual family dates. Add fuel, road costs, parking and any London driving costs.

You end up: Folkestone, Kent, with your car — not central London. You still need to drive into London and plan parking/restrictions.
OPTION C · CAR + FERRY

⛴️ France → Calais → Dover → London

How you book

Choose route, dates, vehicle type and passenger numbers on the ferry operator's booking site. Select a sailing, enter passenger/vehicle details, review conditions and pay.

What happens

Drive to the port, check in, pass border controls and board with the vehicle. Leave the car during the crossing and use passenger areas. Calais–Dover takes roughly 100 minutes, then continue towards London.

Planning cost

DFDS currently advertises Calais–Dover from around €114 one way per car with up to five people, subject to availability/conditions. Add fuel and the onward drive.

You end up: Dover, Kent. The onward road journey to London is substantial and traffic-sensitive.

Booking complications

1 · The cheap train is too early.

Ask the price difference and decide.

How much more is the later train for the four of us? If the difference isn't too large, I'd prefer the later departure because getting to Paris that early would be difficult.
2 · You need flexibility.

Ask about exchanges and refunds.

Could you tell me what happens if we need to change our travel date? Is this fare exchangeable, and would we have to pay a fare difference? Is it refundable?
3 · Train or car?

Explain why the cheapest crossing may not be the cheapest complete journey.

We need to compare the total cost rather than just the crossing. With the car, we'd include fuel, the Channel crossing, the drive into London and parking. With the train, we'd include the journey to Paris, Eurostar and local transport from St Pancras.

06 · GRAMMAR LAB

Four structures for the whole trip

This is a reactivation lab: rule → form → signal words → common mistake → controlled practice → personal transfer.

01 · FUTURE PLAN

BE GOING TO

Use be going to + base verb for an intention or plan already in your mind.

FormLondon example
+subject + am/is/are + going to + verbWe’re going to visit Greenwich.
−subject + am/is/are + not + going to + verbWe aren’t going to drive in central London.
?Am/Is/Are + subject + going to + verb?Are you going to book the museum?

Signal ideas: tomorrow · next week · this weekend · later · tonight · on Saturday. Contractions: I’m, you’re, we’re, isn’t, aren’t.

Common mistake: ❌ We going to visit. → ✓ We are going to visit.

02 · WISH + POLITE CHOICE

WOULD LIKE TO

Use would like + noun or would like to + verb. It is softer than “want” and extremely useful in hotels, restaurants and travel situations.

FormExample
+subject + would like to + verbI’d like to visit the observatory.
−subject + would not like to + verbI wouldn’t like to rush the museum.
?Would + subject + like to + verb?Would you like to take the boat?

Useful chunks: I’d like… · We’d like… · Would you like…? · I’d rather… · I’d prefer…

Common mistake: ❌ I would like visit. → ✓ I would like to visit.

PRESENT PERFECT

Use have/has + past participle for experience connected to now when the finished past time is not stated.

FormExample

09 · REAL LONDON ENCOUNTERS

Talk to Londoners

Listen for the key information, clarify when necessary, then respond naturally. You do not need perfect English to manage a real conversation well.

1 · On the street

You: Excuse me, are we going the right way for Covent Garden?
Local: Yeah, pretty much. Keep going straight, take the second left, then it's just past the piazza. You can't really miss it.

Notice: “pretty much” = more or less; “you can't miss it” = it is easy to recognise.

Your task: Check that you understood the second turn.

Thanks. So we go straight ahead and take the second left — is that right?

2 · In a café

Server: Hi there. Are you ready to order, or do you need another minute?
You: ______

Your task: You need more time, then ask whether one dish contains nuts.

Could we have another minute, please? And could you tell me if this dish contains nuts?
We’re almost ready, but could we have another minute? I also wanted to check whether this dish contains any nuts.

3 · At the Tube station

Staff: The Piccadilly line is part suspended, so you'd be better off taking the Northern line and changing at Leicester Square.

Decode: “part suspended” = not running on part of the line; “you'd be better off…” = this is the better option.

Your task: Ask where to change and whether there is step-free access.

Okay, thank you. Where exactly do we need to change? And is there step-free access on that route? We have luggage with us.

4 · Ask for a recommendation

You: We're with two children and we'd like to do something interesting this afternoon, but not another big museum. What would you recommend?
Local: If the weather holds, I'd head down to the South Bank. You can walk along the river, there's loads going on, and you could always hop on a boat towards Greenwich.

Decode: “if the weather holds” = if the weather stays good; “loads going on” = lots happening; “hop on” = take/get on casually.

Your task: React and ask one follow-up question.

That sounds perfect. How long would you allow for the walk, and where would be the best place to catch the boat?

Communication repair toolkit

Real English is not always clear the first time. Practise: Sorry, I didn't quite catch that. · Could you say that again a little more slowly? · Do you mean…? · So, if I've understood correctly… · What does ___ mean?

Scenario: A local gives you fast directions and you only understand half. Interrupt politely and rebuild the information.

Sorry, I didn't quite catch the last part. Could you say that again a little more slowly? So, if I've understood correctly, we cross the road, go past the station and then turn right after the café?
