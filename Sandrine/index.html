04 · HOW YOU LEARN

Your learning conditions are part of the programme.

The method should help you access English, not force you into one fixed way of learning.

YOUR PREFERENCES
▶

Listen first, then read

You like hearing English before looking at the written version. We can use audio first, then a transcript or text to confirm what you heard.

A

See a model before speaking

A useful example gives you a secure starting point. Then you can adapt it instead of having to create everything from nothing.

↺

Use another English example

When something is difficult, a second example in English can often make the meaning clearer without immediately switching languages.

✓

Sometimes immediate correction is useful; sometimes reformulation or a short grammar explanation works better. We can mix approaches.

✎

You are interested in homework, especially writing. Short tasks can help you reactivate vocabulary and prepare ideas before speaking.

↗

07 · TENTATIVE PROGRAMME

A 30-hour working plan — designed to change when useful.

This is not a contract with the sequence. It is a pedagogical direction based on what you told me in our first session.

30 HOURS · FLEXIBLE ORDER
FLEXIBLE BY DESIGN

We can spend longer on a useful area, shorten something you already control, move a travel topic earlier, or adapt the programme around a future trip, a new interest or the final assessment once confirmed.

MODULE 1 · HOURS 1–4Reconnect & build confidence

Reactivate the English you already have and create secure speaking habits.

  • Your English story and natural self-expression
  • Question forms and conversation building
  • Present / past / future essentials in real contexts
  • Confidence strategies: pause, clarify, reformulate
OUTCOME · Speak for longer with a clearer structure and less hesitation.
MODULE 2 · HOURS 5–10Travel essentials

Build reusable English for the situations you identified as priorities.

  • Airport and train journeys
  • Hotels and accommodation rentals
  • Car hire and directions
  • Restaurants, bookings and requests
  • Ask for information and handle simple problems
  • Polite requests, questions and useful travel grammar
OUTCOME · Manage the core stages of a trip more independently.
MODULE 3 · HOURS 11–15Understand real spoken English

Make listening feel less fragile and more strategic.

  • Listen first → read second
  • British and American accents and vocabulary
  • Key-word listening instead of word-for-word translation
  • Clarification and “keep going” phrases
  • Pronunciation patterns that improve both speaking and listening
OUTCOME · Stay in the conversation even when you do not understand every word.
MODULE 4 · HOURS 16–20Grammar & vocabulary that transfer

Consolidate language through topics you can actually talk about.

  • Sentence structure and word order
  • Useful verb forms and time markers
  • Prepositions, questions and connectors
  • Vocabulary by meaningful category
  • Short writing tasks to prepare stronger speaking
OUTCOME · Build clearer, more accurate sentences with less effort.
MODULE 5 · HOURS 21–26Broader conversation & London project

Use English more freely across travel and personal-interest topics.

  • Films, music, hiking, environment, space and oceans
  • Express opinions, compare, agree and disagree
  • Tell stories and describe experiences
  • Plan a London family trip: transport, accommodation, activities and unexpected situations
  • Integrated listening + reading + speaking + writing
OUTCOME · Move beyond prepared answers into more natural conversation.
MODULE 6 · HOURS 27–30Autonomy, consolidation & final preparation

Pull the programme together and make your progress usable beyond the lessons.

  • Personal high-frequency vocabulary and grammar review
  • Longer mixed-skill scenarios with less support
  • Personal “survival + confidence” phrase toolkit
  • Final-assessment practice once the format is confirmed
  • Progress review and next-step strategy
OUTCOME · Leave with a clearer sense of what you can do and how to keep progressing independently.
IMPORTANT

The hours above are a guide, not a rigid allocation. Your progress decides the real pace.

The roadmap gives us direction. Your progress gives us the next step.

You do not have to remember the whole programme. Each lesson will simply take the next useful step.

OUR WORKING AGREEMENT

You bring your goals and your experience. I bring the structure, guidance and practice.

We will keep the lessons practical, supportive and progressive. When something is useful, we will build on it. When something is already secure, we will move forward. When your priorities change, the programme can change too.

LESSON 2 DIRECTION

Reconnect + travel confidence

A natural next step is to reactivate question forms and sentence building through a concrete travel situation, with models first and freer speaking at the end.

BETWEEN LESSONS

Short writing, when useful

A small written task can help you prepare vocabulary or ideas before the next conversation. Homework can stay flexible rather than automatic.

LISTENING

Keep your BBC habit

Listen first. Notice the words you recognise. Then read or check the transcript. Bring one useful expression into the next lesson.

PROGRESS

We will review, not just continue

Every few lessons, we will look at what feels easier, what still needs support and whether the roadmap should change.

FOR YOU

You already have a real history with English. This programme is not about proving what you do or do not know. It is about helping the language become available to you again — more clearly, more confidently and more independently.
