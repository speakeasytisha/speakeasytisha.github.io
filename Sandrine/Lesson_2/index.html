06 · BEFORE, DURING & AFTER

Choose the right time

Going to — intentions already decided

+ We are going to stay in London. · − We aren’t going to rent a car. · ? Are we going to book breakfast?

Signal ideas: next month, this weekend, tomorrow. Use am/is/are + going to + base verb.

Present continuous — arrangements

+ We are arriving on Friday. · − We aren’t leaving early. · ? Are we checking in at three?

Use am/is/are + verb-ing, often with a fixed date/time. Contrast: intention (going to) versus arranged detail (present continuous).

Present simple — hotel facts & routines

+ Breakfast starts at seven. · − The hotel doesn’t offer parking. · ? Does the hotel have a lift?

For he/she/it, add -s in affirmative statements; after does/doesn’t, use the base verb.

Present continuous — what is happening now

+ We are waiting at reception. · − The key card isn’t working. · ? Is the lift working?

Signal ideas: now, at the moment, currently. One form can describe present action or future arrangement; context tells you which.

Past simple — what happened during your stay

+ We checked in yesterday. · − We didn’t receive a receipt. · ? Did you leave your bag at reception?

Use did + base verb for questions and negatives. Signal ideas: yesterday, last night, this morning (when finished).

07 · WRITING STUDIO

Emails and messages that work

Reliable email structure

Subject → greeting → reservation context → precise questions → polite closing → name.

Useful phrases: I am writing to enquire about… / Could you please confirm…? / Would it be possible to…? / I would appreciate it if… / Thank you in advance for your help.

For informal hotel chat: Hello, we have a booking for… Could you let us know…? Many thanks!

08 · HOTEL LIFE

Check in, settle in, find your way

Your check-in sequence

1. Greet → 2. Give reservation name → 3. Spell your name/reference → 4. Confirm room and dates → 5. Ask about payment and breakfast → 6. Ask for directions → 7. Thank the receptionist.

Numbers: room 314 = “three one four”; third floor = “third floor”; £185 = “one hundred and eighty-five pounds”; 14/40 = fourTEEN / FORty.

UK/US: ground floor (UK) = first floor (US); lift (UK) = elevator (US); reception (UK) = front desk (US).

10 · YOUR COMPLETE HOTEL CHALLENGE

From booking to the final goodbye

This is your interactive hotel simulation. Complete all six checkpoints, listen to the dialogue, choose your responses and write your own final message. You can retry every question and reveal a hint.

🎤 Final role-play · Your entire stay

Introduce yourself, confirm the room, ask about payment and breakfast, resolve a problem, request a local recommendation and arrange check-out. Include at least five polite requests.

Clear model:

Hello. We have a reservation for a family room. Could you confirm our dates? Is breakfast included? Where is the lift? Our Wi-Fi is not working. Could someone help us? Could you recommend a family-friendly restaurant? Can we leave our luggage here after check-out? Thank you very much.

Stretch model:

Good afternoon. We have a reservation under our family name for four nights. Would you mind confirming the room configuration and whether breakfast is included? Could you also explain how to reach the room? I’m afraid the Wi-Fi connection seems to be unreliable; would it be possible for someone to look into it? We would love to discover a memorable local restaurant, preferably somewhere family-friendly. Is there a place you would personally recommend? Finally, as our return train leaves in the evening, could we arrange luggage storage after check-out? Thank you so much for your help.
