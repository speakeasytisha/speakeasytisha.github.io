Teach first · grammar for weather and plans

NOW vs IN GENERAL

It's raining. (right now)
It rains a lot in London. (in general)

⚠️ Not It rains now.

Présent continu pour maintenant ; présent simple pour une généralité.

PREDICTIONS

It's going to rain. — I can see dark clouds (evidence).
It will be sunny tomorrow. — the forecast says so.
It might rain. — maybe (50/50).

going to = indice visible · will = prévision · might = peut-être.

DECISIONS

If it rains, we'll go to the museum.
Since / As it's sunny, let's have a picnic.
It's windy, so we won't take the boat.

⚠️ Not If it will rain.

If + présent, will + verbe. Since/As = puisque ; so = donc.

🌡️ Culture tip: the UK uses Celsius (“It's 18 degrees”). Americans use Fahrenheit: 64°F ≈ 18°C. And the British say brolly for umbrella and sunny spells for “éclaircies”.

06 · HOW DO WE GET THERE?

Plan a real London journey

Teach first · make a polite travel question

Could you tell me how to get to…? · Which line should we take? · Do we need to change? · Which stop should we get off at? · Is it within walking distance?

Directions: go straight on → cross at the pedestrian crossing → turn left / right → go past the pharmacy → it's on your right → opposite / next to / between / on the corner of / at the end of the street.

Question pattern: Could + subject + base verb…? / How + do + we + get…? Negative: We don't need to change. Imperative: Don't cross here!

On your left = sur votre gauche · opposite = en face de · on the corner of = à l'angle de · go past = passez devant.

Real journeys vary with time, closures, route choice and accessibility.

London transport · bus, Tube, DLR, boat and walking

Tube / Underground: find the correct line and direction, tap in and tap out. Bus: tap in only; press the bell to request your stop. DLR: a light railway that is useful for Greenwich (Cutty Sark station). River Bus: a scenic paid boat. Walking: often the easiest way in central London. Black cab: direct, but check the cost.

Family travel: children under 11 travel free. Travellers aged 11–15 may get a Young Visitor discount on a Visitor Oyster card (ask staff to set it). Travellers aged 16+ pay adult fares unless they have a special card. Each adult needs their own card or phone. Always check current rules with TfL.

LANGUAGE LAB · FIRST, UNDERSTAND THE RULE

How much or how many? · Count it or measure it

Before asking about tickets and prices, learn to distinguish quantity from price.

HOW MANY + plural countable noun

Things you can count: tickets, teenagers, stops, lifts, floors, sandwiches, bottles.

How many tickets do we need? — Four tickets.

How many stops are there? — Three stops.

Combien de + nom dénombrable au pluriel.

HOW MUCH + uncountable noun

Things you measure: money, time, water, information, advice, luggage, traffic.

How much time do we have? — About twenty minutes.

How much luggage can we bring? — Two bags each (count the bags, not the luggage).

Combien de + nom indénombrable.

HOW MUCH for prices

How much is it? / How much does it cost? / How much are the tickets?

How much is an adult ticket? — Twenty-two pounds.

How much are four tickets? — Sixty-six pounds.

Pour un prix, toujours how much, même pour plusieurs billets.

Build questions correctly

How many + plural noun + are there? → How many stops are there?
How much + uncountable noun + do we need? → How much water do we need?
How much + does + subject + cost? → How much does admission cost?
How much + are + plural items? → How much are the tickets?

Negatives: We don't have much time. / There aren't many seats left. Questions: Is there much traffic? / Are there many people?

Key exceptions and natural English

Uncountable in English: information, advice, luggage/baggage, furniture, equipment. Count pieces of advice, bags or suitcases instead.

Meaning changes: How much time? (duration) vs How many times? (occasions); How much coffee? (liquid) vs How many coffees? (cups).

Natural alternative: a lot of works with both: We have a lot of time / There are a lot of people.

08 · INSIDE THE ATTRACTION

Floors, signs and finding the loo

UK ↔ US floors · the important difference!

🇬🇧 UK · same as France

Lower ground floor = niveau -1
Ground floor = rez-de-chaussée
First floor = 1er étage
Second floor = 2e étage

🇺🇸 US

Basement = niveau -1
First floor = rez-de-chaussée
Second floor = 1er étage
Third floor = 2e étage

Ordinals: first (1st), second (2nd), third (3rd), fourth (4th), fifth (5th), eleventh (11th), twelfth (12th), twenty-first (21st), twenty-second (22nd), twenty-third (23rd). Exceptions: 11th, 12th, 13th — NOT 11st or 12nd.

UK: lift, toilets / loo (informal), ground floor, queue, way out. US: elevator, restroom / bathroom, first floor, line, exit. In London, “Excuse me, where are the toilets, please?” is perfect.

09 · A FAMILY BREAK

We're starving — lunch for four!

Real-life requests · food, allergies and payment

Could we have a table for four? · Is there somewhere nearby to grab a bite? · Do you have anything vegetarian? · Does this contain nuts? · Is that to eat in or take away? · Could we have the bill, please? (UK) / the check (US).

Countable: two sandwiches, four drinks, some apples. Uncountable: some water, a little cheese, not much time. Would like + noun / to + verb: I'd like a pot of tea. Negative: I wouldn't like anything spicy. Question: Would you like anything else?

British café food: toastie = croque-monsieur grillé · jacket potato = pomme de terre au four · scone = petit pain au lait, servi avec confiture et crème (clotted cream).

11 · TELL THE STORY

Write a believable review

Past simple → present perfect → opinion

Past simple: finished actions at a finished time. (+) We visited the museum yesterday. (−) We didn't wait long. (?) Did you enjoy it? Irregular: go → went; see → saw; buy → bought; take → took; have → had; get → got.

Present perfect: experience/result connected to now; no finished time. (+) We have visited several attractions. (−) We haven't tried the boat yet. (?) Have you ever taken a river bus? Keywords: ever, never, already, just, yet, so far, since, for. Never say “We have visited it yesterday”.

For vs since: for + duration (for two hours); since + starting point (since 10 a.m.). Adjectives describe nouns (a beautiful ship); adverbs describe actions (the guide explained everything clearly). Exception: good → well; fast → fast.

Connectors: first, then, after that, however, although, because, so, finally, overall. Structure: context → what happened → positives → drawbacks → recommendation.
