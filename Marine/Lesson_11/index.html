02 · GRAMMAR LAB

Past simple or present perfect?

Choose the tense by meaning, not by translating word for word.

Past simple · finished time

Rule: Completed event at a known or finished time.

+ I checked the file yesterday.
− I didn’t check it yesterday.
? Did you check the file?

Markers: yesterday, last week, in 2025, two days ago, when.

Watch out: after did, use the base verb: “Did you send?” not “Did you sent?”

Present perfect · connection to now

Rule: Life experience, current result, or unfinished time period.

+ I have checked the file.
− I haven’t checked it yet.
? Have you checked the file?

Markers: ever, never, already, yet, just, so far, since, for.

Watch out: no present perfect with a specific finished time: “I sent it yesterday.”

Key forms, irregular verbs and common exceptions

Past simple: regular verbs + -ed; irregular verbs change (send → sent, go → went). Present perfect: have/has + past participle (send → sent, write → written, go → gone). Use has with he/she/it. Since = starting point; for = duration. “This morning” may be unfinished or finished depending on when you speak and what you mean.
