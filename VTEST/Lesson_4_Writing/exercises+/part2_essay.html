01 · Sentence backbone and word order Teach + try

Form: Subject + verb + object / complement

Affirmative: We sent the report yesterday.

Negative: We did not send the report.

Question: Did we send the report?

FR · Meaning: En anglais, le sujet est normalement exprimé et l’ordre sujet–verbe est essentiel.

Rules, exceptions & common errors: With be: She is ready / She is not ready / Is she ready? Do not use do with be. Adverbs of frequency usually precede the main verb but follow be.

02 · Past simple Teach + try

Form: Subject + past verb (regular -ed / irregular)

Affirmative: We completed the audit last week.

Negative: We did not complete the audit.

Question: Did we complete the audit?

FR · Meaning: Événement terminé à un moment précis du passé : yesterday, last week, in 2024.

Rules, exceptions & common errors: After did / didn’t the main verb returns to its base form. Irregular forms include went, sent, wrote and made. Past of be: was/were, not did be.

Now practise: Which question is correct?

03 · Present perfect Teach + try

Form: Subject + have/has + past participle

Affirmative: The team has finished the report.

Negative: The team has not finished it yet.

Question: Has the team finished it?

FR · Meaning: Résultat ou expérience en lien avec maintenant. Différent du passé simple daté.

Rules, exceptions & common errors: Common markers: already, yet, just, ever, never, for, since. Do not normally combine with a finished time such as yesterday. Irregular participles: written, gone, done. With a finished date, use past simple.

04 · Passive voice — taught from the beginning Teach + try

Form: Receiver + be (correct tense) + past participle (+ by agent)

Affirmative: The meeting was moved to Friday.

Negative: The meeting was not moved.

Question: Was the meeting moved?

FR · Meaning: Voix passive : on met l’action ou son résultat au premier plan, plutôt que la personne qui agit. Active: The manager moved the meeting. Passive: The meeting was moved (by the manager).

Rules, exceptions & common errors: Match tense: is moved / was moved / has been moved / will be moved. The participle does not change with the subject; be does. Irregular participles: sent, written, made, done. Use by only when the agent matters. Not every verb is naturally passive: happen, arrive, sleep are intransitive. Avoid “was happened”.

Now practise: Choose the correctly formed passive.

Choose one answer above, then select “Check answer & explain”.

05 · Questions and auxiliaries Teach + try

Form: Do/Does/Did + subject + base verb?; Be/Have/Modal + subject ...?

Affirmative: Did the client approve the plan?

Negative: The client did not approve the plan.

Question: Did the client approve the plan?

FR · Meaning: Les questions anglaises demandent souvent un auxiliaire avant le sujet.

Rules, exceptions & common errors: No extra do with be or modals: Is she available? Can we help? Have they finished? Wh-questions: Why did the team postpone the meeting? Subject question exception: Who approved the plan? (no did).

Now practise: Choose the correct question.

06 · Modals and recommendations Teach + try

Form: Subject + should/could/must/might + base verb

Affirmative: We should inform the team.

Negative: We should not delay the decision.

Question: Should we inform the team?

FR · Meaning: Should = conseil; could = possibilité; must = obligation; might = possibilité incertaine.

Rules, exceptions & common errors: Never add to after should/could/must: should send, not should to send. No -s in third person: she should send. Stronger recommendations include I would recommend + -ing / that + clause. Must not = prohibition, not absence of obligation.

07 · Reason, contrast and result Teach + try

Form: because + clause; although + clause; however, + sentence; therefore, + result

Affirmative: We delayed the launch because testing was incomplete.

Negative: The launch was not delayed because of cost.

Question: Why was the launch delayed?

FR · Meaning: Because explique pourquoi; although/however opposent; therefore/as a result indiquent une conséquence.

Rules, exceptions & common errors: Because + subject/verb; because of + noun/gerund. Although cannot normally be paired with but in the same clause. However and therefore need punctuation when linking separate sentences. Avoid comma splices.

08 · Examples, precision and paragraph flow Teach + try

Form: Point → reason → specific example → consequence → action

Affirmative: Training helps because people understand their tasks. For example, a mentor can explain the system. As a result, errors may decrease.

Negative: An example alone is not an argument.

Question: How could this help new employees?

FR · Meaning: Le lecteur doit voir une idée, une explication, un exemple concret et la conséquence.

Rules, exceptions & common errors: For example / for instance introduce a real illustration. Such as is usually followed by a noun phrase. Avoid claims without support, repetitions and overly long sentences. A strong paragraph remains relevant to the prompt.

Now practise: Which addition best develops the idea “Mentors help”?

B1REVEAL B1 COMPLETE ESSAY＋

The first week can be stressful for new employees because they do not know the people or the systems. I think every new colleague should receive a clear timetable and a helpful contact person. For example, a buddy could answer everyday questions. As a result, new employees would feel more confident and start working more quickly.

Why this works / how to progress: Clearly covers problem, practical solutions and benefit, with reason and example.

B2REVEAL B2 COMPLETE ESSAY＋

An effective onboarding programme should help new employees feel supported while enabling them to become productive. At present, unclear procedures and too much information on the first day can create unnecessary stress. I would recommend introducing a structured weekly schedule, a named mentor and short follow-up meetings. For instance, essential systems could be introduced gradually rather than all at once. These changes would reduce confusion and allow managers to identify difficulties early.

Why this works / how to progress: Purposeful paragraph flow, specific measures, varied structures and realistic outcomes.

C1REVEAL C1 COMPLETE ESSAY＋

A successful onboarding process should balance operational efficiency with the confidence and wellbeing of new colleagues. When expectations are unclear and information is delivered all at once, even experienced recruits may struggle to contribute effectively. I would therefore propose a phased induction programme in which essential tasks are prioritised, each newcomer is paired with a trained mentor, and progress is reviewed at the end of weeks one and four. Although this approach requires an initial investment of staff time, it is likely to reduce repeated questions, identify training gaps sooner and strengthen employee retention. Its effectiveness could be assessed through short surveys and early-performance indicators.

Why this works / how to progress: Strong position, nuanced concession, thoughtful implementation and measurable impact.

LANGUAGE PRACTICE · NOT A NEW ESSAY TASK

Use these structures with the onboarding scenario from Section 05. The examples about schedules, suppliers and projects demonstrate grammar only; they are not additional writing prompts. After completing the rules, return to your chosen subject and write one sentence using the new structure.

1 · Reasons: because, since, as

Meaning: explain WHY. FR: parce que / puisque / comme.

Form: Main clause + because + subject + verb. We changed the schedule because the supplier was late.

Contrast: because of + noun / -ing: because of the delay, not “because of the supplier was late”. Since can also mean “depuis”; context decides. As may mean “pendant que” too.

Negative: We did not change the schedule because of the cost. Be careful: the meaning may be ambiguous; rewrite clearly.

2 · Contrast: but, although, however, despite

Meaning: show an opposing idea. FR: mais / bien que / cependant / malgré.

Forms: Although + subject + verb, main clause. → Although the budget was limited, the project succeeded. However, usually starts a new sentence or follows a semicolon: The budget was limited. However, the project succeeded.

Exception: Despite / in spite of + noun or -ing: Despite the delay, we finished. NOT “Despite it was late”. Do not normally combine although and but for the same contrast.

3 · Examples and results: for example, such as, therefore

Meaning: support your argument and explain the consequence. FR: par exemple / tel que / par conséquent.

Forms: For example, new employees could receive a welcome guide. Resources such as checklists can reduce confusion. As a result, employees feel more confident.

Exceptions: such as introduces examples (usually nouns or noun phrases), not a complete independent sentence. Therefore connects ideas; use a full stop or semicolon, not a comma splice.

4 · Recommendations: should, could, would, suggest

Meaning: recommend, propose or describe possible results. FR: devrait / pourrait / serait.

Form: subject + modal + base verb: Managers should explain the process. Negative: They should not ignore questions. Question: Should managers offer training?

Exceptions: no -s after a modal: NOT “she should explains”. No to after should/could/would: NOT “could to help”. Suggest + -ing or suggest that + subject + verb: I suggest introducing a checklist. NOT “I suggest to introduce”.

5 · Passive voice: be + past participle (full lesson)

Why? Focus on the action or result, not the person doing it. FR: voix passive — « La réunion a été déplacée », plutôt que « Quelqu'un a déplacé la réunion ».

Active: The manager moved the meeting. Passive: The meeting was moved (by the manager).

Present simple: The reports are checked every Friday. Past simple: The meeting was moved to Friday. Future: The documents will be sent tomorrow. Present perfect: The issue has been resolved. Modal: The form should be completed. Teach and practise each tense separately before mixing.

Negative: The meeting was not cancelled. Question: Was the meeting cancelled? Agent: add by + person only if important: The proposal was approved by the director.

Exceptions and traps: use the correct irregular past participle: written, sent, made, taken (NOT “was wrote”). The passive normally needs a verb that takes an object: NOT “The accident was happened.” Use happened instead. By identifies the agent; with often identifies the instrument. Not every passive is better: use active voice when the actor and responsibility matter.

In writing: New employees are given a welcome guide. Their questions are answered during the first week.

6 · Sentence control: tense, agreement and punctuation

Present simple: routine: She works / She does not work / Does she work? Third-person -s in affirmative only; does takes base verb.

Past simple: completed past: They introduced / did not introduce / Did they introduce? Irregular: went, wrote, made.

Present perfect: past action connected to now: We have improved the process. FR: bilan ou résultat présent, pas simplement un passé daté. With a finished time such as yesterday, normally use past simple.

Proofread: capital I, full stops, commas after introductory connectors, and avoid joining two independent sentences with only a comma.
