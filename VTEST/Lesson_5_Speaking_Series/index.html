01 · SPEAKING BRIEFING · 8 MIN

Three official parts. Two of them build your final Speaking score.

Today you learn exactly what appears in VTest Business English Speaking, what Part 1 is for, what the scored speaking tasks require, how the screens behave, and how to use preparation time before you record.

Key fact: Rejoinders are a pre-speaking screening task. They help assign the level of Parts 2–3, but they are not part of the final reported Speaking score.

En clair : la première partie sert à calibrer le niveau. Les parties 2 et 3 sont les productions orales évaluées. Nous allons travailler chaque partie séparément avant un examen blanc complet.

02 · OFFICIAL PART 1

Rejoinders · hear the situation, choose the natural response.

9 QUESTIONS · ~7 MIN
BEFORE YOU STARTUnderstand the mission before you practise.
LEARN

what Rejoinders test and why Part 1 is screening only

DO

identify the response function before looking for vocabulary

WHY IT MATTERS

it controls the difficulty of the speaking tasks that follow

READY TO MOVE ON WHEN…

you can explain request / apology / suggestion / update / clarification without translating word by word

WHAT HAPPENS

Hear 9 statements or questions.

Choose the best response from three options. The task tests contextual comprehension and natural interaction.

WHAT IT DOES

Sets the level.

Part 1 is used to assign appropriate prompt levels for Parts 2 and 3. It does not enter the final reported Speaking score.

WHAT TO TRAIN

Function before vocabulary.

Recognize requests, apologies, suggestions, updates, problems, invitations, clarification and agreement.

Next page: 5B teaches each response function explicitly, then gives a Practice Exam and a separate 9-item Final Screening.
03 · OFFICIAL PART 2

Read Aloud + Discuss.

1 TEXT + 2 QUESTIONS · ~4 MIN
BEFORE YOU STARTUnderstand the mission before you practise.
LEARN

the three actions inside Read Aloud + Discuss

DO

read with thought groups, then answer comprehension and opinion questions

WHY IT MATTERS

VTest evaluates understandable spoken English, not fast reading

READY TO MOVE ON WHEN…

you know what to do before recording and can distinguish reading skill from discussion skill

STAGE A

Read a text aloud.

Use clear phrasing, natural pauses, intelligible pronunciation and steady pace.

STAGE B

Answer a comprehension question.

Show that you understood the key idea or information in the text.

STAGE C

Give a personal opinion.

Develop the answer with reasons, examples and a clear conclusion. Each spoken question gives 60 seconds to respond.

Scoring: VTest states that this task is scored by its automated scoring system. Our course uses a transparent trainer rubric for practice only.
04 · OFFICIAL PART 3

Discuss Topics · four workplace prompts.

4 QUESTIONS · ~7 MIN
BEFORE YOU STARTUnderstand the mission before you practise.
LEARN

the kinds of workplace prompts VTest documents

DO

prepare 3–4 key ideas and speak for the full minute

WHY IT MATTERS

the task rewards developed, relevant workplace communication

READY TO MOVE ON WHEN…

you can build a 60-second route without memorising a script

DESCRIBE

Your job or a work situation.

Give concrete details rather than a one-sentence answer.

EXPLAIN

How to do something.

Organize the process with sequence language and reasons.

DISCUSS

A common business topic.

The official site gives examples such as remote work, leadership and first jobs. Exact topics can vary.

Official behaviour: you receive preparation time, then 60 seconds to speak for each prompt. The test can auto-advance when time ends.

06 · THE 60-SECOND METHOD

Prepare a route, not a script.

BEFORE YOU STARTUnderstand the mission before you practise.
LEARN

a reusable speaking route

DO

answer → reason → detail → example → result

WHY IT MATTERS

a clear route prevents one-sentence answers and long pauses

READY TO MOVE ON WHEN…

you can produce a full answer from key words only

1 · ANSWERGive the direct response first.
2 · DEVELOPAdd two useful details.
3 · EXAMPLEUse one concrete example.
4 · CLOSEGive a result, opinion or conclusion.
French help: évitez de traduire une réponse entière dans votre tête. Préparez 3–4 idées-clés, puis construisez les phrases pendant que vous parlez.
