02 · ENGLISH RHYTHM VS FRENCH RHYTHM

English does not give every syllable the same weight.

COMMUNICATION LAB
BEFORE YOU READLearn the rhythm first; then apply it to VTEST text.
TYPICAL ENGLISH TENDENCY

Important content words carry stronger beats; small grammar words are often lighter and quicker.

TYPICAL FRENCH TENDENCY

French often gives syllables a more even flow and places prominence near the end of a phrase. This is a useful comparison, not a rule about every speaker.

YOUR GOAL

Do not imitate an accent. Make the information structure easy to hear.

VTEST TRANSFER

Clear stress, rhythm and intonation help the read-aloud sound intelligible and natural.

WORD-BY-WORD RISK

Our · customer · support · team · is · introducing · a · new · ticket · system · next · month.

ENGLISH INFORMATION RHYTHM

Our CUSTOMER SUPPORT TEAM / is introducing a NEW TICKET SYSTEM / next MONTH.

Stress rule: nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs, numbers and contrasts usually carry information stress. Articles, pronouns, auxiliaries and prepositions are often lighter unless they are contrasted.
03 · PUNCTUATION = BREATH + MEANING

Let punctuation tell you where the thought groups change.

HEAR THE DIFFERENCE
MISSIONPause because the meaning changes—not because you have run out of breath.
COMMA ,

Short pause inside the sentence; the idea normally continues.

FULL STOP .

Full thought ends. Pause longer and reset the voice for the next sentence.

COLON / SEMICOLON : ;

Medium pause: explanation, list, contrast or closely linked idea follows.

DASH / BRACKETS — ( )

Signal an aside, interruption or extra information; slightly separate the voice.

04 · SENTENCE BEGINNINGS, ENDINGS & INTONATION

Launch clearly. Carry the thought. Finish the sentence.

↗ RISE · ↘ FALL · ↗↘ LIST
COMMUNICATION RULESYour listener should hear where the message begins, develops and finishes.
BEGINNING

Start the first information word clearly. Do not rush the first five words while looking ahead.

MIDDLE

Keep the voice alive across the thought group. Do not drop volume after every word.

STATEMENT END ↘

Most complete statements finish with a controlled fall that tells the listener the idea is complete.

QUESTIONS / LISTS

Yes/no questions often rise; WH-questions often fall. Lists usually rise on non-final items and fall on the final item.

French → English transfer: a French-speaking learner may naturally keep syllables more even or place prominence late in the phrase. In English, make the important information words stand out earlier and more clearly. The aim is intelligibility—not erasing your accent.

05 · MARK THE TEXT BEFORE YOU READ

Prepare the message visually before you record it.

/ THOUGHT GROUP · CAPS = STRESS
4 PASSESDo not begin by practising pronunciation word by word.
PASS 1

Circle punctuation and sentence boundaries.

PASS 2

Add / between natural meaning groups.

PASS 3

Choose 1–3 information words to stress in each group.

PASS 4

Read once slowly for meaning, then once naturally for flow.

YOUR MARK-UP
The company will introduce a new booking system next month. Staff will receive training on Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday, and the old system will remain available until Friday.
One possible modelThe COMPANY will introduce a new BOOKING SYSTEM / next MONTH. // Staff will receive TRAINING / on MONDAY, TUESDAY and WEDNESDAY / and the old system will remain available / until FRIDAY.

Stress detective

Choose the word that should normally carry the strongest information stress.

READ ALOUD

Flexible schedules

Some companies let workers choose when to start and finish their jobs. For example, people can begin early in the morning and leave in the afternoon, or come in later and go home in the evening. This kind of schedule helps people who have children or other important responsibilities. It can also make travelling to work less stressful. Many employers like it because it makes it easier for employees to plan and organize their day.
Some companies let workers choose / when to start and finish their jobs. / For example, people can begin early in the morning / and leave in the afternoon...
01:15
QUESTION 1 · COMPREHENSION

Why do some companies let people choose when to work?

Plan in key words: flexibility · responsibilities · commute · organize day
Start with: “Companies allow flexible hours because…” Then give two reasons from the text.
01:00
QUESTION 2 · PERSONAL OPINION

Do you think flexible working hours are good for every company? Why or why not?

Plan: opinion → reason 1 → example → limitation/result
Use a contrast: “It can work well when…, however it may be difficult for…”
