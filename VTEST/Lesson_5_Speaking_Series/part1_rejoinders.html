01 · PART 1 BRIEFING

Do not translate the sentence. Identify the social function.

Rejoinders test whether you can recognize what somebody is doing—requesting, apologizing, suggesting, updating, inviting, clarifying—and select the response that naturally continues the exchange.

Official role: 9 questions, approximately 7 minutes. This is a pre-speaking task used to assign the level of Parts 2–3; it does not count in the final reported Speaking score.

Méthode : identifiez d’abord l’intention : demande ? excuse ? proposition ? problème ? Ensuite choisissez la réponse qui continue logiquement la conversation.

02 · BEFORE YOU ANSWER

Use the same 5-step route for every Rejoinder.

LISTEN → FUNCTION → TONE → ELIMINATE → RESPOND
MISSIONUnderstand what the speaker is doing before you choose words.
1 · LISTEN

Catch the situation and the key verb or problem.

2 · FUNCTION

Request? apology? bad news? suggestion? clarification? offer?

3 · TONE

Professional, neutral, worried, positive, or corrective?

4–5 · ELIMINATE + RESPOND

Reject grammar-only distractors and choose the response that naturally continues the exchange.

1 · SITUATIONWho is speaking?
2 · INTENTIONWhat do they want?
3 · KEY DETAILWhat changed?
4 · RESPONSE TYPEAccept / clarify / react?
5 · CHECKDoes it continue the conversation?
French-speaking learner trap: do not choose an answer simply because it repeats the same vocabulary. English workplace exchanges often use a different phrase to perform the correct social action.

02 · RESPONSE FUNCTIONS

Know what kind of answer the situation needs.

BEFORE YOU STARTUnderstand the mission before you practise.
LEARN

the main social functions that create the correct response

DO

compare each function with a natural workplace reply

WHY IT MATTERS

VTest distractors may be grammatical but socially wrong

READY TO MOVE ON WHEN…

you can name the function and predict the response before seeing options

REQUEST

Could you…?

Accept, refuse politely, or clarify.

“Could you send the file?” → “Sure, I’ll send it this afternoon.”
PROBLEM / BAD NEWS

I’m afraid…

Acknowledge + react to the consequence.

“The supplier is delayed.” → “Thanks for letting me know. I’ll update the client.”
SUGGESTION

Why don’t we…?

Agree, disagree with reason, or offer an alternative.

“Why don’t we move it?” → “Good idea. Friday would work.”
APOLOGY

Sorry about…

Accept the apology and move forward.

“Sorry I’m late.” → “No problem. We’ve just started.”
INVITATION

Would you like…?

Accept or decline naturally.

“Would you like to join us?” → “Yes, thanks. I’d be happy to.”
CLARIFICATION

Do you mean…?

Confirm or correct information.

“Do you mean Tuesday?” → “Yes, Tuesday morning.”
UPDATE

Just to let you know…

Acknowledge and identify the next action.

“The room changed.” → “Thanks. I’ll tell the team.”
OPINION

What do you think…?

Give a relevant reaction, not an unrelated fact.

“What do you think of the plan?” → “It looks practical, but the timing is tight.”
OFFER

Shall I…?

Accept, decline, or redirect the help.

“Shall I call Finance?” → “Yes, please. That would help.”
