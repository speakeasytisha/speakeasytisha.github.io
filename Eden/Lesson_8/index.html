03 · Managerial language

From “I hope” to “Here’s what I expect”

Your language can be collaborative without being vague. Choose the level of force that matches the situation.

FR : Votre langage peut être collaboratif sans être vague. Choisissez le degré de fermeté adapté à la situation.

Tentative“I hope you can keep me informed.”
Professional“I’d like you to keep me informed.”
Managerial“What I need is early visibility of significant risks.”
Collaborative + firm“What I need from you is early visibility of significant risks, and I want us to agree on what needs to be escalated.”

04 · Cooperation

Ask for cooperation without asking for permission to manage

Useful

“I’m counting on your cooperation.”

“I’d like us to agree on how we work together.”

“Let’s define the right level of communication.”

Even stronger with senior experts

“I want you to have enough autonomy to work effectively. At the same time, I need visibility when the programme risk changes.”

“Let’s agree on the situations where you need to involve me.”

Avoid

“I hope you will cooperate with me.”

It can sound as though cooperation is optional or that authority needs approval.

06 · Executive extraction

“What is the key point?”

You do not need every technical detail. You need the information that changes the decision, risk, priority or next action.

FR : Vous n’avez pas besoin de tous les détails techniques, mais de l’information qui change la décision, le risque, la priorité ou la prochaine action.

Decision

Recommendation

Impact

Need

What do you need from me?

Interrupt professionally

A senior engineer has given several minutes of detail, but you still do not know whether you need to make a decision.

B2

Let me stop you for a second. What is the key point I need to understand, and is there a decision you need from me?

C1

Let me bring this back to the decision point. What is the material risk, what is your recommendation, and what do you need from me today?

07 · Language precision

Estimate vs. estimation

A small language point from your previous lesson, connected directly to project leadership.

FR : Un petit point de langue vu dans la leçon précédente, directement lié au pilotage de projet.

Estimate

Noun: an approximate figure, cost or duration.

“Our current estimate is six weeks.”

Verb: to calculate approximately.

“We estimate that the work will take six weeks.”

Estimation

More often the process or method of estimating.

“The estimation process needs to be more consistent.”

“Cost estimation is difficult at this stage.”

Practical rule: if you mean “approximately how much / how long?”, use estimate. If you mean the process or method, estimation is possible.

Manage the estimate

The technical lead says: “My current estimate is six weeks, but there are still several unknowns.”

B2

Understood. What are the main assumptions behind the six-week estimate, and which unknown has the greatest potential impact? I also need to know when you expect to have enough information to refine the estimate.

C1

Six weeks gives us a working assumption, but I need to understand the confidence level behind it. What are the two main variables that could move that estimate, and when will we have enough evidence to either confirm or revise it?

08 · Core framework

EXPECT ↔ ENABLE

A strong management relationship is two-way: make what you need explicit, and make what they can expect from you equally explicit.

FR : Une relation managériale solide fonctionne dans les deux sens : rendez explicite ce dont vous avez besoin, et rendez tout aussi explicite ce qu’ils peuvent attendre de vous.

1

EXPECT

What I need from you is…

  • early risk visibility
  • clear recommendations
  • realistic estimates
  • ownership
  • follow-through
2

ENABLE

What you can expect from me is…

  • clear priorities
  • timely decisions
  • support with escalation
  • autonomy
  • protection from unnecessary reporting

Build your two-way contract

B2

What I need from you is early visibility of significant risks, realistic estimates and a clear recommendation when a decision is required. What you can expect from me is clear priorities, timely decisions and enough autonomy to lead the technical work effectively.

C1

What I need from you is transparency around material risk, technically grounded recommendations and clear ownership of agreed actions. What you can expect from me is clarity on priorities, timely decisions, support when escalation is necessary and enough space to lead the technical work without unnecessary interference.

09 · Reverse the question

Understand what they need from you

Meeting their expectations does not mean giving away managerial authority. It means understanding the conditions that help experienced people perform well.

FR : Répondre à leurs attentes ne signifie pas renoncer à votre autorité managériale. Cela signifie comprendre les conditions qui permettent aux personnes expérimentées de bien performer.

Avoid: “How would you like me to manage you?”
Prefer: “What conditions will allow us to work effectively together?”

Oral transfer

Ask a future technical leader three questions that would help you understand what they need from you.

B2

What do you need from me to do your job effectively? Which decisions do you expect me to own? When would you expect me to step in?

C1

What conditions help you operate at your best? Where do you need management decisions quickly, and where do you need more space? What escalation or communication pattern would give us enough visibility without slowing the technical work?

14 · Boundary under challenge

When a senior expert says you do not need to be involved

“I don’t think you need to be involved at this stage. This is a technical matter.”

B2

I agree that the technical solution is yours to lead. I do need to understand anything that could affect timing, cost or programme risk. If none of those are affected, I’m comfortable leaving the technical decision with you.

C1

I’m not looking to take ownership of the technical decision. That sits with you. My involvement is needed when the technical choice has implications for programme timing, cost, interfaces or overall risk. Let’s use that as the boundary for escalation.

16 · CLOE transfer

Explain your future management approach clearly

Professional oral task: answer naturally for 60–90 seconds with a principle, an example and a result.

FR : Tâche orale professionnelle : répondez naturellement pendant 60 à 90 secondes avec un principe, un exemple et un résultat.

CLOE-style prompt

“How would you build a productive working relationship with people who have more technical experience than you?”

01:30
B2

When someone has more technical experience than I do, I see that as an advantage. I make my expectations clear about risks, recommendations and follow-up, but I give the person autonomy on the technical work. I also ask what they need from me, especially for priorities and decisions. For example, if a technical leader needs more time, I want to understand the assumptions behind the estimate and the programme impact. This creates trust without losing management visibility.

C1

I would build the relationship around complementary responsibilities rather than competing expertise. The technical leader owns the depth of the judgement; I own clarity around priorities, decision points, risk visibility and accountability. I make those expectations explicit, then ask what they need from me to operate effectively. The aim is a lightweight management interface: autonomy by default, early escalation on material change, and timely management decisions when the programme needs them.
