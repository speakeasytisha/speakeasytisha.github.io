02 · Speaking cues

Delivery key for the full lesson

The colour-coded cues show how to deliver the message. The same sentence can sound weak, rushed, calm or authoritative depending on the way it is delivered.

Pause Slow down Energy up Calm Firm Emphasise Lower voice Look up Breathe

When to use energy

Use energy when you open, when you introduce the value of the brainstorming session, or when attention is dropping.

Energy up The brainstorming session brought together strong technical expertise from across the group.

When to slow down

Slow down when you name risks, decision criteria, validation limits, safety or compliance.

Slow down The issue is the level of confidence we need before moving forward.

When to be firm

Use firmness when you make a recommendation or ask for a decision.

Firm My recommendation is to define the decision criteria before the next review.

04 · Step 2

The executive communication problem

In a technical role, you often know much more than the audience needs to hear. The challenge is not to say everything. The challenge is to choose what helps the audience understand and decide.

Technical communication

What happened? What are the details? What do the teams know? What still needs to be checked?

Executive communication

Why does it matter? What is the risk? What is the impact? What decision or next step is needed?

Model explanation

When you speak to other engineers, technical detail can be useful because the audience shares the same context. When you speak to management, the purpose is different. You need to select the information that clarifies the risk, the impact and the decision needed. The goal is not to reduce the quality of the technical thinking. The goal is to make the technical thinking usable for decision-making.

Progress checkpoint

05 · Step 3

The audience filter

Before presenting, filter the information. This helps you avoid over-explaining and keeps your message connected to the audience’s priorities.

1. What does the audience already know?

2. What do they need to understand?

3. What are they worried about?

4. What decision do they need to make?

5. What level of technical detail is useful?

Model audience filter

Calm Management does not need every detail from the brainstorming session. They need to understand why the session was valuable, which themes emerged, what still needs to be validated and what decision criteria should guide the next step.

06 · Step 4

New structure: Situation, Risk, Impact, Recommendation, Decision

This is the main structure of the lesson. It is broader and more professional than the Because / I recommend / so that formula because it guides a complete executive briefing.

Situation→Risk→Impact→Recommendation→Decision

Situation

Open The situation is that the validation evidence is not mature enough to close the point with confidence.

Risk

Calm The risk is that we move forward before the compliance position is fully secured.

Impact

Slow down The impact could be rework, delay or a weaker decision path later in the programme.

Recommendation

Firm My recommendation is to organise a focused evidence review before the next milestone.

Decision

Look up What I need today is confirmation that we prioritise this validation step before moving forward.

Complete model briefing

The situation is that the validation evidence is not mature enough to close the point with confidence. Pause The risk is that we move forward before the compliance position is fully secured. The impact could be rework, delay or a weaker decision path later in the programme. My recommendation is to organise a focused evidence review before the next milestone. Look up What I need today is confirmation that we prioritise this validation step before moving forward.

08 · Case study

From brainstorming to executive briefing

Your new project and the brainstorming session are now the real case study. The session was powerful because it brought several strong engineering minds together. The presentation challenge is to transform that collective intelligence into direction.

Communication bridge

Many ideas → clear themes → decision criteria → recommended next step

Model message using the main structure

The situation is that we recently held a brainstorming session with several engineers to explore possible directions for the new project. The session was valuable because it brought together different technical perspectives and allowed us to challenge the problem from several angles. The risk is that the ideas remain too broad if we do not organise them into clear themes and decision criteria. The impact could be a lack of alignment on the strongest direction, the validation effort and the project timeline. My recommendation is to structure the outcomes around feasibility, validation strategy, compliance confidence and project impact. Look up What I need now is alignment on the criteria we will use to evaluate the most promising option.

12 · Optional tool

Where the previous formula still fits

The main structure today is Situation, Risk, Impact, Recommendation and Decision. The old Because / I recommend / so that formula can still be useful, but only as a short logic check inside the recommendation, not as the whole lesson structure.

Because…→I recommend…→so that…

Example of limited use

Because the ideas from the brainstorming session are promising but still too broad, I recommend organising them into clear decision criteria so that management can evaluate the strongest option with confidence.
