Connect and qualify ideas

One important distinction: although vs however

Although + clause: Although the journey is expensive, it appeals to me. No comma-separated independent sentence after although alone.

However + punctuation: The journey is expensive. However, it appeals to me. Or: The journey is expensive; however, it appeals to me.

Whereas contrasts two facts; because explains a reason. Despite differs: it is followed by a noun or an -ing form, not normally a finite clause.

Precise words for your four topics

Your tense pathway · one meaning at a time

Progression: 1 · Past simple → 2 · Present perfect simple → 3 · Present perfect continuous → 4 · Past perfect → 5 · Mixed-time challenge. Complete each set of questions before going to the next. The French wording below explains the meaning, not a mechanical word-for-word translation.

Important: French passé composé can correspond to the English past simple or present perfect. You must look at the time perspective, not simply translate j'ai as I have.

STEP 1 / 5 · PAST SIMPLE

A completed event in a finished past time

Le sens en français : « Je suis allée en Égypte il y a quinze ans. » L'action est située dans une période terminée. Le français dit souvent « je suis allée » (passé composé), mais l'anglais utilise ici le past simple.

Timeline: ● event in a finished past period ───── NOW

Affirmative: I visited Cairo in 2011. (regular -ed) / I went to Egypt. (irregular)

Negative: I did not (didn't) visit Luxor.

Question: Did you see the pyramids? — Yes, I did / No, I didn't.

Keywords: in 2011, fifteen years ago, last year, when I was younger, yesterday.

Rule: Subject + past form in affirmative. In negatives and questions, did + base verb (never *did went*).

Exceptions: The verb be uses was/were: It wasn't crowded. Were you impressed? No did with be.

Compare: “I went to Egypt fifteen years ago” = Je suis allée en Égypte il y a quinze ans (date/period finished). “I have been to Egypt twice” describes a different perspective; you will learn it in Step 2.

Practise STEP 1 · immediate feedback + hint

STEP 2 / 5 · PRESENT PERFECT SIMPLE

A past experience or result viewed from NOW

Le sens en français : « J'ai déjà visité l'Égypte deux fois (dans ma vie, jusqu'à aujourd'hui). » L'important n'est pas quand, mais l'expérience acquise à ce jour. « J'ai perdu mon billet » peut vouloir dire « je ne l'ai toujours pas / c'est un problème maintenant ». Le français emploie souvent le passé composé dans les deux cas.

Timeline: past experience(s) ● ● ──── NOW (still relevant)

Affirmative: I have visited Egypt twice. / She has read the book.

Negative: I haven't visited Norway yet. / She hasn't finished the novel.

Question: Have you ever played Qwirkle? — Yes, I have / No, I haven't.

Formation: have/has + past participle (visited, seen, been, written).

Keywords: ever, never, already, yet, so far, recently, just, since, for.

Use 1 · Life experience: I've been to Egypt twice. Use 2 · Present result: I've lost my notes (I don't have them now). Use 3 · Unfinished time: I've read two articles this week (the week is still ongoing).

Restrictions: Do not use the present perfect with a finished, precise past-time marker: I've visited Cairo fifteen years ago. → I visited Cairo fifteen years ago. The word yesterday also triggers past simple. Have been to = visited and returned; have gone to may mean still away.

Same French form, different English meaning: « J'ai lu ce livre l'an dernier » → I read this book last year. (closed past) versus « J'ai déjà lu ce livre » → I've already read this book. (experience now).

Practise STEP 2 · decide whether NOW matters

STEP 3 / 5 · PRESENT PERFECT CONTINUOUS

An activity continuing up to now (or leaving a visible effect)

Le sens en français : « Cela fait deux semaines que je me renseigne sur la Norvège » / « Je me renseigne depuis deux semaines. » L'activité a commencé avant maintenant et elle continue ou vient de s'arrêter. Ne traduisez pas automatiquement par un passé composé !

Timeline: START ━━━━━━━━━━━━━ NOW

Affirmative: I have been reading about Norwegian fjords.

Negative: I haven't been researching cabins for very long.

Question: Have you been planning the journey for months?

Formation: have/has + been + verb-ing.

Keywords: for two weeks, since Monday, lately, recently, all morning, how long.

Contrast: “I've read three articles” focuses on a completed result / number; “I've been reading all afternoon” focuses on the process and duration.

Exceptions: Stative verbs such as know, believe, own usually avoid the continuous form: I've known her for years (not *I've been knowing*). For questions about quantity or achievement, the simple form is often better.

French contrast: « Je la connais depuis dix ans » → I've known her for ten years. (not continuous). « Je lis ce roman depuis une heure » → I've been reading this novel for an hour.

Practise STEP 3 · activity or result?

STEP 4 / 5 · PAST PERFECT

Look BACK from one past moment to an even earlier past

Prerequisite: This step uses the past participle you saw in Step 2. Unlike the present perfect, its reference point is another moment in the past, not NOW.

Le sens en français : « J'avais déjà lu des livres sur l'Égypte avant d'y aller. » C'est l'idée du plus-que-parfait français : une action antérieure à une autre action passée. I had read = « j'avais lu », et non « j'ai lu ».

Timeline: EARLIER PAST (had read) ● ─── LATER PAST (visited) ● ─── NOW

Affirmative: I had read about the pyramids before I travelled to Cairo.

Negative: I hadn't expected the scale of the monuments.

Question: Had you seen photographs before you arrived?

Formation: had + past participle, for all subjects.

Keywords: before, by the time, already, when, after, until then.

Use: Tell two past events and make the order clear: When I arrived, the museum had already closed. First the museum closed; then I arrived.

Exceptions and nuance: If before or after already makes the order perfectly clear, past simple can be natural for both events: We ate before we boarded. Past perfect is useful when you want to stress the earlier event; do not use it for every past action.

Compare three perspectives: “I visited Egypt fifteen years ago” = Je suis allée… (finished past); “I have visited Egypt twice” = J'ai visité… deux fois jusqu'à aujourd'hui (experience now); “I had visited Egypt before my second trip” = J'avais visité… avant mon deuxième voyage (earlier past).

Practise STEP 4 · identify which past came first

GRAMMAR A · REPORTED CLAIMS & EVIDENCE (AFTER THE TENSE PATHWAY)

Reporting without treating an allegation as a fact

Use: reporting verbs and the passive to separate who says what from what has been proven. Essential for nuanced discussions about AI.

Patterns

Active: Critics claim that the novel was AI-assisted. / The author denies using AI.

Passive: The novel has been accused of being AI-generated. / It is alleged that AI was involved.

Negative: The allegations have not been conclusively established.

Question: Has the claim been verified? / What evidence has been presented?

Rule: deny + -ing (deny using), claim + that clause / to-infinitive (claim that; claim to have), accuse someone of + -ing. The passive is be + past participle; the present perfect passive is has/have been + past participle.

Important exceptions: According to X attributes a view without endorsing it. Apparently signals uncertainty, not proof. It is said that is impersonal; don't write *it says that* for an anonymous allegation. Don't confuse evidence (uncountable) with *evidences*.

Worked transformation: “An anonymous account accused the writer.” → “The writer was accused by an anonymous account.” The sentence reports the accusation; it does not verify it.
GRAMMAR C · COMPLEX SENTENCES & CONCESSION

Compare, concede and sharpen an argument

Use: linkers to express cause, contrast and concession without repeating but or and.

Structures

Although / even though + subject + verb: Although it is not a luxury cruise, the itinerary is appealing.

Whereas / while + subject + verb: An inside cabin has no window, whereas a premium cabin offers a wider view.

Because / since + subject + verb: I'm interested because the ship also serves coastal towns.

Despite / in spite of + noun or -ing: Despite the modest facilities, the trip sounds remarkable.

However / nevertheless: The journey may be expensive. Nevertheless, it is unusual.

Affirmative / negative / question: Although I agree, I see a drawback. / Although I don't agree, I understand the reasoning. / Why might someone disagree, even though the evidence seems convincing?

Common traps: Don't write *although … but* in the same clause; don't use despite of; a comma alone cannot join two independent sentences with however. While can express either time or contrast: the context clarifies its meaning.

Basic: “The rules are simple, but the game is strategic.” → Nuanced: “Although the rules are relatively straightforward, the decisions can be surprisingly strategic.”
GRAMMAR D · HYPOTHESIS & DIPLOMACY

Explore possibilities without sounding absolute

Use: second conditional for hypothetical present/future; third conditional for imagined changes to the past; mixed conditional for a past cause with a present result; modals to express degrees of certainty.

Second: If a publisher used AI openly, how would readers react? / If a book didn't include disclosure, would that matter? / Would you read it if it were AI-assisted?

Third: If the author had disclosed AI use, the debate might have been different. / If the judges hadn't seen the allegations, would they have reacted differently?

Mixed: If I hadn't discovered Qwirkle, I wouldn't enjoy strategic board games as much today. (Imagined past → present)

Hedging: might, may, could, seems to, appears to, to some extent, arguably, as far as I know.

Exceptions: Avoid *would* in the normal if-clause (*if I would know*). Were is a formal alternative with all subjects in hypothetical clauses (If I were…). Must have signals a strong deduction; might have leaves more uncertainty.

Instead of “The detector proves it,” say: “The software may raise questions, but its findings would need independent verification.”
