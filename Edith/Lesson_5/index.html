CHAPTER 02 · GRAMMAR & DISCUSSION

Which place is better for you?

Build comparisons to explain your opinion, not just to memorise grammar.

1

Comparatives · Compare two things

When? To explain why you prefer one destination to another.

A + is + comparative adjective + than + B

Short adjectives: small → smaller; nice → nicer (drop final e); big → bigger (double consonant); happy → happier (y → i).

Long adjectives: more interesting than; less expensive than.

Irregular: good → better; bad → worse; far → farther/further.

“Lisbon is sunnier than Edinburgh, but Edinburgh is more historic.”

Remember: use than, not then, after a comparison.

2

Superlatives · Choose the top option

When? To choose one place from three or more options.

the + adjective-est / the most + adjective

Short: the tallest, the nicest, the biggest, the happiest. Long: the most interesting. Irregular: the best, the worst, the farthest/furthest.

“For me, Kyoto is the most peaceful destination.”

Watch out: “the most beautiful”, not “the beautifulest”.

Talk it through

Which two destinations would you compare? Say why you prefer one.

A2: “I prefer Lisbon because it is warmer than Edinburgh. It is also very beautiful.”

B1: “Although Edinburgh has fascinating historical sites, I would choose Lisbon because the weather is usually milder and I love exploring colourful neighbourhoods.”

CHAPTER 03 · EXPRESSING OPINIONS

What kind of trip do you enjoy?

3

Present simple · Preferences and facts

Use: what you like, prefer, enjoy, want or usually do.

I/you/we/they + like · he/she + likes

Negative: I don’t like crowds. / He doesn’t like crowds. Questions: Do you like museums? / Does your husband enjoy hiking?

After like/love/enjoy: “I enjoy walking.” After would like: “I would like to visit Rome.”

“I prefer peaceful places, but my husband likes lively cities.”

Conversation · Ask for an opinion

Take turns: A: “What do you think about Rome?” B: “I think it looks exciting, but I prefer…”

A2: A: “Do you like Kyoto?” B: “Yes, I do. I like gardens.” A: “Shall we go there?”

B1: A: “Would you rather visit Kyoto or Rome?” B: “I’m more interested in Kyoto because I enjoy peaceful places. What do you think?” A: “That sounds lovely, although Rome might be easier to reach.”

Pick a real hotel — to practise decisions, not memorise hotel facts

Choose a destination above, then select between two real properties. Compare location, comfort and style using English. These are examples, not live prices or availability.

Speaking prompt: “Which accommodation would you choose, and why?”

A2: “I prefer the first hotel. It looks comfortable and it is near the city centre.”

B1: “I would choose the smaller hotel because I usually prefer a quieter atmosphere. Before booking, I would check its location and cancellation policy.”

CHAPTER 04 · TIME & DATES

When shall we go?

4

British dates · Read, write and say them

British written order: day / month / year → 14 October 2026. You can also write 14th October 2026.

Spoken: “the fourteenth of October, twenty twenty-six.”

Ordinal endings: 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th–20th; 21st, 22nd, 23rd, 24th; 31st. Exceptions: first, second, third, fifth, eighth, ninth, twelfth, twentieth, twenty-first.

Prepositions: on Monday / on 14 October; in October / in autumn; at 9 a.m.

“We are leaving on Friday, 16th October.”

Days, months and seasons

Days: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday.

Months: January, February, March, April, May, June, July, August, September, October, November, December.

Seasons: spring, summer, autumn (UK) / fall (US), winter.

CHAPTER 05 · FUTURE PLANS

Turn your dream into a plan

5

Three useful ways to talk about the future

Going to · Your intention

I am / we are + going to + base verb

Positive: We are going to visit Kyoto. Negative: We aren’t going to drive. Question: Are you going to book a hotel?

Signal: a plan or intention already in mind; also a prediction based on evidence.

Present continuous · An arranged plan

am / is / are + verb-ing + future time

Positive: We are flying on Saturday. Negative: We aren’t staying long. Question: Are you meeting friends?

Signal: a fixed arrangement, often booked or agreed.

Will · A decision now, offer or prediction

will + base verb

Positive: I’ll call the hotel. Negative: I won’t forget. Question: Will you join us?

Signal: spontaneous decision, promise, offer or opinion-based prediction.

“We’re going to visit Lisbon. We’re flying on 12th June. I’ll send you the details tonight.”

CHAPTER 06 · COMMUNICATION WORKSHOP

Invite, suggest, negotiate, decide

6

Modals · Make suggestions and ask politely

Could: a possible idea — “We could visit the castle.” Should: advice — “We should book early.” Shall we…? a suggestion — “Shall we take the train?” Would you like to…? an invitation — “Would you like to come with us?”

modal + base verb (no “to” after could / should)

Negative: We shouldn’t rush. Questions: Could we leave earlier? Should we book now?

“What should we do first?” “We could explore the old town.”

Write a message to invite someone

Include: destination, British-style dates, what you want to do, why you chose it, and a question asking for their opinion.

A2: “Hi! Would you like to go to Edinburgh with me from 10th to 17th May? We can visit the castle and walk around the city. I think it will be lovely. What do you think?”

B1: “Hi! I’ve been looking at a few ideas for our next holiday, and Edinburgh seems like a wonderful choice. We could travel from 10th to 17th May, explore the historic centre and enjoy some lovely walks. Would you be interested, or would you prefer somewhere warmer?”

CHAPTER 07 · READING & COMMUNICATION CHECKPOINT

Ready to use your English?

Original exam-inspired practice: read a realistic message, find information and respond. This is not an official exam paper.

Read this message

Hi! I found two possible trips for our holiday. Edinburgh has interesting museums and beautiful historic streets, but the weather may be cool. Lisbon is usually warmer, and we could explore the city by tram. I’m free from 12th to 19th June, although I would rather travel by train if possible. Which destination would you choose? Let me know by Friday! — Alex

Writing task · Reply to Alex

Write 50–80 words. Choose a place, compare the two options, mention the dates and make one suggestion.

A2: “Hi Alex! I prefer Lisbon because it is warmer than Edinburgh. I like the idea of travelling by tram. The dates are good for me. We could visit the old town together. What do you think?”

B1: “Hi Alex, thanks for looking into both options. Although Edinburgh sounds fascinating, I’d choose Lisbon because I’d love some warmer weather and the chance to explore its colourful neighbourhoods. The dates from 12th to 19th June suit me. Shall we compare transport options before making a final decision?”
