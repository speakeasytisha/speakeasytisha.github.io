LANGUAGE LESSON · STEP 1

Express a preference · prefer / would prefer

Two useful patterns. Learn them separately, then compare a hotel and a B&B.

01

I prefer · your general preference

Form: I prefer + noun / verb-ing.

I prefer small hotels.
I prefer staying downtown.

Negative: I don’t prefer busy hotels.

02

I would prefer · one particular choice

Form: I would prefer + noun / to + base verb.

I would prefer a quiet room.
I would prefer to stay downtown.

Negative: I would prefer not to stay far away.

03

Ask someone about their preference

Question: Would you prefer + noun + or + noun?

Would you prefer a hotel or an apartment?

Answer: I would prefer an apartment, please.

04

French connection & common mistake

Je préfère → I prefer.

Je préférerais → I would prefer.

❌ I would prefer stay.
✓ I would prefer to stay.

LANGUAGE LESSON · STEP 2

Comparatives and superlatives · One idea at a time

First learn how to compare two choices. Then learn how to identify one option in a group.

01

Comparative · compare two things

Meaning: plus … que / moins … que.

Short adjectives: adjective + -er + than.

quiet → quieter than

Long adjectives: more + adjective + than.

expensive → more expensive than

Negative: This hotel is not cheaper than that one.

02

Superlative · choose one from a group

Meaning: le/la plus …

Short adjectives: the + adjective + -est.

cheap → the cheapest

Long adjectives: the most + adjective.

luxurious → the most luxurious

Question: Which hotel is the most convenient?

03

Spelling changes · apply the rules

Final -e: nice → nicer → nicest.

Consonant + y: busy → busier → busiest.

Short vowel + consonant: big → bigger → biggest.

Compare: a big room → a bigger room → the biggest room.

04

Irregular adjectives · learn separately

good → better → the best

bad → worse → the worst

far → farther/further → the farthest/furthest

Never say “more better”.

05

Questions and answers

Two: Is this hotel cheaper than that one?

Yes, it is. / No, it isn’t.

Several: Which is the quietest option?

The B&B is the quietest.

06

Common errors to avoid

❌ more cheaper → ✓ cheaper

❌ the most cheapest → ✓ the cheapest

❌ quieter that → ✓ quieter than

Try aloud: This room is larger, but that one is the quietest.

Practise with the exercises immediately below. First compare two rooms, then choose the best of three.

NEW COMPLETE MINI LESSON · TELLING TIME

Telling the time · From basic hours to hotel schedules

Hotels give check-in, breakfast, parking and check-out times. Learn how to read, ask, understand and confirm the time.

01

1 · Ask what time it is

Question: What time is it?

Answer: It’s three o’clock. (3:00)

Useful hotel question: What time is check-in?

02

2 · Digital times · say hour then minutes

7:05 → seven oh five
8:15 → eight fifteen
9:30 → nine thirty
10:45 → ten forty-five

For minutes 01–09, “oh” is common.

03

3 · Past / to · useful UK expressions

8:15 → a quarter past eight
8:30 → half past eight
8:45 → a quarter to nine

These are common in British English. Digital-style times work in both varieties.

04

4 · A.M. / P.M. · morning or afternoon

8:00 a.m. → eight in the morning.
3:00 p.m. → three in the afternoon.
7:00 p.m. → seven in the evening.

12:00 a.m. = midnight · 12:00 p.m. = noon. Use “noon” and “midnight” to avoid confusion.

05

5 · 12-hour / 24-hour clock

14:30 → 2:30 p.m. → two thirty in the afternoon.

18:45 → 6:45 p.m. → six forty-five in the evening.

21:00 → 9:00 p.m. → nine in the evening.

Say at with clock times: Breakfast starts at seven.

06

6 · How to ask about a schedule

BE: What time is check-out?

DOES: What time does breakfast start?

CAN: Can I check in at two?

FROM / UNTIL: Breakfast is served from seven until ten.

07

7 · Confirm what you heard

“Sorry, did you say seven fifteen or seven fifty?”

“So breakfast starts at 7:30 a.m., is that right?”

“Could you repeat the check-out time, please?”

08

8 · Hotel schedule example

Breakfast: 7:00–10:00 a.m.
Check-in: from 3:00 p.m.
Check-out: by 11:00 a.m.
Restaurant: opens at 6:30 p.m.

Q: When does breakfast finish?
A: At ten in the morning.

FR : « À quelle heure ? » = “What time…?” Use at for a precise time, from … to/until for a time range, and by for a deadline.

03 / LANGUAGE

Polite questions at a hotel

A complete mini-lesson before the front-desk role-play.

Direct and polite questions · Both are correct

01

Direct · simple and clear

Form: Do / Does + subject + base verb?

“Do you have parking?”

“Does the hotel offer breakfast?”

Perfect for a clear A2 question.

02

Polite · softer and more formal

Form: Could you + base verb…?

“Could you tell me if parking is available?”

“Could you explain the breakfast options?”

Useful for requests at reception.

A direct question is not necessarily impolite. Add please, thank you, and a friendly tone when appropriate.

LANGUAGE LESSON · ONE PATTERN AT A TIME

Would like · Can · Could · Do you have…?

Learn each pattern before using them together in a conversation.

01

I would like · say what you want

+ noun: I’d like a double room.

+ to + verb: I’d like to check in.

Negative: I would not like a room near the lift.

Question: Would you like a receipt?

02

Can · ability or a simple request

Can + subject + base verb?

Can I check in now?
Can you help me?

Negative: I can’t find my booking.

Answer: Yes, you can. / Sorry, you can’t.

03

Could · a courteous request

Could you + base verb…?

Could you check my reservation?

Could I have a receipt, please?

Answer: Of course. / Certainly.

04

Do you have…? · availability

Do you have + noun?

Do you have a restaurant?

Third person: Does the hotel have parking?

Negative: We don’t have a shuttle. It doesn’t have a pool.

05

Choose the correct form

What you want: I’d like…

Possibility: Can I…?

More polite: Could you…?

Availability: Do you have…?

Mistakes to avoid

❌ Could you to help me?

❌ I would like check in.

LANGUAGE LESSON

Present simple questions for hotel facts

Use the present simple for schedules and facts: Breakfast starts at seven. To ask a question, use do/does (except with be).

✓ Positive

Breakfast starts at 7.
The hotel has a restaurant.

✕ Negative

Breakfast doesn’t start at 6.
We don’t need a taxi.

? Question

What time does breakfast start?
Where is the elevator?

After does, the main verb has no -s: “What time does breakfast start?” not “starts”. “Where is…” uses the verb be and needs no “does”.

05 / UNDERSTAND

Paying, deposits and card holds

Know the difference before handing over your card.

What might the hotel charge? · Six different concepts

A price, a charge and a temporary hold are not always the same thing.

01

Room rate

The price of your accommodation, often per night.

Ask: What is the nightly rate?

02

Taxes

Additional government charges that may be added to the price.

Ask: Are taxes included?

03

Security deposit

Money collected as security and potentially refunded subject to the hotel’s conditions.

Ask: Is the deposit refundable?

04

Card hold / pre-authorisation

Money or available credit temporarily reserved—not necessarily a final charge.

Ask: Is this a temporary hold?

05

Destination / resort fee

An extra hotel fee where applicable; ask whether it is mandatory and included in the total.

Ask: Are there any extra fees?

06

Receipt / final bill

A written record of what was charged.

Ask: Could I have an itemised receipt, please?

Always confirm the amount and conditions with the property. A card provider or bank may control how quickly released funds become available.

PAYMENT WORKSHOP · STEP 1

What exactly is a card hold?

A hold is a temporary reservation of funds or available credit. It is not automatically a completed payment.

01

1 · Card hold / pre-authorisation

The hotel temporarily reserves an amount for possible extra costs (incidentals), such as room service.

FR: empreinte bancaire / préautorisation.

02

2 · A simple $100 example

Room charge: $200.

Separate temporary hold: $100.

The $100 may be unavailable temporarily, but does not automatically become a final $100 charge.

03

3 · Different payment words

Charge: money charged to your account.

Refundable deposit: money taken and potentially paid back.

Hold: temporary reservation.

Pending transaction: operation not yet final.

04

4 · What to ask the receptionist

“How much is the hold?”
“Is it a charge or a pre-authorisation?”
“When will it be released?”
“Can I use my debit card?”

05

5 · What happens afterward?

If there are no extra charges, the hotel releases the hold according to its policy. The card issuer or bank determines when the funds become available again.

06

6 · French explanation

Une préautorisation peut bloquer temporairement une somme ou une partie du plafond disponible sans être un débit définitif.

Demandez le montant et le délai de libération.

PAYMENT WORKSHOP · STEP 2

How much and how many · Complete grammar

French uses « combien de » for many contexts; in English, look at the noun and decide whether you can count individual items.

01

1 · Countable nouns → HOW MANY

one guest → two guests; one ticket → three tickets.

Question: How many guests are staying?

Answer: Two guests.

Form: How many + plural noun…?

02

2 · Uncountable nouns → HOW MUCH

water, money, information, advice, luggage, time, traffic.

Question: How much water do you need?

Answer: A little water.

Form: How much + uncountable noun…?

03

3 · Prices → HOW MUCH

How much is breakfast?
How much are these tickets?
How much does parking cost?

Important: We do not use “How many” to ask a price.

04

4 · Ask about existence

Countable: How many towels are there?

Uncountable: How much water is there?

Positive: There are four towels / There is some water.

Negative: There aren’t many towels / There isn’t much water.

05

5 · Quantities: many, much, a lot of

Countable: many, a few, several (a few towels).

Uncountable: much, a little (a little water).

Both: a lot of (a lot of guests / water).

06

6 · The useful exceptions

Coffee: uncountable as a drink; “a coffee” = one serving.

Luggage: uncountable; say “two bags” or “two pieces of luggage”.

Advice / information: uncountable; “some advice”, “a piece of information”.

Not “how many money” or “two informations”. Say “how much money” and “two pieces of information”.

LANGUAGE LESSON

Where is / Where are · Prepositions

Ask Where is for singular places and Where are for plural things. Use on for floors, next to for beside, opposite for facing, and between for two landmarks.

✓ Positive

✕ Negative

The lift isn’t next to reception.

? Question

Where is the breakfast room?

“Where is the elevators?” is incorrect because elevators is plural: “Where are the elevators?”

QUESTION WORKSHOP · STEP BY STEP

How to make questions · Learn one structure at a time

Build the question in this order: choose the type of verb → move the helper verb → add a WH word when you need a detail.

01

1 · YES / NO with BE

Form: Am / Is / Are + subject…?

Is breakfast included?
Are the elevators nearby?

Negative: Breakfast isn’t included.

02

2 · YES / NO with THERE

Form: Is / Are + there…?

Is there a restaurant?
Are there any parking spaces?

Negative: There aren’t any free spaces.

03

3 · YES / NO with DO / DOES

Form: Do / Does + subject + base verb?

Do you offer breakfast?
Does the hotel have parking?

❌ Does the hotel has…?

04

4 · POLITE request with CAN / COULD

Form: Can / Could + subject + base verb?

Can you show me the way?
Could you tell me the opening hours?

Negative: I can’t find the lift.

05

5 · WH words · choose what to ask

Where = place · Where is the lift?
When / what time = time · When does breakfast start?
How = method · How do I get there?
Which = choice · Which floor?
Why = reason · Why is parking extra?

06

6 · WH + correct word order

WH + BE: Where is the lift?

WH + DOES: When does breakfast start?

WH + DO: How do I get there?

Challenge: Could you tell me where the lift is? (indirect question).

FR : avec be, inversez le sujet et is/are. Avec les autres verbes, utilisez do/does + verbe de base. Commencez par les questions directes.

SMALL TALK · FRIENDLY CONVERSATION

Small talk · One social skill at a time

Small talk is a short, friendly exchange. It can be warm and natural without slang or overly personal questions.

01

1 · What is small talk?

A brief conversation about an everyday subject: a greeting, the weather, a trip, or sightseeing plans.

You can answer briefly. You do not have to disclose private information.

02

2 · Four steps · Start a conversation

  1. Greet: Hello! How are you?
  2. Respond: I’m well, thank you.
  3. Add one detail: I’m visiting from France.
  4. Ask back: And you? Do you live here?

Finish: It was lovely talking to you!

03

3 · Everyday conversation

Setting: hotel lobby, café, museum queue.

“Hello! Is this your first visit?”

“Yes, it is. I’m here for a week.”

Tone: friendly and simple, with natural short sentences.

04

4 · At a nice restaurant

Setting: greeting a host or talking to another guest.

“Good evening. Thank you for welcoming us.”

“It’s a pleasure. Is this your first time dining here?”

“Yes, and we’re looking forward to it.”

Tone: a little more formal, with complete sentences.

05

5 · Useful questions · A2 first

Where are you from?
Is this your first visit?
How long are you staying?
What are you planning to visit?
What do you enjoy doing?

B1 extension: Have you been to San Francisco before? (present perfect; optional after teaching).

06

6 · Model answers · Add information

Keep talking: And you? / What about you?

07

7 · Privacy · What not to ask

Choose non-intrusive subjects. Avoid asking strangers about their income, age, relationships or other personal matters.

You may say “I’d rather not say” or “I’m not sure yet.”
