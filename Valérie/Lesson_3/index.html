01 / EXPLORE

Find your perfect San Francisco stay

Continue from the hotel-choice activity in your previous lesson.

1. What kind of accommodation?

Hotel: reception, housekeeping, room service or a concierge may be available. Bed and breakfast (B&B): often smaller and more personal; breakfast may be included. Serviced apartment / holiday rental: more space, often a kitchen, and sometimes no staffed reception.

American English: vacation rental, elevator, front desk. British English: holiday let, lift, reception.

First decision: Do you prefer a lively central hotel, a traditional B&B, or a private apartment? Why?

LANGUAGE LESSON

Expressing a preference: prefer / would prefer

I prefer + noun / -ing: I prefer small hotels. I prefer staying near the centre. I would prefer + noun / to + verb: I would prefer a quiet room. I would prefer to stay downtown.

✓ Positive

I would prefer a B&B.

✕ Negative

I would prefer not to stay far away.

? Question

Would you prefer a hotel or an apartment?

Do not say “I would prefer stay”. Say “I would prefer to stay”. FR: « Je préférerais séjourner… »

LANGUAGE LESSON · BEFORE SPEAKING

Comparatives and superlatives · Compare your stays

Comparative = two choices: short adjective + -er + than (quiet → quieter than); longer adjective → more + adjective + than (more expensive than). Superlative = one in a group: the + -est (the cheapest) or the most + adjective (the most luxurious).

Positive

The B&B is quiet.

Comparative

The B&B is quieter than a busy hotel.

Superlative

Which is the quietest option?

Spelling: big → bigger → biggest (double final consonant); nice → nicer → nicest (drop final e); busy → busier → busiest (y → i). Irregular: good → better → best; bad → worse → worst; far → farther/further → farthest/furthest.

Questions: Is this hotel cheaper than that one? Which hotel is the most expensive? Negatives: This room is not bigger than that room. It is not the cheapest choice.

FR: « plus … que » = comparative; « le/la plus … » = superlative. Avoid more cheaper and the most cheapest.

02 / PLAN

Choose dates, guests and room types

Learn the words you need before opening the booking form.

1. Read a booking listing

Check-in: arrival date. Check-out: departure date. Guests: the people staying. Room type: the bed arrangement. Rate: the price for the room, often per night.

US date: October 22, 2026 → “October twenty-second”. UK date: 22 October 2026 → “the twenty-second of October”. Say “from October twenty-second to October twenty-fifth” for a three-night stay.

Number practice: 13 / thirteen; 30 / thirty; 15 / fifteen; 50 / fifty; 115 / one hundred fifteen (US), one hundred and fifteen (UK).

MINI LESSON · NUMBERS & DATES

Read a real booking listing

Example: Check-in: October 22, 2026 · Check-out: October 25, 2026 · 2 adults · 1 queen room · $215 per night (illustrative).

How many nights? Three. What is the nightly rate? Two hundred fifteen dollars. When do you check out? October twenty-fifth.

US: October twenty-second, twenty twenty-six. UK: the twenty-second of October, twenty twenty-six. Ordinals: 1st first, 2nd second, 3rd third, 4th fourth, 21st twenty-first, 22nd twenty-second, 23rd twenty-third.

Number traps: thirteen (13) vs thirty (30); fifteen (15) vs fifty (50). US: one hundred fifteen; UK: one hundred and fifteen.

LANGUAGE LESSON

There is / There are · Describing what is available

Use there is for one thing or uncountable nouns, and there are for plural things. FR: « Il y a ».

✓ Positive

There is a king bed.
There are two beds.

✕ Negative

There isn’t a kitchen.
There aren’t two beds.

? Question

Is there a lift?
Are there two bathrooms?

A question reverses the order: Is there…? / Are there…? Do not say “There is a lift?” in a standard direct question.

03 / LANGUAGE

Polite questions at a hotel

A complete mini-lesson before the front-desk role-play.

1. Notice the difference

Direct: “Do you have parking?” Polite: “Could you tell me if parking is available?” Both are useful. Start with the simpler version, then try the polite version.

LANGUAGE LESSON

Would like · Can / Could · Do you have…?

I’d like + noun: I’d like a double room. I’d like to + verb: I’d like to check in. FR: « Je voudrais… ».

Can / Could + base verb: Can you help me? Could you recommend a restaurant? Negative: I can’t find my booking. I couldn’t find the entrance. Question: Could you check my reservation? Short answer: Yes, I can. / Sorry, I can’t.

Do you have + noun? Do you have a restaurant? Negative: We don’t have a shuttle. Third person: Does the hotel have parking? / It doesn’t have parking.

✓ Positive

I’d like to check in.
We have breakfast.

✕ Negative

I don’t need parking.
We can’t check in yet.

? Question

Could you help me?
Does the hotel have a lift?

After can/could, use the base verb: “Could you help?” not “Could you to help?” After “I’d like”, use to + verb.

04 / ARRIVE

Check in · The real procedure

Learn what happens at reception before you act it out.

CULTURE & LANGUAGE · BEFORE CHECK-IN

How to greet hotel staff · US and France

France: Bonjour Madame / Monsieur is a normal polite opening; a greeting before a request is important. United States: Hello! / Good morning! / Good afternoon! + a friendly smile is natural. Hi! is common and friendly, even in many hotels; Hey! can feel too casual at reception. Good evening is a greeting; good night is usually a goodbye or bedtime wish.

Natural dialogue: Receptionist: “Good afternoon! Welcome to San Francisco.” You: “Hello! I have a reservation under the name Valérie.” Receptionist: “May I see your ID, please?” You: “Of course. Here you are. Thank you!”

Politeness: In American service settings, “How are you?” is often a brief friendly greeting; “Good, thanks. And you?” is a natural answer. Avoid assuming everyone greets the same way.

Practise greetings before checking in

1. Follow the check-in sequence

  1. Walk to the front desk (US) / reception (UK).
  2. Greet the receptionist: “Hello, I have a reservation under the name…”
  3. Show your passport or ID if requested.
  4. Confirm the number of nights and room type.
  5. Provide a payment card for incidentals or a security hold, if required.
  6. Receive your key card, room number and directions to the lift/elevator.
  7. Ask about breakfast times, Wi-Fi and checkout.

LANGUAGE LESSON

Present simple questions for hotel facts

Use the present simple for schedules and facts: Breakfast starts at seven. To ask a question, use do/does (except with be).

✓ Positive

Breakfast starts at 7.
The hotel has a restaurant.

✕ Negative

Breakfast doesn’t start at 6.
We don’t need a taxi.

? Question

What time does breakfast start?
Where is the elevator?

After does, the main verb has no -s: “What time does breakfast start?” not “starts”. “Where is…” uses the verb be and needs no “does”.

05 / UNDERSTAND

Paying, deposits and card holds

Know the difference before handing over your card.

1. What might the hotel charge?

Room rate: price of your stay. Taxes: added local charges. Security deposit: money taken and potentially refunded later. Pre-authorisation / incidental hold: a temporary amount reserved on your card, not necessarily a completed charge. Resort / destination fee: an additional property fee, if applicable. Receipt: proof of payment.

Ask the hotel about the amount, timing and refund/release conditions; policies vary by hotel and payment card.

Learn the words first

credit card ＋

FR: carte de crédit

Can I pay by credit card?

debit card ＋

FR: carte de débit

Do you accept debit cards?

security deposit ＋

FR: dépôt de garantie

Is there a security deposit?

card hold ＋

FR: empreinte bancaire / somme bloquée

How much is the card hold?

receipt ＋

FR: reçu

Could I have a receipt, please?

total including tax ＋

FR: total taxes comprises

Is that the total including tax?

LANGUAGE LESSON

How much / How many · Prices

How much asks about price or uncountable amounts: “How much is the deposit?” How many asks about countable items: “How many nights?”

✓ Statement

The deposit is $100.
There are three nights.

✕ Negative

There isn’t an extra fee.
I don’t need a receipt.

? Question

How much is the hold?
How many nights are booked?

Say one hundred dollars for $100. “How many money?” is incorrect; money is uncountable, so say “How much money?”

06 / SETTLE IN

Enjoy your hotel

Ask about breakfast, parking and finding your way around.

1. Ask useful questions

Breakfast: “Is breakfast included?” “What time is breakfast served?” Restaurant: “Is there a restaurant in the hotel?” Parking: “Is overnight parking available?” Directions: “Where is the lift?” “Is the breakfast room on the ground floor?”

American English: first floor = street level; second floor = one level up. British English: ground floor = street level; first floor = one level up. Confirm the floor when travelling.

Learn the words first

amenities / facilities ＋

FR: équipements / services

What amenities does the hotel offer?

breakfast included ＋

FR: petit-déjeuner compris

Is breakfast included?

valet parking ＋

FR: service de voiturier

Do you offer valet parking?

ground floor ＋

FR: rez-de-chaussée (UK)

The restaurant is on the ground floor.

concierge ＋

FR: concierge

Could I speak to the concierge?

laundry service ＋

FR: service de blanchisserie

Is there a laundry service?

LANGUAGE LESSON

Where is / Where are · Prepositions

Ask Where is for singular places and Where are for plural things. Use on for floors, next to for beside, opposite for facing, and between for two landmarks.

✓ Positive

The restaurant is on floor two.

✕ Negative

The lift isn’t next to reception.

? Question

Where is the breakfast room?

“Where is the elevators?” is incorrect because elevators is plural: “Where are the elevators?”

07 / DISCOVER

Ask for a local recommendation

Get ready for the next chapter: exploring San Francisco.

1. Choose what you enjoy

Ocean & nature: walk by the bay and enjoy views of the Golden Gate Bridge. Culture: explore museums or a neighbourhood bookshop. Cinema: ask about famous film locations. Food: ask for a good place for breakfast or dinner.

Start with: “Could you recommend…?” Follow up with: “How do I get there?” “Is it within walking distance?”

Learn the words first

scenic view ＋

FR: beau panorama

local recommendation ＋

FR: conseil local

Do you have a local recommendation?

bookshop / bookstore ＋

FR: librairie (UK / US)

Is there a good bookstore nearby?

Food around the world · country and cuisine

Country → adjective / cuisine: France → French food; Italy → Italian food; Greece → Greek food; China → Chinese food; Japan → Japanese food; Mexico → Mexican food; the United States → American food; India → Indian food; Thailand → Thai food; Spain → Spanish food; Vietnam → Vietnamese food.

Useful patterns: “I'd like to try Italian food.” “Is there a good Japanese restaurant nearby?” “Could you recommend a Greek restaurant within walking distance?” “Do they have vegetarian options?” “Do I need a reservation?” “Is it expensive?”

Careful: say French restaurant, not “France restaurant”; Italian cuisine, not “Italy cuisine”. US: check / takeout; UK: bill / takeaway.

LANGUAGE LESSON

Could you recommend + noun / -ing?

Could you recommend a restaurant? or Could you recommend visiting a museum? Use a noun or verb + -ing after recommend.

✕ Negative

Do not say “recommend me to visit”. Say “recommend visiting” or “recommend a visit to…”.
