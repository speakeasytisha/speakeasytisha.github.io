LANGUAGE TOOL · 1

I am / You are — introducing yourself

Use am / is / are to say who you are, where you are and how you feel.

✓ Positive

I am Valérie.
You are in California.

✕ Negative

I am not American.
It is not my first trip.

? Question

Are you here on holiday?
Is this your bag?

Remember: I am → I'm; you are → you're; is not → isn't. Never say “I is”.

Simple questions with “be”

03 / BAGGAGE CLAIM

Find your suitcase

After immigration, follow the signs to baggage claim, collect your bag and continue through customs.

1. Your useful words

LANGUAGE TOOL · 3

There is / There are

Use there is for one thing and there are for several things.

✓ Positive

There is a suitcase.
There are two bags.

✕ Negative

There isn't a trolley.
There aren't any signs.

? Question

Is there a trolley?
Are there any signs?

British English: luggage trolley. American English: baggage cart. “Luggage” and “baggage” are normally uncountable: say two bags, not “two luggages”.

2. Choose the right expression

LANGUAGE TOOL · 3B · LEARN BEFORE SPEAKING

Ask for help: CAN and COULD

What is a modal? A modal is a helping verb. Can expresses ability or a simple request; could makes a request more polite. In an airport, both are useful.

✓ Positive

I can find the information desk.
The employee can help me.

✕ Negative

I cannot / can't find my bag.
I couldn't find the desk earlier.

? Questions / requests

Can you help me, please?
Could you check my bag, please?

Rule: subject + can/could + base verb. No to, no -s: “Could you help?”, not “Could you to help?” The modal goes before the subject in questions. Could is polite here, not necessarily past tense.

Airport employee

How can I help you?

You

Excuse me. I can't find my suitcase. Could you help me, please?

Airport employee

Of course. Do you have your baggage tag?

Useful phrases: Excuse me… · My suitcase hasn't arrived (stretch) · I can't find my bag. · Where is the baggage service desk? · Could you repeat that, please?

3. Practise modals before asking for help

Choose from the visible words. Then click the tiles to build a polite request. Read your sentence aloud before the role-play.

4. Ask for help — role-play

Your bag has not appeared. What could you say to an airport employee?

A2: Excuse me. I can't find my suitcase. Can you help me, please?

B1: Excuse me, I've been waiting at the baggage carousel, but my suitcase hasn't arrived. Could you tell me where to report it?

04 / TRANSPORT

How will you get into the city?

Choose the option that suits your trip. BART, taxis, rideshare and rental cars are possible at SFO; follow current airport signs.

1. Your journey from the airport, step by step

Step A — Follow the signs. After customs, enter the arrivals hall. Look for Ground Transportation, BART, Taxi, Ride App Pickup or Rental Car Center. The exact pickup locations can change: check SFO signs.

Step B — Decide where you're going. Your destination is your hotel in San Francisco. Say: “I'm going to a hotel near Union Square.” For BART, check whether your destination is close to a station; for a taxi or ride app, give the hotel address.

Step C — Compare the options. BART = train, generally economical for central destinations; taxi = direct with luggage; Uber/Lyft = book in an app and follow designated pickup instructions; rental car = more useful for a road trip than for central city parking.

Step D — Ask before you travel. “Where is the BART station?” “Where can I get a taxi?” “How much is the fare?” “Can I pay by card?”

UK / US: city centre / downtown · taxi rank / taxi stand · hire car / rental car · single ticket / one-way ticket.

LANGUAGE TOOL · 4

Polite requests: Can I…? / Could you…?

Use Can I…? to ask permission or request something. Use Could you…? for a particularly polite request.

✓ Request

Can I pay by card?

✕ Negative

I can't find the taxi rank.

? Polite question

Could you take me to my hotel, please?

After can / could, use the base verb: “Could you help?” not “Could you to help?” In US English, taxi stand is common; in UK English, taxi rank.

2. Choose, then practise a real conversation

BART: “Excuse me, where is the BART station?” → “Follow the signs to the station.”

Taxi: “Could you take me to this address, please?” → “Of course. Please get in.”

Ride app: “Is this the pickup area for Uber?” → “Please check the zone shown in your app.”

Rental car: “Where is the Rental Car Center?” → “Follow the signs for the AirTrain.” At the desk: “I'd like to collect my reservation. Here's my driving licence and credit card.” Check licence, payment and rental requirements with the provider.

Listen and repeat:

3. Practise a polite request

Talk to your driver or station employee

Ask how to get to your hotel, and ask about the price.

A2: Hello. Can you take me to my hotel, please? How much is it?

B1: Hello, could you tell me the easiest way to get downtown? I'd also like to know roughly how much the journey will cost.

05 / YOUR HOTEL

Choose your San Francisco stay

Explore three different areas and build a simple booking request. These are learning examples, not live offers.

1. Plan the stay before choosing a room

Step A — Choose your neighbourhood. Fisherman's Wharf is lively and close to waterfront attractions; Union Square is central for shops and transit; the Marina is quieter and near the bay. Consider walking distance and transport access.

Step B — Choose dates. Say: “I'd like to check in on October 20 and check out on October 23.” In American English dates are commonly spoken October twentieth; in British English the twentieth of October.

Step C — Choose your room. Single room (one guest), double room (one double bed), twin room (two beds). Check the room description: occupancy varies.

Step D — Ask what is included. “Is breakfast included?” “Is parking available?” “Do you have an airport shuttle?” “What time is check-in?”

Step E — Check the total. Ask about taxes, extra fees, cancellation rules and parking charges before confirming. The choices below are illustrative neighbourhood examples, not real hotel listings or prices.

LANGUAGE TOOL · 5

I'd like + noun / I'd like to + verb

Use I'd like to express a preference politely.

✓ Positive

I'd like a quiet room.
I'd like to book a room.

✕ Negative

I wouldn't like a noisy room.

? Question

Would you like breakfast?

“I'd like” = “I would like”. Say I'd like to book, not “I'd like book”.

2. Practise booking language

Use the visible word bank to complete each sentence. Read it aloud, then use the booking builder.

Build your first booking request

Choose an area, dates, number of guests and room type. Your English message updates automatically.

Ask the hotel a question

Ask about parking, airport transfers or breakfast.

A2: Hello. I'd like a double room for two nights. Is breakfast included?

B1: Hello, I'd like to enquire about a double room for two guests. Could you tell me whether breakfast is included and if you offer airport transfers?
