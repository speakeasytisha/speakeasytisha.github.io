02 / MY STORY

Introducing Myself

Begin with the introduction you already created, then add personal details and your specific aircraft-interior work.

YOUR CONFIRMED INTRODUCTION

Five sentences you can rely on

I'm a mechanical designer working in the aviation sector.
My company operates in the aviation industry.
I help identify technical issues and find solutions.
I particularly enjoy finding creative solutions.
My goal is to communicate more naturally with international colleagues.
MAKE IT MORE SPECIFIC

Introduce what you actually design

A2+: I design furniture for aircraft cabins, including storage cabinets. I work with technical drawings.

B1: I'm a mechanical designer specialising in aircraft cabin interiors. I design furniture and storage units, and I work on how different components fit together.

B1+: My role involves designing aircraft cabin furniture, coordinating technical details, and making sure separate modules can be assembled correctly.

En anglais professionnel, « aircraft cabin furniture », « cabin interiors » et « galley cabinets » sont naturels selon les pièces exactes. Utilisez « mechanical designer » pour votre intitulé déjà validé.

How your introduction works

Job title · I am a mechanical designer. / I work as a mechanical designer.
Use I am + a/an + job title to name your profession. Use I work as + a/an + job title to describe your role, and in + field to add the industry. Both sentences can answer “What do you do?”
Je suis conceptrice mécanique. / Je travaille comme conceptrice mécanique dans l’aéronautique. En anglais, gardez a devant mechanical designer.
Industry · I'm a mechanical designer working in the aviation sector.
Here working in aviation describes the designer. It is a shorter way of saying “a mechanical designer who works in aviation.” The main verb is am in “I'm a mechanical designer.” You can also use two sentences: “I'm a mechanical designer. I work in aviation.” To talk about a current project, use am + working: “I'm working on a new cabinet this week.”
working in aviation = « qui travaille dans l’aéronautique ». Pour un projet en cours, dites par exemple I'm working on a new cabinet this week (« Je travaille sur un nouveau meuble cette semaine »).
Company facts · My company operates in the aviation industry.
Use the present simple for facts, professions and regular work. My company works like it, so add -s to operate. With I, use the base form: “I design cabin furniture.”
Présent simple pour un fait ou une activité habituelle. My company operates in aviation = « Mon entreprise exerce son activité dans l’aéronautique ».
Contribution · I help identify technical issues and find solutions.
Use help + base verb. Help + to + base verb also works: “I help to identify technical issues.” Here identify and find share help.
J’aide à repérer les problèmes techniques et à trouver des solutions. En anglais, le to après help est facultatif.
Enjoyment · I particularly enjoy finding creative solutions.
Use enjoy + verb-ing when you name an activity you like. Find becomes finding. Try “I enjoy drawing” or “I enjoy solving design problems.”
J’aime particulièrement trouver des solutions créatives. Après enjoy, un verbe prend la forme en -ing.
Goal · My goal is to communicate more naturally with international colleagues.
Use My goal is + to + base verb for what you want to achieve. Try “My goal is to speak up in meetings.”
Mon objectif est de communiquer plus naturellement avec mes collègues à l’international. L’infinitif anglais est to communicate.
BUILD YOUR INTRODUCTION

Personal → professional → purpose

1. Home: I was born in Albi and I still live there. (Je suis née à Albi et j'y habite toujours.)

2. Role: I work as a mechanical designer in aviation. (Je travaille comme conceptrice mécanique.)

3. Responsibilities: I design cabin furniture and explain technical drawings.

4. Team: I work with colleagues in Italy, Germany and Spain.

5. Motivation: I'd like to feel more confident speaking English at work.

6. Personal touch: Outside work, I'm interested in art, cars and motorbikes. I have a dog and a cat. (À personnaliser selon ce que vous souhaitez partager.)

Opening formula
Hi, I'm Laura.
I'm from Albi, in southwest France.
I work as a mechanical designer.
My job involves designing cabin furniture.
I work with international colleagues.
What about you?

Talk about confidence and interests

I'd like to feel more confident speaking English at work.
I'd like + to + base verb expresses a wish. Confident + verb-ing names the activity you feel confident doing. You can also say “I’d like to feel more confident about speaking English in meetings.”
J’aimerais me sentir plus à l’aise en parlant anglais au travail. To feel exprime ce que vous souhaitez, et speaking précise l’activité.
I'm interested in art. / I'm interested in learning more about design.
In is the preposition that goes with interested when you name what interests you. Use interested in + noun (art) or interested in + verb-ing (learning).
Je m’intéresse à l’art. / J’aimerais en apprendre davantage sur la conception. Retenez interested in comme une expression.

03 / LANGUAGE TOOLS

Grammar you can actually use.

Learn the meaning, the sentence structure, and common mistakes before practising.

A / HABITS & RESPONSIBILITIES

Present simple

Use: regular tasks, facts, job responsibilities.

I / we / they + design
She / he + designs

Negative: I don't design engines. / She doesn't design engines.

Question: Do you work with drawings? / Does she work in aviation?

Keywords: usually · often · every day

Exception : après does/doesn't, verbe sans -s : « Does she design…? »

B / NOW & CURRENT PROJECTS

Present continuous

Use: something happening now or a temporary current project.

I am working
We are checking

Negative: We aren't assembling the modules today.

Question: Are you checking the drawings?

Keywords: now · at the moment · this week

« I design cabinets » = métier/habitude ; « I'm designing a cabinet » = projet en cours.

C / POLITE TECHNICAL REQUESTS

Could you…?

Use: request help, confirm information, ask for a change.

Could you + base verb + …?

Example: Could you check the dimensions, please?

Negative request: Could you please avoid changing the mounting points?

Reply: Certainly. / Of course. / I'll check and get back to you.

Ne dites pas « Could you to check » : pas de « to » après could.

CONNECT IDEAS

Because, so, and to

because + subject + verb → reason: “We need a revision because the cabinet doesn't fit.” parce que

so + subject + verb → result: “The cabinet doesn't fit, so we need a revision.” donc

to + base verb → purpose: “We need a revision to improve the fit.” pour + infinitif

Exception: “because of + noun”: “because of a dimension issue.”

REASON, RESULT OR PURPOSE?

Why do we need a revision?
Because the cabinet doesn't fit.

What happens as a result?
The cabinet doesn't fit, so we need a revision.

What are we trying to achieve?
We need a revision to improve the fit.

Say it well · Responsibilities, tasks and next steps

Responsibility · I'm responsible for designing the module.
Be responsible for + noun or verb-ing tells someone what you are in charge of. For is a preposition, so use designing when an action follows. With a noun: “I'm responsible for the cabin layout.”
Je suis chargée de concevoir le module. Responsible for introduit ce qui relève de votre responsabilité.
Regular task · My job involves checking the dimensions.
Involve + noun or verb-ing describes what a job includes. Use involves with my job and checking for the activity. Try “My job involves reviewing drawings.”
Mon travail consiste notamment à vérifier les cotes. Involves indique une activité qui fait partie de votre travail.
Action needed · I need to confirm the measurements.
Need + to + base verb says what you have to do. Say “I need to confirm the measurements to avoid a mistake.” The first infinitive gives the action, and the second explains its purpose.
Je dois confirmer les mesures pour éviter une erreur. To confirm indique l’action nécessaire, et to avoid indique le but.

Practise · Meaning before memorisation

04 / TECHNICAL STUDIO

Two cabinets. One clear explanation.

Learn to explain a realistic design issue without needing perfect technical vocabulary.

VISUAL REFERENCE · SCHEMATIC, NOT ENGINEERING DRAWING

Illustrative English-learning sketch only. Real aircraft installation must follow approved drawings, specifications and procedures.

EXPLAIN IT IN FOUR STEPS

The fit-and-check conversation

1Identify: “These are two galley storage modules.”
2Connect: “We need to join the two units together.”
3Check: “We need to check the alignment and mounting points.”
4Request: “Could you confirm the dimensions on the latest drawing?”
TECHNICAL PRECISION

Choose the right verb

join / connect two parts = assembler / relier (general)

attach / fasten a part to another = fixer

align two units = aligner

fit together = s'emboîter / s'ajuster correctement

assemble components = assembler plusieurs composants

mounting point = point de fixation

clearance = jeu / espace libre nécessaire

Attention : « cabinet » peut signifier un meuble de rangement ; « galley » désigne l'office de bord. Choisissez les termes correspondant à vos plans réels.

USEFUL STRUCTURE
First, we align the two modules.
Then, we check the mounting points.
Before assembly, we confirm the dimensions.
Finally, we verify the fit against the drawing.
These are communication examples, not aircraft assembly instructions.

What kind of unit is it?

Unit is a general word for one item. Name it precisely the first time, then use the unit or the two units once your colleague knows what you mean. Use cabinet for a piece of storage furniture and module for a section that forms part of a larger design.

A component is one part of a design. An assembly is a set of parts put together. A panel is a flat piece, and a bracket is a support or attachment part.

“These are two galley storage modules. We need to check how the two units fit together.”

Présentez d’abord le nom précis de la pièce, puis utilisez the unit si votre collègue sait de quel élément vous parlez. Choisissez le terme utilisé sur votre plan ou par votre équipe.

See more aircraft-interior examples in the Safran cabin interiors overview.

Practice · Describe the design

ORAL MISSION · DESIGN REVIEW

Explain the join to a colleague in Germany

Choose two galley cabinets, two storage modules, or a pair from a drawing you can discuss, such as a cabinet and a side panel. Find the right names in the vocabulary.

Use four steps: what the items are → what must happen → what needs checking → a polite request. Name each item first. If the pair contains different parts, you can then call them “the two components.” Speak for 45–60 seconds, then try a shorter version without reading.

WHEN YOUR MIND GOES BLANK

Use a bridge phrase. Then continue.

Buy time: “Let me think for a moment.” Laissez-moi réfléchir un instant.

Rephrase: “What I mean is…” Ce que je veux dire, c'est…

Clarify: “Just to make sure I understand…” Pour être sûre de bien comprendre…

Check: “So, if I understand correctly, …” Donc, si j'ai bien compris…

Ask for help: “Could you say that a little more slowly?” Pourriez-vous parler un peu plus lentement ?

3-SECOND RECOVERY
1 · Pause: “Let me think.”
2 · Say the main idea: “The parts don't fit.”
3 · Add the next step: “Could we check the dimensions?”

Short, clear sentences are professional.

06 / REAL MISSIONS

From practice to a real workday.

Three authentic scenarios: an introduction, a telephone clarification, and an email.

MISSION A · FACE-TO-FACE / LILATE-STYLE

A new colleague meets Laura

Colleague: “Hi Laura! It's great to meet you. Tell me a little about what you do and what you enjoy about your job.”

Your structure: greeting → job → furniture/design → why you enjoy it → question back.

MISSION B · PHONE / VIDEO

Ask for a drawing clarification

Colleague: “We're ready to join the two cabinet modules, but I'm not sure about the latest dimensions.”

Your structure: acknowledge → say what to check → ask for the latest drawing → agree next step.

MISSION C · PROFESSIONAL EMAIL

Request the updated technical drawing

Situation: Two galley storage units need to fit together. You must check alignment and mounting points. Write to your colleague in Spain to request an updated drawing.

Subject: Updated drawing – cabinet modules

1. Greeting: Hi [Name],

2. Context: I'm writing about the two cabinet modules.

3. Request: Could you send me the latest drawing, please?

4. Why: We need to check the dimensions before our design review.

5. Close: Thanks for your help. Best regards, Laura
