02 / DESCRIBE THE POSITION

Where is the part — and which way up is it?

Look at the finished unit. Name the part, then use a second part as your reference point. Then say which way it is facing.

Build a position sentence

The tray + is + under the shelf.

Use is for one part and are for more than one. “The two cabinets are next to each other.”

Ask: “Where is the tray?” / “Is the tray under the shelf?”

Correct a detail: “The tray isn’t above the shelf. It’s under it.”

Check the intended position: “Where should the tray go?” / “It should go under the shelf.”

Add the orientation: “The container is on its side. It should be the right way up.”

Où est le bac ? / Est-ce qu’il est sous la tablette ? / Où doit-on placer le bac ? / Le conteneur est couché sur le côté ; il devrait être dans le bon sens.

Up, down and beside

under / underneath · sous / juste en dessous
The tray is under the shelf. It sits lower, directly beneath it.
above / below · au-dessus de / en dessous de
The shelf is above the tray. The tray is below the shelf. These words compare height.
over / across · au-dessus de / d’un côté à l’autre
The worktop goes over both cabinets. It runs across the top of the unit, from one side to the other.
on / on top of · sur / posé sur
The handle is on the door. The worktop rests on top of both cabinets. Both uses of on involve contact.
next to / beside · à côté de
Cabinet B is next to Cabinet A. Add to the right of or to the left of to give the exact side.
directly above / just below · juste au-dessus / juste en dessous
Add directly or just to be precise: “The drawer unit is directly above the trolley.”

Inside, between and towards the back

between A and B · entre A et B
The divider is between Cabinet A and Cabinet B.
inside / outside · à l’intérieur de / à l’extérieur de
The shelf is inside Cabinet A. The handle is on the outside of the right-hand door.
in front of / behind · devant / derrière
With a cabinet door closed, the door is in front of the contents. The contents are behind the door.
against / near · contre / près de
“The panel is against the back wall” means it touches the wall. “The handle is near the right edge” means it is close to the edge.
at the top / at the bottom · en haut / en bas
The worktop is at the top of the unit. The base rail is at the bottom.
in the upper / lower space · dans l’espace du haut / du bas
Upper and lower are adjectives: “the upper compartment”, “the lower shelf”.

⇅ Which way up? · Orientation

the right way up · dans le bon sens
The arrow points up. The top is at the top.
upside down · à l’envers (haut en bas)
The tray is upside down: the open side faces down.
on its side · couché sur le côté
The container is on its side. Stand it up.
upright / vertical · flat / horizontal · debout / vertical · à plat / horizontal
The handle on the door is vertical. On a drawer it is horizontal.
back to front · devant derrière
The drawer is back to front: the handle is at the back.
face up / face down · face visible vers le haut / vers le bas
Put the panel face down to protect the finish.

⌖ Where in the space?

in the middle / in the centre · au milieu / au centre
The container is in the centre compartment. (US: center)
at the front / at the back · à l’avant / à l’arrière
The handle is at the front. The hinge is at the back of the lid.
in the top-left corner · dans le coin en haut à gauche
The label is in the top-left corner of the door.
on the left-hand / right-hand side · du côté gauche / droit
The hinges are on the left-hand side of the door.
along · le long de
The seal runs along the front edge.
opposite · en face de
The galley is opposite the entry door.

✈ Aircraft directions · how to talk about the cabin

forwardtowards the nose (the front of the aircraft) vers l’avant de l’avion (vers le nez)“Move the bracket 10 mm forward.”
afttowards the tail (the back of the aircraft) vers l’arrière de l’avion (vers la queue)“The galley is at the aft end of the cabin.”
LH sideleft-hand side, seen when you face forward côté gauche, vu en regardant vers l’avant“The cabinet is on the LH side.”
RH sideright-hand side, seen when you face forward côté droit, vu en regardant vers l’avant“The galley is on the RH side.”
inboardtowards the centre line of the aircraft côté intérieur, vers l’axe de l’avion“Move the panel 5 mm inboard.”
outboardtowards the sidewall (the outside) côté extérieur, vers la paroi / le fuselage“The cabinet is outboard, against the sidewall.”
⚠ Important: on an aircraft, left and right never change. Imagine you are sitting in the cockpit, facing the nose: that is how LH and RH are defined — not from the view on your drawing!Sur un avion, gauche et droite ne changent jamais : on les définit assis dans le cockpit, face au nez — pas selon la vue du plan.

Also: aisle (allée) · sidewall (paroi latérale) · bulkhead (cloison) · monument (élément fixe : office, toilettes).

up / down · adverbs“Move it up.” No reference after it.
The shelf is up the tray.
above / below · prepositions“It’s above the tray.” Always + a reference point.
upper / lower · adjectives“the upper space”, “the lower shelf”. Before a noun.

03 / GIVE PLACEMENT INSTRUCTIONS

Where do you want it to go?

Start with the action. Add the part, then say where to put it or which way to move it.

Describe where it is

Part + is / are + position

“The tray is inside Cabinet A.”

“The two cabinets are next to each other.”

Le bac est à l’intérieur du meuble A.

Give a short instruction

Base verb + part + destination

“Put the tray under the shelf.”

“Slide the tray into Cabinet A.”

To stop an action, use don’t + base verb. “Don’t move the worktop yet.”

Placez le bac sous la tablette. / Faites coulisser le bac dans le meuble A.

Ask a colleague to move it

Could you + base verb + part + direction?

“Could you move the handle to the right, please?”

“Could you raise the shelf a little?”

Pourriez-vous déplacer la poignée vers la droite ? / remonter un peu la tablette ?

Choose the verb and direction

Put / place / position name the destination. “Position the divider between the cabinets.” Place and position sound more technical than put.

Slide describes a smooth movement. “Slide the tray into the cabinet.” Into means from outside to inside. Use out of for the reverse movement.

Raise / lift mean move up. Raise = move to a higher position (also for adjustments: “raise the shelf by 5 mm”). Lift = take it up with your hands. Lower means move down. ⚠ Raise needs an object; rise does not: “The shelf rises” is rare — say “Raise the shelf.”

Shift is useful for a small change of position. “Shift the divider slightly to the left.” Swap = exchange two positions. “Swap the oven and the coffee maker.”

Turn / rotate / flip change the orientation. “Turn it upside down.” “Rotate it 90° clockwise.” “Flip it over.”

Towards gives a direction. “Move the handle towards the right edge.” Away from means increase the distance from something.

« In / inside » décrit la position. « Into » exprime le mouvement vers l’intérieur. « Towards » (ou « toward », fréquent en anglais américain) = vers. Raise = monter / relever · lift = soulever · lower = abaisser · shift = décaler · swap = intervertir · flip = retourner.

THREE WAYS TO SAY IT
The shelf is inside Cabinet A.
Move the shelf up a little.
Could you move the shelf up a little, please?

04 / TECHNICAL STUDIO

Build the galley.

Drag the modules into the galley. Copy a target drawing, build from a colleague’s spoken instructions, fix a colleague’s draft — or design your own layout and explain it.

Galley modules

oven four · coffee maker cafetière · standard container conteneur · drawer unit bloc tiroirs · trolley (US cart) chariot · waste bin (US trash can) poubelle · Cabinet A / B

Rows, columns & bays

The galley has three rows (top, middle, bottom) and three columns (left, centre, right). A space with a door is a compartment; an open space for a trolley is a bay.

“The oven is in the top-left compartment.” “The trolley goes in the bottom-left bay, under the worktop.”

Useful verbs for modules

install the oven · slide the trolley into the bay · put / place the container · swap two modules · move it one compartment to the left · leave the centre compartment empty.

Speak · Explain YOUR galley to a colleague

Build a galley in the game first. Then imagine your colleague is seeing it for the first time. Everything below — the frame, the models and the check — uses the galley you built.

  1. Start with the view. “Let’s look at the galley from the front.”
  2. Go row by row. “In the top row, the oven is on the left, …” · “Under the worktop, …”
  3. Give two relations. “The oven is directly above the drawer unit.” · “… between … and …”
  4. Say what is empty or turned. “The centre compartment is empty.” · “The container is on its side.”
  5. Finish with a check. “Let’s check that the front edges line up.”
Word bank for this galley

Describe how the parts go together

Assemble a unit means put its parts together. Join Cabinet A to Cabinet B means connect the two cabinets. Attach a handle to a door describes fixing one part to another.

Align the front edges means line them up. Check the fit means check that the parts go together as intended. Clearance is the free space a part needs, for example for a tray to slide out.

Assemble = assembler · join = relier / assembler · attach = fixer · align = aligner · fit = ajustement · clearance = jeu / espace libre.

GIVE YOUR COLLEAGUE AN ORDER TO FOLLOW
First, slide the trolley into the bottom-left bay.
Next, put the waste bin next to the trolley.
Then, install the oven directly above the drawer unit.
After that, make sure the container is the right way up.
Finally, check the front edges and the clearance around the trolley.

05 / CHECK & ADJUST

What needs to move?

Compare the two front views. Find the shelf, divider and handle, then describe what is wrong — and move them yourself.

Name the problem

Too + adjective: “The shelf is too low.” / “The divider is too far to the right.”

Adjective + enough: “The handle isn’t close enough to the right edge.”

Enough + noun: “There isn’t enough clearance for the tray.”

Give the amount or the destination

“Raise the shelf by 5 millimetres.” By gives the amount of change.

“Move the shelf to the upper mark.” To gives the destination.

“Shift the divider slightly to the left.” Use a little or slightly if the amount is approximate.

by 5 mm = de 5 mm · to the upper mark = jusqu’au repère du haut. (US: millimeters)

Check the new position

“Let’s check the gap above the tray.”

“Could you check that both front edges line up?”

“The front faces should be flush” means the faces should be in the same plane. “The shelf is level” means it is horizontal.

gap = espace entre deux éléments · flush = affleurant · level = de niveau.

PROBLEM → ACTION → CHECK “The shelf looks too low. Could you raise it a little? Then let’s check the clearance above the tray.”

La tablette semble trop basse. Pourriez-vous la remonter un peu ? Vérifions ensuite l’espace libre au-dessus du bac.

    06 / GLOBAL TEAM

    Take the drawing into a design review.

    Listen to a colleague, explain the changes aloud, then write a short follow-up message.

    Listen to a colleague’s update

    Listen for which parts to move, in which direction, and what to check afterwards. Replay the message or open the transcript whenever you need it.

    Hi Laura. I’ve compared the draft with the target. Could you shift the divider slightly to the left and move the handle closer to the right edge of the door? Please leave the worktop where it is. Once the shelf has been raised, could you check that the tray still slides out easily? Thanks.

    Speak · Walk a colleague through the drawing

    Your colleague asks “Where do the parts go, and what should we change in this draft?” Use both drawings.

    1. Open with “Let’s start with the front view.”
    2. Describe three positions. Use next to, between and under.
    3. Request the three changes to the shelf, divider and handle.
    4. Finish with a fit or clearance check. Try speaking for 45–60 seconds, then repeat using keywords only.

    Write · Confirm the changes by email

    Write to a colleague after the review. Give the drawing context, request the changes and ask them to confirm once the drawing has been updated.

    Opening: “I’m writing about the latest galley cabinet drawing.”

    Request: “Could you adjust the position of the …?”

    Confirmation: “Please let me know when the revised drawing is ready.”
