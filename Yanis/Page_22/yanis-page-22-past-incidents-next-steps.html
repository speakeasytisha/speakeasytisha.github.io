Report What Happened & Explain the Next Step ✈️

This lesson introduces the past simple in a structured way so you can explain what happened during an airline incident. You will also connect the past event, the current situation, and the next step.

Goal: explain an incident clearly, accurately and professionally.

1) The three-time method

Use this simple structure to organise a professional answer.

⏮️

Past event

What happened?

The passenger missed the connection.
The bag did not arrive.
⏺️

Situation now

What is true now?

The passenger is waiting at the desk.
The bag is missing.
⏭️

Next step

What will happen next?

I will check the next flight.
The baggage team will contact the passenger.
Important: The new tense in this lesson is the past simple. The phrases with will are used as practical “next step” language. A complete future-tense lesson will come separately.

2) Grammar workshop — Past simple

Learn one rule at a time. Each block includes examples, mistakes, and notes.

A. Past of “be” — was / were

I / he / she / it was · you / we / they were
The flight was delayed yesterday.
The passengers were worried.
I was not at the gate.
Were the passengers on board?
Negative: was not / wasn’t · were not / weren’t
Question: Was / Were + subject ...?
❌ The passengers was worried.
✅ The passengers were worried.

B. Regular verbs — verb + -ed

Subject + regular verb + -ed
The passenger asked for help.
The crew checked the documents.
The flight arrived late.
We informed the passengers.
Spelling notes:
  • Most verbs: check → checked
  • Verb ending in -e: arrive → arrived
  • Consonant + y: carry → carried
  • Short verb: stop → stopped

C. Irregular verbs

Irregular verbs do not use a simple -ed ending. Learn the past form.

gowentThe passenger went to the wrong gate.

loselostThe passenger lost the boarding pass.

fallfellA passenger fell in the aisle.

D. Negative and questions with did

Negative: subject + did not / didn’t + base verb
Question: Did + subject + base verb ...?
The bag didn’t arrive.
Did the passenger miss the flight?
Yes, he did. / No, he didn’t.
Important exception: after did / didn’t, use the base verb, not the past form.
❌ Did the passenger missed the flight?
✅ Did the passenger miss the flight?
❌ The bag didn’t arrived.
✅ The bag didn’t arrive.

E. Past time markers

yesterday — The flight arrived late yesterday.
last night / last week — The passenger called last night.
two hours ago — The bag arrived two hours ago.
this morning — The incident happened this morning.
at 6:15 p.m. — Boarding started at 6:15 p.m.
These time expressions help the listener understand that the action is finished.

6) Writing ladder — incident report

Models

LevelModel
