TRAINER / EXAMINER VIEW

Yanis — PNC Live Mock Guide

Use this on a second screen.

The candidate page should remain in Real exam mode. Do not show model answers, hints or this script during the timed run.

00

Examiner posture

Supportive, natural, adaptive — but do not teach during the run.

Observe the mission

Can you understand the candidate without rescuing every sentence? Does the candidate obtain essential information and move the professional situation forward?

Use follow-ups

Start with the base prompt. If the answer is very short, ask one clarification. If it is stable, add a realistic complication. Do not turn this into a grammar interview.

Rate after the part

Prioritise communicative success: interaction, comprehension, reformulation, transmission and appropriate professional response.

01

Part 1 — Welcome & interaction · ~15 min

Goal: enter a professional exchange, identify needs, ask questions and provide an appropriate response.

1A · Introduction

Say: “Good afternoon. Before we begin, could you tell me about yourself and the role you would like to do?”

Choose 2–3:
  • Why would you like to work as cabin crew?
  • What qualities are important for a cabin crew member?
  • Tell me about a time you had to help a customer or guest.
  • What can be difficult when working with international passengers?
  • What would you do if you did not understand a passenger?
B1 evidence: connected 45–60 second answer; understandable past/present/future; can develop an answer after a follow-up; does not collapse when searching for a word.

1B · Seat conflict role-play

You are the passenger: “Excuse me, I think someone is sitting in my seat. My boarding pass says 14A, but the passenger says this is his seat.”

Escalate naturally:
  • “I don’t understand. We both have 14A. Who has to move?”
  • “I paid extra for a window seat. I don’t want another seat.”
  • “Can you guarantee that I’ll keep a window seat?”
B1 evidence: asks to see both boarding passes; checks details; empathises; does not promise what cannot be confirmed; explains a next step.

1C · Tight connection

Say: “My next flight leaves in thirty-five minutes. I’m in row 29 and I’m really worried I’ll miss it. Can I get off first?”

  • “Why can’t you just let me go first?”
  • “Who will help me when we land?”
B1 evidence: acknowledges concern; distinguishes what is possible from what is not guaranteed; asks for useful connection detail; offers to relay information.
02

Part 2 — Collect & transcribe · ~15 min

Goal: extract operational information and reformulate it in a different format.

2A · Audio handover

Ask the candidate to listen to the page audio without transcript. After the first listen, say: “Please tell me the key information. Imagine I am another crew member and I need the handover.”

  • “Which passenger issue requires an alternative solution?”
  • “Where will the wheelchair passenger board?”
  • “What caused the delay?”
B1 evidence: captures flight/destination, delay/cause, special meal issue, family seating request and wheelchair boarding point; reformulates rather than reciting.

2B · Written operations document

Give 60–90 seconds to read. Then say: “What are the three most important things I need to know before boarding is complete?” After the oral response, ask for the short written internal note.

  • “Why did you choose those three?”
  • “Which information can wait?”
B1 evidence: prioritises action, not every detail; can explain seat change, tight connection and special assistance clearly; written note is concise and usable.
03

Part 3 — Collect & transmit · ~15 min

Goal: relay third-party instructions and adapt the message to a passenger or colleague.

3A · Captain message → passenger announcement

Play the audio. Say: “Please explain this information to the passengers in clear English.”

  • “Can you make that simpler for a passenger who does not speak English very well?”
  • “What information should you avoid adding?”
B1 evidence: accurate core message; passenger-friendly wording; logical sequence; calm delivery; no invented facts.

3B · Angry connection passenger

Role-play: “This delay is ridiculous. My connection leaves in forty minutes and nobody is telling me anything. I need you to guarantee that someone will be waiting for me.”

  • “That’s not good enough. I need a guarantee.”
  • “What exactly are you going to do now?”
  • “Why should I trust that?”
B1 evidence: empathises without over-apologising; asks connection number; sets a boundary; offers a concrete action; stays in the interaction under pressure.

3C · Crew handover

Say: “Now tell me what happened as if I were the senior crew member.”

B1 evidence: clear sequence — concern → verified info → what was communicated → action requested.
04

Part 4 — Collect & exploit · ~15 min

Goal: connect several sources, identify priorities, justify choices and manage uncertainty.

4A · Operations dashboard

Give 90 seconds to read. Play the additional gate message. Then ask: “What are your three priorities and why?”

  • “Which passenger should be reported first?”
  • “What is confirmed, and what is not confirmed?”
  • “What would you tell the family with children?”
B1 evidence: selects relevant data; distinguishes confirmed/unconfirmed information; gives reasons; proposes safe, realistic next actions.

4B · Ms. Novak role-play

Say: “Am I going to miss my connection? Can you rebook me now?”

  • “So what can you actually do for me?”
  • “When will you know more?”
B1 evidence: answers from the document; does not promise rebooking; gives the 18:50 update and reporting action; maintains a calm professional tone.

4C · Written handover

Ask for the 80–110 word written handover. Give no sentence-level correction during the timed task.

B1 evidence: readable structure, essential facts, priority passengers, confirmed/unconfirmed distinction and next update/action.
✓

Internal B1 readiness scale

Use the candidate page report. This is pedagogical evidence only, not official LILATE scoring.

1 · Major support

Communication repeatedly stops; essential information is lost.

2 · Supported

Mission partly succeeds with prompting, repetition or reformulation from the trainer.

3 · Independent / B1-style

Mission succeeds independently despite noticeable errors; meaning and action remain clear.

4 · Strong / B1+

Flexible, clear, well-organised performance; handles follow-ups and nuance confidently.

Debrief order

Start with what he accomplished professionally. Then choose only 1–2 high-impact language priorities. Finish by replaying one scenario so the final experience is a successful one.
