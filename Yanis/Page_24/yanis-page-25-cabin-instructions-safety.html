2

Complete grammar rule — instructions, obligation and prohibition

Repère rapide : impératif = consigne directe · must / have to / need to = obligation ou nécessité · mustn’t = interdit · don’t have to = pas obligatoire.
RULE 1

The imperative — direct instructions

Positive
Base verb + complement

Fasten your seat belt.

Place your bag under the seat.

Negative
Do not / Don’t + base verb

Do not block the aisle.

Don’t open the overhead locker.

Professional tone
Please + imperative / Could you + base verb…?

Please remain seated.

Could you place your bag under the seat?

Important: after could you, use the base verb: “Could you fasten…” — not “Could you fastens…” and not “Could you to fasten…”.

L’impératif utilise le verbe à la base sans sujet. Pour une consigne plus professionnelle, ajoutez please ou utilisez Could you + verbe à la base…?.

RULE 2

Must / have to / need to — what is required?

FormMain meaningStructureCabin example
muststrong rule / speaker presents it as essentialmust + base verbYou must keep your seat belt fastened.
have toexternal rule, regulation or practical obligationhave / has to + base verbYou have to switch your phone to flight mode.
need tonecessary action to achieve a safe resultneed / needs to + base verbYou need to move your bag away from the exit.
For today: in many real cabin situations, must, have to and need to can all communicate necessity. The important goal is to build the form correctly and choose a tone that is clear and professional.

Must exprime une obligation forte ; have to correspond souvent à une règle extérieure ; need to insiste sur une action nécessaire.

RULE 3

Mustn’t ≠ don’t have to — the essential contrast

⛔

mustn’t + base verb

Meaning: prohibited / not allowed.

You mustn’t smoke on board.

Il est interdit de fumer.

✓

don’t / doesn’t have to + base verb

Meaning: not necessary / optional.

You don’t have to remove your shoes.

Vous n’êtes pas obligé d’enlever vos chaussures.

Never confuse them: “You mustn’t do it” = do not do it. “You don’t have to do it” = you can do it, but it is not necessary.
RULE 4

Sequence a procedure — make every step easy to follow

First, identify the problem. Then, give the first instruction. Next, check the passenger understands. After that, confirm the safe position. Finally, thank the passenger.

Comma rule: when the sequencing word comes first, use a comma: First, fasten your seat belt.

Ces connecteurs permettent d’expliquer une procédure dans un ordre logique et facile à suivre.

OPTIONAL B1 BRIDGE

Reason language — explain why the instruction matters

Please keep the aisle clear because passengers need access to the exit.

For safety reasons, you need to remain seated.

To keep the exit clear, please move your bag.

Ajoutez une raison courte uniquement quand cela aide le passager à comprendre la consigne.

6

Reading under pressure — short cabin procedure

Light turbulence procedure

During light turbulence, passengers should remain seated when possible and keep their seat belts fastened. Cabin bags must be stored safely. The aisle and exits must remain clear. If a passenger needs assistance, assess the situation and inform the cabin supervisor when necessary.

Pendant de légères turbulences, les passagers doivent rester assis si possible et garder leur ceinture attachée. Les bagages cabine doivent être rangés en sécurité. L’allée et les issues doivent rester dégagées. Si un passager a besoin d’aide, évaluez la situation et informez le chef de cabine si nécessaire.

Writing transfer — passenger instruction + crew procedure

Your 4-step writing method

  1. Situation: state the problem in one sentence.
  2. Instruction: give the required action.
  3. Reason: add a short safety reason if useful.
  4. Check: confirm the next step or thank the passenger.
