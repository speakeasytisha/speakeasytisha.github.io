01 · THE GOOD NEWS · 7 MIN

Tell your story — then make it more precise

First speak naturally for 45–60 seconds. What changed this week? How do you feel about leaving? What does it mean for your next chapter?

Racontez librement, sans chercher la perfection. Votre formateur relève les temps et expressions à améliorer.

Before: first attempt

Three prompts: What was offered? What have you agreed on? Why does this matter now?

B1: My company reorganised my department and offered me another role, but I decided not to take it. We have now reached an agreement about my departure. I'm excited about Australia, although I feel a little nostalgic.
B2: Following a reorganisation, the position I had held for six years was removed. Rather than accept a different role, I negotiated a mutually agreed departure. I'm relieved by the outcome, but leaving a company where I had a promising future is bittersweet.

Confirmed, planned, or still to check?

These are your account of events, not a legal determination or a guarantee of future payment.

Say exactly what you mean

Choose a category. Every entry has a French translation, an English definition, a model sentence and manual listening.

Use the new expressions

🇦🇺 Natural Australian English: “negotiated a separation agreement” or “reached a mutual agreement to end my employment” explains the French concept better than literally saying “conventional rupture.” For an informal account, “I negotiated an exit package” is natural. Redundancy in Australian employment law is not automatically the same as a French rupture conventionnelle; avoid saying you were made redundant unless that is factually and legally accurate.
03 · GRAMMAR 1 · PAST SIMPLE · 9 MIN

What happened: finished events

Meaning and timeline

Past simple = an action finished at a finished past time: Last week, I negotiated the terms. Six years ago, I joined the company.

En français « j'ai négocié » ressemble au present perfect, mais si le moment est terminé, l'anglais utilise le past simple.

Affirmative

I negotiated. I had an idea. They agreed.

Negative

I didn't accept another role.

Question

Did they accept your proposal?

Exception: be

I was proud. We were surprised. Were you relieved?

Spelling: agree → agreed; plan → planned (double final consonant); carry → carried; stop → stopped. Irregular: leave → left, get → got, build → built, make → made. After did/didn't, use the base verb, never the past form.

Signals: last week, in 2020, six years ago, yesterday, when I worked there.

04 · GRAMMAR 2 · PRESENT PERFECT · 10 MIN

Why the past matters now

Present perfect = have / has + past participle. Use it for a result now, a life experience without a finished time, or a situation continuing to the present.

Affirmative

We have reached an agreement.

Negative

I haven't started my new adventure yet.

Question

Have you decided when to leave?

Key signals

already, just, yet, ever, never, so far, since, for.

Past simple contrast: “Last week, they accepted my proposal” (when?) → “They've accepted my proposal” (result now). Do not use present perfect with a finished time: ✗ They've agreed yesterday. ✓ They agreed yesterday.

Since / for: since 2020 (start); for six years (duration). If the job is finished, say “I worked there for six years.” If it is still ongoing, “I have worked there for six years.” Because the negotiated departure may still be pending, choose the tense that reflects the actual contract status.

Participles: agree–agreed–agreed; get–got–got (UK/AU); make–made–made; take–took–taken; build–built–built.

05 · YOUR PROUDEST CONTRIBUTION · 10 MIN

The idea that saved money

You spotted an expensive weakness in the invoicing workflow: inconsistent date formats could cause payments or invoices to be processed incorrectly. You proposed an application or tracking solution, and your contribution was recognised with an award.

Accuracy matters: You originated the idea; you did not personally code the application. The precise technical mechanism and total verified savings still need confirmation, so describe the impact as “helped prevent duplicate payments” or “helped the company avoid significant losses,” and use “millions of euros” only if you can substantiate it.

Grammar 3: describing impact without overstating your role

I had the idea for…

Ownership of the concept (past simple)

I proposed / helped introduce…

Your real involvement; not “I coded”

It helped prevent…

help + base verb; practical effect

My contribution was recognised…

Passive: was + past participle

Passive formation: present: is recognised; past: was recognised; negative: wasn't recognised; question: Was your idea recognised? Use the passive when the result is more important than the person taking the action.

Build your real success story

Models (illustrative)

B1: I worked as a process analyst for six years. I noticed that different date formats were causing problems in our invoicing process. I suggested an application to track the information and help prevent duplicate payments. I didn't develop the application myself, but I came up with the idea. I received an award for my contribution.
B2: While reviewing billing processes, I identified a potential source of costly duplicate payments linked to inconsistent date formats. I conceived a tracking solution that was subsequently implemented with technical support. The initiative helped reduce avoidable losses, and the company recognised my contribution with an award. I'm particularly proud of having spotted a problem and proposed a practical solution.
06 · YOUR NEXT CHAPTER · 9 MIN

You're not walking away — you're moving towards something

You and your partner are choosing experience, freedom and a different kind of work rather than continuing directly along conventional corporate career paths.

Grammar 4: four ways to talk about the future — one at a time

Going to + base verb

Intention: We're going to explore Australia.

Present continuous

Arranged plan: We're leaving next year (only if arranged).

Will + base verb

Prediction/decision: I think we'll learn a lot.

May / might + base verb

Possibility: We might work on a farm.

Negative and questions: aren't going to / Are you going to…?; aren't leaving / Are you leaving…?; won't / Will you…?; might not / Might you…? No to-infinitive after modals: ✗ might to work → ✓ might work. Not every intention is a booked arrangement.

going to = intention; présent continu = arrangement concret; will = prédiction/décision; might = possibilité, sans garantie.

Working Holiday visa — subclass 417: based on your joint plan described in Marine's lesson, you're considering agriculture or entry-level mining-support work. Specific jobs, regions, paid work periods and proof must meet official conditions before anyone assumes they count towards a second-year visa. For French passport holders, the current age band is 18–35 at application. Visa approval and job eligibility are not automatic. Official specified-work rules ↗

Practice the real reason

Try: “We could both continue our careers, but we don't want to keep postponing an experience that may become harder later. This is a deliberate choice, not an escape from work.”

07 · VTEST-INSPIRED SKILLS · 7 MIN + HOMEWORK

Listen, think, respond

This is custom VTest-inspired preparation, not an official VTest sample or a prediction of the exam questions.

Listening: the turning point

Listen without reading. Answer the questions, then reveal the transcript.

Interviewer: You spent six years at the same company. Why are you leaving?
Candidate: My role was affected by a reorganisation. I was offered alternatives, but they weren't the right fit for my next chapter. We have now reached a mutually agreed arrangement. I'm proud of my achievements, and I'm looking forward to gaining practical experience in Australia.
Interviewer: What kind of work are you interested in?
Candidate: I'm open to hands-on work, such as agriculture or suitable entry-level support roles. My priority is to learn, contribute and experience a different way of life.

Speaking: two audiences

Friend: Tell the full story, including the agreement and your feelings.
Australian employer: Keep it concise and positive. Mention your reliability and flexibility. You do not need to disclose your payout or private negotiations.
B1 (friend): We've reached an agreement about my departure. The company accepted the compensation amount I proposed, and my unused annual leave will also be paid out. I'm excited, but I'll miss some people and opportunities.
B2 (employer): After six valuable years in process analysis, I've chosen to pursue a Working Holiday in Australia. I'm keen to try practical work, learn quickly and contribute to a new team.

60–90 second mission: background → turning point → achievement → feelings → future. Record attempt 1 and attempt 2, then compare.
