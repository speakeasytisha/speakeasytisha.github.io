STAGE 5 · GRAMMAR · TRAINING HABITS

Present simple: what you normally do

When to use it

Habits, routines, facts and regular training. Signals: usually, always, often, sometimes, rarely, never, every week, on Mondays, twice a week.

🇫🇷 Habitudes, routines, vérités générales. Repérez les mots-signaux : usually, every week, twice a week…

Affirmative

I run. You train. He/She runs. We lift weights.

Negative

I don’t skip warm-ups. He doesn’t train every day.

Question

Do you run every morning? Does your partner train with you?

Be

I am consistent. They are motivated. Is it difficult?

He/she/it spelling: + -s (runs) · -es after -sh/-ch/-x/-ss/-o (watches, goes) · consonant + y → -ies (studies) · vowel + y → -s (plays) · have → has. After does/doesn’t, the verb has no -s.

Word order: frequency adverbs go before the main verb (I usually run) but after be (I am usually tired).

Word order check

STAGE 6 · GRAMMAR LAB A · 12 MIN

Past simple: the race is finished

When to use it

Completed actions at a finished time: last weekend, on Saturday, in Geneva, two days ago, yesterday. Tell events in order.

🇫🇷 Action terminée, moment terminé : last weekend, on Saturday, two days ago…

Affirmative

I ran eight kilometres. We stayed nearby.

Negative

I didn’t stop. We didn’t drive home immediately.

Question

Did you finish? Where did you stay?

Be: special case

I was exhausted. We were excited. Were you nervous?

Remember: after did / didn’t → base verb (Did you finished finish?). Regular: train → trained · plan → planned · carry → carried. Irregular: run → ran · go → went · feel → felt · take → took · drive → drove.

Produce it yourself

🔊 Pronunciation lab: the three sounds of -ed

/t/

after p, k, s, sh, ch, f
booked, pushed, jumped

/d/

after vowels and voiced sounds
stayed, pulled, planned

/ɪd/

only after t or d — an extra syllable
competed, started, needed

🇫🇷 Le « -ed » ne se prononce « id » qu’après t ou d. Sinon, pas de syllabe en plus : « booked » = 1 syllabe.

STAGE 7 · GRAMMAR LAB B · 12 MIN

Present perfect: the experience still matters

When to use it

have/has + past participle for life experience with no finished time, a result that matters now, or a period that continues until now.

🇫🇷 have/has + participe passé : expérience sans date précise, résultat visible maintenant, ou période qui continue jusqu’à aujourd’hui.

Affirmative

I have competed in HYROX. I’ve improved my endurance.

Negative

I haven’t tried a Pro race yet.

Question

Have you ever raced abroad? Has your training changed?

Signals

ever · never · already · yet · just · recently · so far · since · for

since + starting point (since January) · for + duration (for six months) · been = visited and came back · gone = went and is still there.

Participles: run–ran–run · do–did–done · go–went–gone · see–saw–seen · take–took–taken · learn–learnt/learned–learnt/learned.

Watch out: name a finished time → past simple. ✗ I have competed last weekend. ✓ I competed last weekend. · have been training (present perfect continuous) stresses the ongoing activity: I’ve been training for months.
