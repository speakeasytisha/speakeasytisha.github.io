02 · The writing method

Plan → answer every bullet → develop → proofread.

STEP 1Read

Identify the situation and who you are writing for.

STEP 2Bullets

Turn each bullet into one idea.

STEP 3Plan

Use the 1-minute prep: keywords, not sentences.

STEP 4Develop

Add a reason, an example and a result to each idea.

STEP 5Check

Word count, verbs, spelling, punctuation.

The 1-minute plan

0–10 SEC

Read the prompt and the 3 bullets.

10–25 SEC

Choose your position or story.

25–45 SEC

One keyword idea per bullet.

45–55 SEC

Add one example or result.

55–60 SEC

Choose your opening sentence.

Build every paragraph with this formula

Point→because…→For example,…→As a result,…→Next step / recommendation

Example: “The tool is useful because everyone sees the same deadlines. For example, we can check who is responsible for each task. As a result, there are fewer repeated questions.” Une idée, une explication, un exemple concret, la conséquence.

Different wording, same job. These are training shapes, not official categories.

Change / improvement

People / teamwork

“Describe a time you worked with someone very different from you.”

03 · Grammar essentials

Eight structures you can control.

Affirmative · negative · question
StructureForm+ / − / ?Watch out
1 · Word orderSubject + verb + objectL’ordre sujet–verbe est essentiel.We sent the report yesterday. / We did not send it. / Did we send it?No do with be: Is she ready? Frequency adverbs go before the main verb but after be.
2 · Past simpleSubject + past verb (-ed / irregular)Action terminée, date précise.We completed the audit. / We did not complete it. / Did we complete it?After did/didn’t → base verb. Irregular: went, sent, wrote, made.
3 · Present perfecthave/has + past participleRésultat ou bilan lié au présent.The team has finished. / It has not finished yet. / Has it finished?Markers: already, yet, just, ever, for, since. Never with yesterday or a finished date.
4 · Passive voicebe (right tense) + past participle (+ by…)On met l’action au premier plan.The meeting was moved. / It was not moved. / Was it moved?be changes, the participle does not. Add by only if the person matters. No passive with happen/arrive.
5 · QuestionsDo/Does/Did + subject + base verb?L’auxiliaire avant le sujet.Why did the team postpone the meeting?No extra do with be/have/modals. Subject questions: Who approved the plan?
6 · Modalsshould/could/must/might + base verbshould = conseil · could = possibilité · must = obligationWe should inform the team. / We should not delay. / Should we inform them?No to, no -s: she should send. Must not = prohibition.
7 · Reason, contrast, resultbecause + clause · because of + noun · although + clause · However, + sentenceparce que · à cause de · bien que · cependantWe delayed the launch because testing was incomplete.Never although … but. However/therefore need a full stop or semicolon before them.
8 · Paragraph flowPoint → reason → example → consequence → actionTraining helps because… For example,… As a result,…An example alone is not an argument. Such as + noun phrase.

The passive in every tense

TenseFormExample
Present simpleam/is/are + participleThe reports are checked every Friday.
Past simplewas/were + participleThe meeting was moved to Friday.
Present perfecthas/have been + participleThe issue has been resolved.
Futurewill be + participleThe documents will be sent tomorrow.
Modalshould/could be + participleThe form should be completed today.

In an essay: “New employees are given a welcome guide. Their questions are answered during the first week.” Use the passive when the result matters more than the person, not in every sentence.

04 · Fix these before you click NEXT

The twelve most common errors.

She should to email the client.She should email the client.Modal + base verb, no to.
She should emails the client.She should email the client.No -s after a modal.
Did they sent the email?Did they send the email?After did, base verb.
We have sent it yesterday.We sent it yesterday.Finished time → past simple.
The meeting was move to Friday.The meeting was moved to Friday.Passive needs the past participle.
The report was wrote last week.The report was written last week.Irregular participles: written, sent, made, taken.
The problem was happened.The problem happened.happen / arrive have no passive.
Why they did postpone it?Why did they postpone it?Auxiliary before the subject.
Although it was late, but we continued.Although it was late, we continued.One connector for one contrast.
Because of it was late, we stopped.Because it was late, we stopped.because + clause; because of + noun (because of the delay).
Despite it was late, we finished.Despite the delay, we finished.Despite + noun or -ing.
I suggest to introduce a checklist.I suggest introducing a checklist.suggest + -ing, or suggest that + subject + verb.
Comma splice: The tool is useful, however some people need training. → The tool is useful. However, some people need training. Pas deux phrases complètes reliées par une simple virgule.

05 · Writing language bank

Phrases that connect your ideas.

EN · FR · example
Opinion
  • In my view à mon avisIn my view, the new system would improve coordination.
  • I believe je pense / je croisI believe practical training is essential.
  • From my perspective de mon point de vueFrom my perspective, flexibility can improve motivation.
Reason
  • because / since / as parce que / puisque / commeWe changed the schedule because the supplier was late.
  • because of + noun à cause deThe launch was delayed because of the cost.
Contrast
  • however cependantHowever, the change may create difficulties at first.
  • although bien queAlthough the system is useful, some employees may need support.
  • on the other hand d’un autre côtéOn the other hand, remote work can make communication harder.
Example
  • for example / for instance par exempleFor example, a shared dashboard can reduce duplicated work.
  • such as + noun tel queResources such as checklists can reduce confusion.
  • in addition de plusIn addition, managers should provide clear guidance.
Result
  • as a result par conséquentAs a result, employees may adapt more quickly.
  • therefore donc / par conséquentThe tool saves time. Therefore, it should be used by every team.
  • this would… cela permettrait…This would reduce confusion in the first week.
Recommendation
  • I would recommend + -ing je recommanderaisI would recommend running a short trial period.
  • should / could devrait / pourraitManagers should explain the purpose of the change.
  • I would suggest + -ing je suggéreraisI would suggest piloting the timetable for one month.

Use connectors, but do not overuse them: one per sentence is plenty. No text-message forms, no ALL CAPS.

06 · How answers grow from A2 to C1

Same task. What changes is the development.

Task: A company is changing its meeting schedule. Explain one benefit and one challenge. Read the four answers and notice what has been added, not how many difficult words appear.
A2

Make the message understandable. Answer the bullets with clear, correct short sentences.

The company has new meeting times. This is good because we can plan our day. But some people may be busy. I think the manager should tell everyone early.
ANTOINE · NOWB1

Explain and illustrate. Add a reason, a workplace example and an explicit result.

Our company is introducing a new meeting schedule. I think this could help employees organise their work because they will know when to prepare. However, some colleagues may find the new times difficult. For example, parents may need more flexibility. I recommend asking teams for feedback before confirming the schedule.
ANTOINE · TARGETB2

Develop and evaluate. Compare options, explain consequences, propose a realistic action.

Changing the meeting schedule could improve efficiency by reducing interruptions during busy periods. For instance, holding shorter meetings in the morning may leave the afternoon free for focused work. Nevertheless, the company should consider colleagues in different time zones and those with caring responsibilities. I would suggest piloting the timetable for one month and reviewing attendance and feedback before making it permanent.
C1

Qualify and synthesise. Add balance, a feasible implementation and a measured conclusion.

Although a revised meeting schedule may initially disrupt established routines, a well-designed timetable could significantly improve coordination without sacrificing individual flexibility. … A four-week pilot, supported by clear participation guidelines and anonymous feedback, would allow management to assess the impact before adopting a permanent policy.
B1 → B2 move 1

Name the mechanism

“could help employees” → “could improve efficiency by reducing interruptions”.

B1 → B2 move 2

Qualify the difficulty

“some colleagues may find it difficult” → “Nevertheless, the company should consider colleagues in different time zones”.

B1 → B2 move 3

Make the action testable

“ask for feedback” → “pilot it for one month and review attendance before making it permanent”.

More sophisticated language only helps when it is accurate, relevant and clearly organised.

07 · Worked example

One prompt, one plan, two answers.

Practice exam prompt
WRITING · ESSAY · 20:00

Your team has recently started using a new digital project-management tool.

Write about:

  • one benefit of the new tool
  • one difficulty employees may experience
  • what the company should do to help people use it successfully
01 · Benefit

Clearer teamwork

Everyone sees the same deadlines → fewer repeated questions.

02 · Difficulty

Learning a new system

Some colleagues forget to update their tasks.

03 · Support

Training + short guide

Demonstrations + a named contact for the first month.

This is what your 1-minute plan should look like: three keywords, one example each.

Shorter answerA2–B1

I think using a project-management tool can be useful for a team. First, it gives everyone the same information about tasks and deadlines. For example, employees can see what is finished and what still needs to be done. However, some people may find the new system difficult at first, especially if they are not comfortable with technology. I think the company should organize a short practical training session and give employees a simple guide. A colleague could also be available to answer questions during the first weeks. This would help people feel more confident and use the tool correctly.

Covers all three bullets with an example and a recommendation. Ideas are listed rather than analysed.

Developed answerB2–C1

Introducing a digital project-management tool can improve visibility and coordination because team members can follow responsibilities, deadlines and progress in one place. The main difficulty is likely to be adoption: employees who are comfortable with existing routines may see the new platform as additional work rather than a useful resource. I would therefore recommend a short hands-on training session using realistic projects, followed by simple written guidance and accessible support during the first few weeks. Managers should also explain why the tool is being introduced and agree on clear rules for updating tasks. This combination of practical training and consistent expectations would make the transition smoother and reduce duplicated work.

Not simply longer: precise vocabulary (visibility, adoption, transition), the cause of the difficulty is explained, and the recommendation has a clear result.

Sentence starters for this prompt
  • B1: “Because the tasks are visible to everyone, we can plan our work better. For example, we can check who is responsible for each deadline.”
  • B2: “Although the platform improves transparency, the transition may temporarily slow down employees who are unfamiliar with it.”
  • C1: “A phased rollout, supported by hands-on demonstrations and follow-up feedback, would minimise disruption.”
One idea, upgraded (onboarding)
  • New workers have problems. Managers help them.
  • B1: Starting a new job can be stressful because employees may not understand the company’s procedures. For example, a welcome guide could explain how to request IT support.
  • B2: New employees often receive too much information without knowing which tasks to prioritize. Essential IT access could be arranged before they arrive, allowing them to contribute from day one.

09 · Quick grammar check

Five questions. Answer, then reveal.

1. The meeting ___ moved to Friday.

was · were · did
SHOW ANSWER

was: meeting is singular; past passive = was + moved.

2. The report should ___ completed today.

be · is · to be
SHOW ANSWER

be: after should → be + past participle.

3. ___ the delay, we finished on time.

Despite · Although · However
SHOW ANSWER

Despite + noun; although + clause.

4. She ___ explain the procedure.

should · shoulds · should to
SHOW ANSWER

should: modal + base verb.

5. The problem ___ resolved yesterday.

was · has · is being
SHOW ANSWER

was: yesterday = finished time → past simple passive.

10 · Before you click NEXT

Proofreading checklist and practice rubric.

Trainer rubric · /25
  • I answered all three bullets (benefit, difficulty AND recommendation).
  • I wrote at least 100 words.
  • Each point has a reason (because…) and an example (for example…).
  • My ideas follow an order: opening → three points → closing action.
  • Verbs: tense, third-person -s, base verb after did/should.
  • Passive: be + past participle (written, sent, made).
  • Capital letters, full stops, a comma after For example / However.
  • No comma splices, no although … but.
/5Task coverage

Every bullet answered; who, what and why.

/5Organisation

Clear opening, paragraphs, connectors, final action.

/5Grammar

Tense, agreement, questions; passive when useful.

/5Vocabulary

Precise, relevant, varied but natural.

/5Accuracy

Spelling, punctuation, readable sentences.

This 25-point rubric is a training tool for tracking progress. It is not the official VTest scoring formula.

Picture description and professional email.

Not in the Business English exam
General English · picture task
  • Overview: The picture shows…
  • Actions: present continuous: three colleagues are sitting around a table
  • Position: in the foreground / behind / next to
  • Careful inference: they seem to be discussing… / they may be…

Separate what you can see from what you think.

Professional email · 6 blocks
  • Greeting: Hello Sam, / Dear Ms Smith,
  • Purpose: I’m writing to…
  • Context: As discussed / Regarding…
  • Request or update: Could you… / Please note…
  • Deadline / next step: I will update you by 3 p.m.
  • Closing: Kind regards, Antoine
