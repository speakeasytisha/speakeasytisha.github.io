02 · CONVERSATION TOOLKIT

Sound natural when the conversation changes direction

Use these phrases to buy time, clarify, react and keep the conversation moving.

Fast transfer

Your interviewer says: “That’s interesting. Can you give me a concrete example?”

That's interesting. Can you give me a concrete example?

Strong B1

Sure. In my previous role, I often had to improve a process that involved several teams. One example was when we needed to make the workflow simpler and reduce unnecessary steps. I spoke with the people involved, identified the main problem and proposed a clearer process.

B2 / LILATE target

Absolutely. A good example would be a cross-functional process I had to streamline. I first mapped the existing workflow, then spoke with the teams using it to understand the friction points. Based on that feedback, I proposed a simpler process and helped align the different stakeholders around the change.

04 · YOUR PROFESSIONAL PITCH

From “what you did” to “what you can bring”

A strong Australian-style introduction is concise, practical and conversational.

Your pitch will appear here.

Strong B1

I’ve spent the last six years working as a process analyst. I’m used to understanding how people work, finding problems and making processes clearer. I’m now looking for a new challenge where I can use those skills in an international environment. Australia is attractive to me because I want both a professional change and a new life experience.

B2 / LILATE target

I’ve spent the last six years as a process analyst, working on ways to make workflows more efficient and easier for teams to use. I particularly enjoy understanding how a process works in practice, identifying friction points and bringing people together around a workable solution. I’m now looking for a role where I can apply that experience in a more international setting, and Australia feels like the right place for both a professional step forward and a broader personal experience.

05 · CAREER BREAK

Explain your current situation positively and professionally

You do not need to over-explain. Keep the answer factual, forward-looking and comfortable.

Past→Transition→Action now→Next goal

Question: “What have you been doing since leaving your previous role?”

Strong B1

After six years in the same company, my position came to an end because of a reorganisation. I decided to use the transition as an opportunity to think carefully about my next step. I’ve been staying active, improving my English and preparing for a possible move to Australia. I’m now ready to return to work in a role where I can use my experience and continue developing.

B2 / LILATE target

My previous role ended as part of a wider reorganisation, so I chose to use the transition productively rather than rush into the first available position. I’ve been focusing on my English, keeping a structured routine and exploring opportunities in Australia. It has also given me time to clarify what I want from my next role: a position where I can use my process-analysis experience, work with different stakeholders and contribute in an international environment.

Aide FR : Évitez les formulations négatives comme “I have no job” ou “I’m doing nothing”. Préférez transition, reorganisation, exploring opportunities, preparing for my next role.

10 · AUSTRALIAN WORKPLACE ENGLISH

Understand the tone without forcing the slang

“No worries”

Often means “that’s fine / no problem”. You can understand it without needing to use it constantly.

“Keen”

Common for interested or enthusiastic: “I’d be keen to learn more about the role.”

First names

Professional communication is often relatively direct and informal, while still being respectful.

Concise examples

Concrete examples usually sound stronger than long theoretical explanations.

Aide FR : Le but n’est pas d’imiter l’argot australien. Cherchez surtout un ton direct, chaleureux et naturel.
