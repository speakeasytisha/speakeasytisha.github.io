My next major project is to spend an extended period in Australia in 2027, combining travel, everyday life and possibly work. In English, I can usually communicate what I mean, but I want my grammar and phrasing to become more automatic so that I can speak with greater precision and ease.

06 · PAST → PRESENT → FUTURE

Tell the story, then control the timeline

One of the most useful B2 skills is choosing a tense because of the meaning you want — not because you are translating from French.

PAST

A travel experience

Tell me about a trip that changed the way you travel. Where did you go? What happened? What was different?

  • past simple for finished events
  • used to for an old habit
  • past perfect for the earlier past
NOW

What has changed?

How has travel changed for you over the years? What have you learned from those experiences?

  • present perfect for experience/change
  • present simple for what is true now
FUTURE

Australia

What are you planning? What is already arranged? What might change? What will you decide later?

  • present continuous for arrangements
  • going to for intentions
  • will for decisions/predictions
Model answer · Strong B1

A few years ago, I travelled to Thailand and it changed the way I like to travel. Before that, I used to plan more things in advance, but during that trip I discovered that I enjoyed travelling more freely. Since then, I’ve had other experiences as a backpacker. In 2027, I’m going to spend a long period in Australia, and I hope I’ll be able to combine travel, everyday life and work.

Model answer · B2

One trip that genuinely changed my approach to travel was Thailand. Before we went, I had usually preferred having a fairly clear plan, but once we were there we gradually adopted a more flexible backpacking style. Since then, I’ve realised that I enjoy having enough structure to feel organised without planning every detail. That experience is one reason Australia appeals to me: we’re planning to spend an extended period there in 2027, but I also want to leave room for unexpected opportunities.

09 · FINAL MISSION

Who you are + where you’re going

Réutilisez ce que vous avez travaillé dans une prise de parole et une production écrite proches de situations réelles.

ORAL · 2 MINUTES

Explain your project to someone you have just met in Australia.

  1. Introduce yourself briefly.
  2. Mention one travel experience that shaped you.
  3. Explain your 2027 Australia project.
  4. Say what you hope to gain from it.
  5. Finish with one thing you are still deciding.
Model · Strong B1

Hi, I’m from France and I’m planning to spend a long period in Australia in 2027. I’ve always enjoyed travelling, and one experience that really changed me was a backpacking trip in Thailand. It made me realise that I like discovering a country with more freedom. In Australia, I’d like to travel, experience everyday life and possibly work. I also want to improve my English because I want to communicate more naturally. We still haven’t decided exactly where we’ll spend the most time, so that will depend on the opportunities we find.

Model · B2

Hi, I’m from France, and my next big project is to spend an extended period in Australia in 2027. A few years ago, a backpacking trip in Thailand changed the way I approach travel because I discovered that I enjoy having flexibility and adapting as I go. Australia feels like a natural next step: I’d like to combine travel with everyday life, meet people, possibly work and become much more comfortable using English spontaneously. We have a general direction, but we deliberately haven’t fixed every detail yet because I’d like to be able to respond to opportunities once we’re there.

WRITING · 100–140 WORDS

A new housemate asks you to introduce yourself before you move in.

Write a friendly message: who you are, why you are in Australia, what kind of person/housemate you are, and one question for them.

0 wordsTarget: 100–140
Model · Strong B1

Hi, thanks for your message. I’m from France and I’m coming to Australia for a long stay in 2027. I’d like to travel around the country, improve my English and possibly work while I’m there. I’m quite independent, but I also enjoy spending time with other people and sharing a meal or going out sometimes. I’ve travelled as a backpacker before, so I’m used to adapting and living with other people. At home, I like things to be relaxed but reasonably tidy. I’m looking forward to discovering the area and meeting everyone. By the way, how do you usually organise food and cleaning in the house?

Model · B2

Hi, thanks for getting in touch. I’m from France and I’ll be spending an extended period in Australia in 2027, partly to travel and partly to experience everyday life rather than simply visit as a tourist. I’m fairly independent and easy-going, and previous backpacking trips have made me comfortable adapting to new routines and sharing space with different people. I enjoy socialising, but I also appreciate having some quiet time, so a friendly house with a good balance would suit me well. I’m tidy without being obsessive, and I’m always happy to discuss how a household prefers to organise shared responsibilities. What is the usual routine in the house during the week?
