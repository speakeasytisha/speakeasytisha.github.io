Strong B1

Sure. In my previous role, I often had to improve a process that involved several teams. One example was when we needed to make the workflow simpler and reduce unnecessary steps. I spoke with the people involved, identified the main problem and proposed a clearer process.

B2 / VTest target

Absolutely. A good example would be a cross-functional process I had to streamline. I first mapped the existing workflow, then spoke with the teams using it to understand the friction points. From “what you did” to “what you can bring”

Your pitch should not sound like a list of duties. Build a short story that moves from experience → action → value → next step.

Your background

STEP 03

STEP 04

What you can bring next

Connect your experience to a future employer: understand fast, ask the right questions and turn complexity into workable solutions.

Think: role + years + type of environment. Keep it factual and easy to follow.

Choose 2–3 actions that sound concrete. Avoid “I did many things”.

This is the part recruiters remember. Explain what your experience allows you to do for a new team.

Focus on the next challenge, not on escaping your current situation.

Keep the pitch conversational

A recruiter does not need your whole CV in 60 seconds.

  1. Start broad: who you are professionally.
  2. Add proof: what you actually worked on.
  3. Name the value: what that taught you to bring.
  4. Finish forward: what you want next and why Australia.

Upgrade the language

“I organised meetings.”→ I brought different teams together to align on priorities and next steps.
“I analysed processes.”→ I identified friction points and translated them into practical improvements.
“I worked with many departments.”→ I worked cross-functionally and adapted my communication to different stakeholders.

Strong B1

I spent six years working as a process analyst. My role was to understand how processes worked in practice, identify problems and work with different teams to make them clearer and more efficient. That experience helped me become organised, analytical and comfortable communicating with different people. I'm now looking for a new challenge where I can use those skills in an international environment. Australia interests me because I want both professional experience abroad and a real change of lifestyle.

B2 / VTest target

I spent six years working in process analysis, with a strong focus on understanding how workflows operated in practice and how they could be improved. I regularly worked across teams to identify friction points, clarify needs and turn them into workable solutions. What I can bring is a structured way of thinking, the ability to communicate with different stakeholders and a practical approach to continuous improvement. I'm now looking for a role where I can apply that experience in a new environment, and Australia appeals to me because it combines an international professional challenge with a broader personal move.

05 · CAREER BREAK

Explain your current situation positively and professionally

You do not need to over-explain. Keep the answer factual, forward-looking and comfortable.

Past→Transition→Action now→Next goal

Question: “What have you been doing since leaving your previous role?”

Strong B1

After six years in the same company, my position came to an end because of a reorganisation. I decided to use the transition as an opportunity to think carefully about my next step. I’ve been staying active, improving my English and preparing for a possible move to Australia. I’m now ready to return to work in a role where I can use my experience and continue developing.

B2 / VTest target

My previous role ended as part of a wider reorganisation, so I chose to use the transition productively rather than rush into the first available position. I’ve been focusing on my English, keeping a structured routine and exploring opportunities in Australia. It has also given me time to clarify what I want from my next role: a position where I can use my process-analysis experience, work with different stakeholders and contribute in an international environment.

Aide FR : Évitez les formulations négatives comme “I have no job” ou “I’m doing nothing”. Préférez transition, reorganisation, exploring opportunities, preparing for my next role.

10 · AUSTRALIAN WORKPLACE ENGLISH

Communicate naturally at work — not just with slang

The goal is not to “sound Australian”. It is to understand a workplace style that is often relatively direct, collaborative and informal while still being professional. Practise the communication moves you will actually need in meetings, projects and everyday teamwork.

01

Direct, not blunt

Get to the point, but soften disagreement and requests enough to keep the exchange constructive.

“I’m not sure that approach will solve the main issue. Could we look at another option?”
02

Informal, not careless

First names and relaxed wording are common, but clarity, reliability and preparation still matter.

“Hi Sam, just checking whether you’ve had a chance to review the latest version.”
03

Collaborative

Show that you can contribute your view while making space for other people’s expertise.

“That’s how I see it from the process side. What are you seeing operationally?”
04

Action-oriented

Translate discussion into ownership, timing and a clear next step.

“I’ll update the workflow today and send the team a revised version by tomorrow morning.”
05

Comfortable with clarification

Asking a concise question is better than pretending you understood an unclear instruction.

“Just to confirm, do you want the full analysis or only the key issues for now?”
06

Evidence over theory

Use short examples and results when explaining a recommendation.

“We tried something similar before, and the main bottleneck was the handover between the two teams.”

Useful expressions you may hear — without forcing the slang

“No worries”

Often means “that’s fine / no problem”. Understand it first; use it only if it feels natural.

“Keen”

Interested or enthusiastic: “I’d be keen to learn more about the role.”

“Sounds good”

A very natural way to agree to a plan without sounding overly formal.

“Just checking…”

A useful softener for follow-ups: “Just checking whether the figures have been confirmed.”

Strong B1 model

My background is in process analysis, and I spent six years working on how teams and workflows operate in practice. A big part of my role was understanding problems, speaking with the people involved and helping make processes clearer and more efficient. That experience taught me to be structured, practical and comfortable working with different teams. My role ended because of a wider reorganisation, and I have used the transition to work on my English, stay active and think carefully about my next step. I’m now looking for a new professional challenge, and Australia interests me because I want international experience as well as a real lifestyle change. I’d be interested to know what the main priorities would be for someone joining this role in the first few months.

B2 / VTest target model

I’ve spent the last six years in process analysis, working at the point where operational needs, people and workflows meet. My role involved understanding how a process worked in practice, identifying friction points and then working across teams to turn those issues into realistic improvements. What I can bring is a structured way of analysing a problem, an ability to communicate with different stakeholders and a strong preference for solutions that actually work for the people using them. My previous role ended as part of a broader reorganisation, so I’ve used the transition to improve my English, maintain a disciplined routine and define what I want from the next stage of my career. Australia appeals to me because I’m looking for both an international professional challenge and a meaningful personal change. I’d be keen to understand what success in this position would look like after the first six months and which stakeholders the role works with most closely.
