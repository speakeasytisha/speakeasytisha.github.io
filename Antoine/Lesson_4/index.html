02 · 5–20 MIN · COMPLETE GRAMMAR REVIEW

Rules you know — accuracy you need at speaking speed

Use the tabs as a reference. The challenge underneath mixes all of the grammar points we have been consolidating.

Present simple

Routine, fact, general truth.

I train regularly. I work well in a team.

He/she/it → add -s: “She works.”

Present continuous

Now / current temporary period / arranged future.

I’m preparing for Australia at the moment.
We’re leaving next year.

Past simple

Finished past action or story.

I worked at Decathlon for six years. We travelled around Thailand.

With finished time: last year, in 2025, when we went…

Used to

Past habit/state that is no longer true or no longer the same.

We used to plan every part of our trips.

Form: used to + base verb.

Present perfect

Past connected to now / experience / unfinished period.

I’ve worked in a structured corporate environment for six years.

Do not use with a finished past time such as “last year”.

Present perfect continuous

Activity continuing up to now; duration matters.

I’ve been preparing for a move to Australia.

Often natural for current projects and recent ongoing activity.

Aide FR : past simple = terminé ; present perfect = lien avec maintenant ; present perfect continuous = activité/durée jusqu’à maintenant.

going to

Existing intention.

I’m going to look for work when I arrive.

Present continuous

Firm arrangement.

We’re flying to Australia in March.

will

Prediction, spontaneous decision, promise.

I think I’ll adapt quickly.

1st conditional

Realistic future possibility.

If I find a physical job, I’ll be comfortable with the workload.

2nd conditional

Hypothetical / imagined.

If I had to change industries completely, I’d be open to it.

Future time clauses

After when, if, as soon as, before, after, use present form.

When I arrive, I’ll start applying.
Aide FR : “When I arrive” et non “when I will arrive”.

Present simple

Wh-word + do/does + subject + base verb?

What kind of work do you want?

Past simple

Wh-word + did + subject + base verb?

Why did you leave your previous role?

With be

be + subject.

When are you available?

Subject questions

No do/does/did when the question word is the subject.

What makes you a reliable employee?

Indirect questions

Use statement word order after the opener.

Could you tell me what the hours are?

Follow-up questions

Keep the conversation moving instead of ending after one answer.

How does that usually work in your team?

Articles

a/an = one non-specific singular countable noun. the = specific. Zero article = general plural/uncountable.

I’m looking for a job. Work is important to me.

Prepositions

go/move to Australia · work/live in Australia · arrive in a country / at a workplace · travel around Australia.

Reflexives

I → myself · you → yourself · we → ourselves · they → themselves.

We organised the trip ourselves.

Third person

he/she/it works · doesn’t work · does he work?

A good manager listens and gives clear instructions.

Countability

work, advice, information, equipment, accommodation are normally uncountable.

I’m looking for work. I need some information.

Natural English

Avoid direct French calques.

I’m looking for a job — not “I search a job”.
I’ve been out of work for three months — not “since three months”.

Contrast

although / however / whereas / nevertheless

Although my background is analytical, I’m open to practical work.

Cause & result

because / therefore / as a result

I’m physically active, so I’m comfortable with demanding work.

Add information

also / moreover / in addition

I’m reliable. In addition, I adapt quickly.

Relative clauses

who people · which/that things · where places.

I’d like a job where I can learn quickly and be useful.

B2 target

06 · 38–46 MIN · PROFESSIONAL PITCH + CAREER TRANSITION

Same skills. Different objective.

Describe your previous experience clearly, then pivot to what you want in Australia: work, adaptability, practical experience and a new environment.

Your pitch will appear here.

Strong B1

I spent six years working as a process analyst, so I’m used to solving problems, organising information and working with different people. In Australia, I’m not looking only for the same type of job. I’m open to practical or hands-on work, and I’m ready to learn something new. I’m reliable, very active and comfortable working hard, so I think I could adapt well to different types of roles.

B2 target

I spent six years in process analysis, which gave me strong transferable skills in organisation, problem-solving and working across different teams. I value that experience, but I’m deliberately keeping my options open in Australia. I’d be very happy to take on something more practical or hands-on, particularly if it gives me the chance to learn quickly and contribute to a team. I’m physically active, adaptable and used to being disciplined, so I’m comfortable with the idea of moving into a very different working environment.

Career transition review

Question: “What have you been doing since leaving your previous role?”

Strong B1

My previous position ended because of a reorganisation. Since then, I’ve been preparing for Australia, improving my English and keeping a very active routine. I’ve also been thinking about the type of work I’d like to try there. I’m now ready to work again, and I’m open to different kinds of jobs.

B2 target

My previous role ended as part of a wider reorganisation, and I’ve used the transition to prepare seriously for Australia rather than rush into another similar position. I’ve been working on my English, staying very active and thinking about the kind of working experience I want next. I’m now ready to get back into work, and I’m intentionally open to a broader range of roles, including practical and hands-on jobs.

08 · 56–60 MIN · FINAL REVIEW MISSION

2-minute recruiter answer: “Tell me about yourself and what you’re looking for.”

This should pull together everything from the review: accurate grammar, natural structure, transferable skills, openness to practical work, Australia motivation and conversation control.

  1. Give a concise professional background.
  2. Give 2–3 transferable strengths.
  3. Explain the career transition positively.
  4. Explain why Australia.
  5. Say clearly that you are open to different types of work.
  6. Mention practical/hands-on work naturally.
  7. Finish with why you would be a good person to hire.
