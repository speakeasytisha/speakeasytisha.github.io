THE ESSAY SUCCESS FORMULA

Answer every bullet. Give real details. Check every sentence.

The VTest Business English writing task asks you to respond to a professional situation and guiding bullet points. This page teaches a reusable method, NOT a memorised answer.

YOUR 5-PART METHOD

1. Introduction (situation / viewpoint)
2. Bullet 1 (context and details)
3. Bullet 2 (action, reason, example)
4. Bullet 3 (result, lesson learned)
5. Conclusion (brief reflection)

NEW · CORE TRAINING · STEP BY STEP

One idea → a professional, convincing paragraph

Do not memorise a long essay. Learn seven writing moves. Build ONE real workplace idea into a stronger paragraph using POINT → REASON → EXAMPLE → RESULT, then add contrast, reinforcement and a conclusion. Every step shows what changed and why.

Our starting idea · Notre idée de départ

“Communication is good.” « La communication est bien. »

It is understandable, but too vague. Good for an initial idea, not a developed professional essay. We will make it specific, justify it, prove it and show its impact.

1

01 · STATE A POINT

Présenter une idée

BEFORE · Trop simple

Teamwork improves efficiency.

AFTER · Plus précis

Clear communication is essential for efficient teamwork.

Phrase to use: In my view, = À mon avis,

Why it is better: Use when you introduce your argument. Point = the main idea.

2

02 · GIVE A REASON

Expliquer pourquoi

BEFORE · Trop simple

It helps.

AFTER · Plus précis

This is because everyone understands their responsibilities.

Phrase to use: This is because = C’est parce que / Cela s’explique par le fait que

Why it is better: Use a full clause after because; because of + noun.

3

03 · GIVE EVIDENCE

Illustrer avec un exemple

BEFORE · Trop simple

We had meetings.

AFTER · Plus précis

For example, during a process review, I introduced short weekly updates.

Phrase to use: For example, = Par exemple,

Why it is better: Add a real action, situation or observable detail.

4

04 · SHOW THE RESULT

Présenter la conséquence

BEFORE · Trop simple

It worked.

AFTER · Plus précis

As a result, the team reduced misunderstandings and met the deadline.

Phrase to use: As a result, = En conséquence / Résultat :

Why it is better: A result must follow logically from the action.

5

05 · ADD A DIFFERENT VIEW

Nuancer / opposer

BEFORE · Trop simple

Some people disagree.

AFTER · Plus précis

Although meetings take time, brief updates can prevent delays later.

Phrase to use: Although = Bien que / Même si

Why it is better: Although + clause; however begins a contrasting sentence.

6

06 · REINFORCE THE IDEA

Renforcer / ajouter

BEFORE · Trop simple

It is good.

AFTER · Plus précis

Furthermore, this approach creates greater accountability across departments.

Phrase to use: Furthermore, = De plus / En outre

Why it is better: Add a second supporting benefit, not just the same idea again.

7

07 · DRAW A CONCLUSION

Conclure

BEFORE · Trop simple

Communication matters.

AFTER · Plus précis

Overall, structured communication helps teams work more efficiently without sacrificing accuracy.

Phrase to use: Overall, = Dans l’ensemble / Globalement

Why it is better: Summarise the argument; do not introduce a new example.

What you have created · Ce que tu as construit

P Point (your claim) → R Reason (why) → E Example (proof) → R Result (what changed). Then, if relevant, contrast an alternative and reinforce the benefit.

Une idée principale → sa justification → un exemple concret → le résultat. La nuance et le renforcement sont utiles, mais uniquement si cela répond au sujet.

Small grammar differences that change the meaning

because = parce que + proposition complète: We changed it because it was slow.

because of = à cause de / en raison de + nom: We changed it because of the delays.

although = bien que / même si + proposition: Although it was difficult, we succeeded. (Do not add “but” to the same contrast.)

however = cependant / toutefois, normally links two independent ideas: It was difficult. However, we succeeded.

despite / in spite of = malgré + nom ou -ing: Despite the delay, we succeeded.

as a result = en conséquence (result); for example = par exemple (illustration). They are not interchangeable.

FROM B1 TO B2+ · EXACT IMPROVEMENTS

Same message. Stronger writing. Understand every improvement.

Compare these versions in order. Under each model, the notes show what improved, where, how and why. These are training examples, not official grades or guaranteed CEFR scores.

A2 · THE FOUNDATION · Reveal + analyse
Communication is important at work. We need to talk together. We have meetings. This helps our team.

What changed / where / why: Clear basic idea, but almost no explanation, example or measurable result.

La communication est importante au travail. Nous devons échanger. Nous avons des réunions. Cela aide notre équipe.

B1 · ADD REASON AND EXAMPLE · Reveal + analyse
Good communication is important because people need to understand their tasks. For example, we had short meetings to discuss problems. As a result, our team worked better.

What changed / where / why: WHERE: “because” adds the reason; “For example” introduces an action; “As a result” states the consequence. WHY: the reader can follow the logic.

Une bonne communication est importante parce que chacun doit comprendre ses tâches. Par exemple, nous avons organisé de courtes réunions pour discuter des problèmes. Résultat : notre équipe a mieux travaillé.

B2 · MAKE THE WORKPLACE IMPACT SPECIFIC · Reveal + analyse
Clear communication is essential for efficient teamwork because it helps colleagues understand their priorities and responsibilities. For example, during a process-improvement project, I introduced weekly progress updates to identify potential delays. As a result, we completed the project on schedule and reduced avoidable errors.

What changed / where / why: WHAT: vague words become “priorities”, “responsibilities”, “potential delays”, and “avoidable errors”. HOW: specific situation + personal action + two concrete outcomes. WHY: this reads as credible professional evidence.

Une communication claire est essentielle à un travail d’équipe efficace : elle permet de comprendre les priorités et responsabilités. Par exemple, lors d’un projet d’amélioration des processus, j’ai mis en place des points hebdomadaires pour repérer les retards éventuels. Ainsi, nous avons tenu les délais et réduit les erreurs évitables.

B2+ / C1 · ADD BALANCE AND PRECISION · Reveal + analyse
Although regular updates require some time, they can prevent costly misunderstandings. In my previous role, I introduced concise weekly progress reviews to clarify responsibilities and flag potential delays. Consequently, the team delivered a process-improvement project on schedule while maintaining the accuracy of its reporting. This experience reinforced my belief that efficient communication should be both structured and proportionate to the task.

What changed / where / why: WHERE: “Although” introduces a counterargument; “Consequently” links action and result; “while maintaining” shows balance. WHY: nuanced reasoning, careful word choice and a broader conclusion — not simply longer words.

Bien que les points réguliers prennent du temps, ils peuvent éviter de coûteux malentendus. Dans mon ancien poste, j’ai instauré des suivis hebdomadaires concis pour clarifier les responsabilités et anticiper les retards. L’équipe a ainsi livré le projet dans les délais tout en préservant la fiabilité des rapports. Cette expérience a renforcé ma conviction qu’une communication efficace doit être structurée et adaptée à la tâche.

Important rule: better does not mean more complicated.

A clear and accurate B1 sentence is better than an incorrect C1-style sentence. Upgrade one feature at a time: precise noun → reason → example → result → relevant contrast.

Speaking rehearsal → writing improvement

Say your answer aloud first: “My point is … / The reason is … / For example … / As a result …”. Then write it and replace vague words with precise professional vocabulary.

Oral model · B1

“I think regular meetings help the team because we can discuss problems. For example, we checked our progress every week. As a result, we finished on time.”

Je pense que des réunions régulières aident l’équipe, car elles permettent de discuter des problèmes. Par exemple, nous faisions le point chaque semaine. Résultat : nous avons terminé dans les délais.

Written model · B2

“Regular progress reviews improve coordination because they help teams identify problems early. For example, weekly meetings enabled us to clarify responsibilities. As a result, we completed the project on schedule.”

Des suivis réguliers améliorent la coordination puisqu’ils permettent d’identifier les problèmes rapidement. Des réunions hebdomadaires nous ont par exemple aidés à clarifier les responsabilités, ce qui nous a permis de respecter le calendrier.

03 · DEVELOP

Build one complete point

Build a paragraph with POINT → REASON → EXAMPLE → RESULT. Avoid a list of short, disconnected opinions.

Model · Modèle

One challenge was the tight deadline. This mattered because our tasks depended on one another. For example, we agreed on daily updates. As a result, we finished on time.

Un défi était le délai serré. C’était important car nos tâches dépendaient les unes des autres. Par exemple, nous avons convenu de points quotidiens. Résultat : nous avons terminé à temps.

Exam strategy · Conseil

Each body paragraph needs a specific action or example.

One paragraph, four moves

Point: We faced a communication problem. (Nous avions un problème de communication.)

Reason: Different departments used different information. (Plusieurs services utilisaient des informations différentes.)

Example: For example, I introduced a shared dashboard. (Par exemple, j’ai mis en place un tableau de bord partagé.)

Result: As a result, the team saved time. (Par conséquent, l’équipe a gagné du temps.)

04 · CONNECT

Use logical connectors accurately

Choose connectors according to meaning: ADD → because/reason → example → contrast → result. “However” does NOT mean “therefore”.

Model · Modèle

Although we disagreed at first, we found a practical solution. As a result, communication improved.

Bien que nous ayons été en désaccord au début, nous avons trouvé une solution pratique. Par conséquent, la communication s’est améliorée.

Exam strategy · Conseil

Avoid beginning every sentence with “Moreover”.

05 · SEQUENCE

Tell a professional story in order

For workplace experience prompts, use past simple for finished actions; past continuous for background; present perfect for what you have learned or achieved.

06 · UPGRADE

Replace vague English with precise English

Upgrade weak phrases: good → effective / beneficial; bad → inefficient / challenging; things → tasks / procedures; do → implement / manage / handle. Accuracy always comes first.

Model · Modèle

J’ai aidé l’équipe à améliorer le processus en clarifiant les responsabilités.

Exam strategy · Conseil

Better: precise verbs + meaningful details, not unnecessarily long words.

07 · FINISH

Finish with an outcome or reflection

The conclusion should answer: What changed? What did you learn? Why is this useful professionally? Do not introduce an unrelated argument at the end.

Model · Modèle

Overall, this experience taught me that listening carefully and adapting my approach can improve teamwork.

Dans l’ensemble, cette expérience m’a appris qu’écouter attentivement et adapter mon approche peuvent améliorer le travail d’équipe.

Exam strategy · Conseil

Use a short conclusion, usually one or two sentences.

SEE THE DIFFERENCE

The same professional prompt, four levels

Practice prompt

Describe a time you worked with someone whose approach was different from yours.

Cover: who the colleague was and your differences / how you handled the challenge / what happened and what you learned.

Open the models one at a time. Compare the specific actions, reasons, examples and conclusions. Your objective is clear B1 writing, then stronger B2 writing—not artificial C1 vocabulary.

A2 · Reveal full essay + study the progression
I worked with a colleague on a project. We had different ideas. I wanted to finish the work quickly, but my colleague wanted to check every detail. At first, it was difficult. We talked about our ideas and made a simple plan. I prepared the information, and my colleague checked the results. We met every morning to discuss our progress. The project was finished on time. I was happy because we worked well together. This experience taught me that people can have different opinions and still be a good team.

Study the structure: opening → three bullets → outcome. Check how examples and connectors improve as the level rises. These are independent training models, not official VTest answers.

B1 · Reveal full essay + study the progression
In my previous role, I worked with a colleague whose way of working was different from mine. I usually focused on completing tasks efficiently, whereas my colleague preferred to check every detail. Initially, this created some tension because we had a tight deadline. To manage the situation, I suggested a short meeting to discuss our priorities. We agreed to divide the responsibilities: I prepared the reports, and my colleague reviewed the figures. As a result, we finished the project on time and avoided unnecessary mistakes. Overall, this experience taught me that listening to different perspectives can improve teamwork and help a team achieve better results.

Study the structure: opening → three bullets → outcome. Check how examples and connectors improve as the level rises. These are independent training models, not official VTest answers.

B2 · Reveal full essay + study the progression
During a process-improvement project, I collaborated with a colleague whose working style differed significantly from mine. While I prioritised efficiency and clear deadlines, my colleague preferred to analyse each detail before making a decision. Initially, our contrasting approaches slowed our progress and created uncertainty about responsibilities. Rather than allowing the disagreement to continue, I proposed a meeting to clarify our shared objectives. We agreed to separate the work into stages: I coordinated implementation, while my colleague checked the accuracy of our results. We also introduced brief weekly updates to identify potential issues early. Consequently, the project was completed on schedule, and the quality of our reporting improved. Looking back, the experience reinforced the importance of adapting my communication style, respecting different perspectives and finding practical compromises to achieve a common goal.

Study the structure: opening → three bullets → outcome. Check how examples and connectors improve as the level rises. These are independent training models, not official VTest answers.

C1 · Reveal full essay + study the progression
One of the most valuable professional experiences I have had involved collaborating with a colleague whose approach to problem-solving was markedly different from my own. I tended to prioritise efficiency and timely implementation, whereas my colleague favoured extensive analysis before committing to a decision. Although both approaches had clear advantages, the contrast initially caused delays and misunderstandings within the project. Recognising that the issue was not a lack of commitment but a difference in priorities, I initiated a constructive discussion about our shared objectives. We subsequently established clear responsibilities, agreed on decision-making criteria and introduced regular progress reviews. This enabled us to maintain momentum without compromising accuracy. The project was delivered successfully, and communication within the team improved considerably. More importantly, the experience taught me that effective collaboration is not about imposing one approach, but about combining complementary strengths to deliver a stronger outcome.

Study the structure: opening → three bullets → outcome. Check how examples and connectors improve as the level rises. These are independent training models, not official VTest answers.
