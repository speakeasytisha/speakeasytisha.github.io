02 · GRAMMAR MAP · 5 MIN

Your five precision locks

1

Time anchor first

Finished time → Past Simple. Life / unfinished connection → Present Perfect. Earlier past → Past Perfect.

last year → worked · since June → have been preparing · before the reorganisation → had worked

2

Auxiliary before subject

Direct question = question word + auxiliary + subject + base form.

Why did you choose…? · What have you learned…? · What can you bring…?

3

One tense marker is enough

After did / does / can / would / might, use the base verb.

Did you enjoy…? · He doesn’t understand… · I could relocate…

4

Noun needs a frame

Use a/an for one non-specific countable noun, the when identified, and no article for many general plural/uncountable ideas.

a process · the role we discussed · experience · stakeholders

5

Learn chunks, not translations

Prepositions and verb patterns are often fixed combinations.

go to Australia · interested in working · responsible for improving · used to working

Aide FR : Avant de parler, repérez mentalement le “signal” : temps fini ? question ? modal ? nom singulier ? expression fixe ? Cela déclenche automatiquement la bonne structure.

09 · SPEAKING PRODUCTION · 4 MIN

Read, react, then develop your answer

Workplace text: A company is redesigning its onboarding process for new employees. The current process includes several long documents, separate meetings with different departments and repeated information. The company wants the new process to be clearer, faster and easier for employees to follow.

1

What is the main problem with the current onboarding process?

2

What would you change first, and why?

01:00

Strong B1

The main problem is that the process is too long and some information is repeated. I would first combine the documents and meetings into a clearer sequence. This would help new employees understand what they need to do and avoid wasting time.

B2 / VTest target

The main weakness is fragmentation: new employees receive overlapping information from several departments, which makes the process longer than necessary. I would start by mapping the full onboarding journey and removing duplicated steps. That would make responsibilities clearer and create a more consistent experience for new starters.

10 · WRITING PRODUCTION · 5 MIN

100-word B1/B2 workplace response

Prompt: An Australian company is considering allowing new employees to work partly from home after their first month. Write a short response giving your view.

  • State your position clearly
  • Give one advantage
  • Give one possible difficulty
  • Suggest one practical solution
0 wordsTarget: 100+ words · 2+ connectors · controlled verb forms

Strong B1

I think hybrid work can be a good option after the first month because new employees have time to understand the company and meet their colleagues first. Working from home can then give them more flexibility and reduce commuting time. However, it can also make communication more difficult, especially for someone who is still learning the job. I would suggest keeping regular team meetings and clear weekly priorities. This would give employees more flexibility while making sure they still receive enough support and stay connected with the team.

B2 / VTest target

In my view, a hybrid arrangement can work well once a new employee has completed an initial period in the workplace. It offers greater flexibility and can reduce unnecessary commuting, which may improve concentration and work-life balance. The main risk is weaker communication during the onboarding phase. To avoid this, managers could set clear expectations, schedule regular check-ins and define which meetings should take place in person. This would preserve the advantages of remote work without reducing the support and collaboration that new employees need.

I’m now ready to bring that experience into a new environment, contribute from the start and keep developing in an international setting.
