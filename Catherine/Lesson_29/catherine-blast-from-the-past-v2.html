Catherine — A Blast from the Past

A complete step-by-step lesson to help you reconnect with an old friend or relative, talk about your life now, remember the past, and express what has shaped you.

💌 Reconnect 🕰️ Memories 👨‍👩‍👧 Family 🧭 Life journey 🗣️ Step by step

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • contact someone you have not spoken to in a long time,
  • talk about your present life and your regular routine,
  • talk about the past and shared memories,
  • use present simple, present continuous, past simple, past continuous, and present perfect more naturally,
  • write and speak in a warm, personal, structured way.

4) Grammar toolkit — key rules and key words

🟢 Present simple — regular life, routine, facts

When do you use it?

Use it for what you do regularly, what is generally true, and your current situation in a general sense.

Key words: usually, often, every week, always, sometimes

Examples

You live in France.

You work as an assessor in your free time.

You usually run twice a week.

🟣 Present continuous — now / around now

When do you use it?

Use it for what is happening now or around now.

Key words: now, at the moment, these days, currently, right now

Examples

You are getting back in touch with an old friend.

You are planning a new trip to Denmark.

Your granddaughter is growing up fast.

🔵 Past simple — finished past

When do you use it?

Use it for finished events in the past.

Key words: yesterday, last week, when we were younger, in high school, years ago

Examples

You met in high school.

You laughed a lot together.

You lost touch after school.

🟤 Past continuous — past background / interrupted action

When do you use it?

Use it for what was happening at a moment in the past, especially with another action.

Key words: while, when, at that time, during that period

Examples

While you were studying, you were also spending time together.

When life was changing, you were going in different directions.

You were talking every day at that time.

🟠 Present perfect — what has changed up to now

When do you use it?

Use it for life changes, experience, and what connects the past to the present.

Key words: since, for, already, yet, ever, never, recently, so far

Examples

You have been retired for 7 months.

You have worked as an assessor for 6 months.

You have never forgotten those memories.

Many things have changed since then.

5) Step-by-step catch-up guide — what to say

Step 1 — Open the conversation

It has been such a long time.

I was thinking about you recently.

I would love to catch up.

Hello! It has been such a long time. I was thinking about you recently, and I would love to catch up.

Step 2 — Say what you do now

I am retired now, but...

These days, I...

I usually...

I am retired now, but I work as an assessor in my free time. These days, I also run twice a week and enjoy a calmer rhythm of life.

Step 3 — Talk about your children and family

I have ... children.

My son lives in...

My granddaughter is...

I have children, and my son lives in Denmark. My family has grown, and this has become a very important part of my life.

Step 4 — Explain what has changed

Since then, many things have changed.

I have...

Life has taken me in different directions.

Since then, many things have changed. I have retired, I have started working as an assessor, and I have built a different life from the one I had when we were younger.

Step 5 — Remember the past together

Do you remember when we used to...?

I still remember...

When we were in high school...

Do you remember when we used to talk for hours after school? I still remember those moments very clearly. When we were younger, those conversations meant a lot to me.

Step 6 — Say what you miss / what made you who you are

I miss...

I never forgot...

That time helped make me who I am today.

I never forgot your friendship and the way we used to laugh together. Those memories are still important to me, and in many ways, that period helped make me who I am today.

8) Writing and speaking studio — emotionally magic catch-up

Speaking recipe

  1. Open warmly
  2. Say what you do now
  3. Talk about your family
  4. Say what has changed
  5. Share a memory
  6. Say what you miss or never forgot

Model catch-up paragraph

Hello, it has been such a long time. I was thinking about you recently, and I would love to catch up. I am retired now, but I work as an assessor in my free time, and I usually run twice a week. Since we last spoke, many things have changed, and my family has grown. My son lives in Denmark now, and I am planning another visit there. I still remember our time together, and I have never forgotten those moments. In many ways, those memories helped make me who I am today.

10) Speaking missions — go deeper

Speaking mission A — introduce your life now

Use this frame:

These days, I __________.
I usually __________.
I am also __________.
This part of my life is important because __________.

Model answer:

These days, I live a quieter life. I usually run twice a week, and I work as an assessor in my free time. I am also planning another trip to Denmark to see my family. This part of my life is important because it gives me balance and meaning.

Speaking mission B — explain how life has changed

Use this frame:

Since we last spoke, __________.
I have __________.
My family has __________.
However, __________.

Model answer:

Since we last spoke, many things have changed. I have retired, but I have also started working as an assessor in my free time. My family has grown, and my son now lives in Denmark. However, I have never forgotten the people and memories that were important to me in the past.

Speaking mission C — talk about a memory

Use this frame:

Do you remember when we used to __________?
At that time, we __________.
While we were __________, we were also __________.
I still remember __________.

Model answer:

Do you remember when we used to talk for hours after school? At that time, we were full of dreams and curiosity. While we were studying, we were also imagining what our future would look like. I still remember the atmosphere, the friendship, and the feeling that anything was possible.

11) Writing missions — guided and personal

Write a little more each time. Use the models to guide you.

Writing mission A — short message

Task: Write 4–5 lines to reopen contact.

Checklist:

  • Open warmly
  • Say why you are writing
  • Say one thing about your life now
  • Finish kindly

Model

Hello, it has been such a long time. I was thinking about you recently, and I wanted to write to you. I am retired now, but I work as an assessor in my free time, and my family has grown a lot. I would love to hear how you are and what you have been doing.

Writing mission B — a deeper catch-up

Task: Write 6–8 lines about who you are now, what has changed, and what you still remember.

Checklist:

  • Use present simple
  • Use present perfect
  • Use one memory sentence
  • Use one connector

Model

These days, I live a quieter life, but I still like to stay active. I have been retired for several months, and I work as an assessor in my free time because I enjoy helping young people. My family has grown, and my son now lives in Denmark. Since we last spoke, many things have changed. However, I still remember our school days and the many conversations we used to have after class.
