03 / TEACH → PRACTISE → SPEAK

Past simple — what you did before

Meaning: a finished action or period in the past. French: j'ai travaillé / je travaillais when describing a completed earlier period.

How to build it

+ I worked as a social worker.

− I didn't work as an assessor then.

? Did you work with families?

Rule: regular verb + -ed; irregular verbs change (go → went, meet → met). After did / didn't, use the base verb: “Did you work?” not “Did you worked?”

Clues: before, in the past, when I was..., several years ago, last year.

🎙️ Speaking ladder: “I worked as…” → “I worked as… and helped…” → “I worked as… because…”
04 / TEACH → PRACTISE → SPEAK

Present simple — what you do now

Meaning: your current role, habits and regular responsibilities. French: je travaille / je participe.

How to build it

+ I attend hearings.

− I don't decide cases alone.

? Do you support young people?

Rule: I/you/we/they + base verb; he/she/it + verb-s. Questions: do/does + subject + base verb. Exception: be uses am/is/are without do (“Are you an assessor?”).

Clues: now, currently, usually, regularly, sometimes, every week.

🎙️ Speaking ladder: “I am an assessor.” → “I take part in hearings.” → “My role involves…”
05 / CONTRAST

Used to — connect your two careers

Meaning: something was true before, but is different now. French: avant, je… / j'avais l'habitude de….

The pattern

+ I used to be a social worker.

− I didn't use to work in court.

? Did you use to work with families?

Important: after did, write “use to” (not “used to”). Be used to + -ing means being accustomed to something; it is a different structure. Example: “I'm used to speaking with families.”

Contrast: I used to be a social worker. I now serve as an assessor.

🎙️ Speaking ladder: past role → contrast word (“now”, “whereas”) → current responsibilities.
06 / EXPERIENCE

Present perfect — experience that matters now

Meaning: life experience with a connection to the present, without naming a finished time. French often uses j'ai + participe passé, but the English meaning is not identical.

How to build it

+ I have worked with young people.

− I haven't worked in every area of social care.

? Have you ever taken part in a running race?

Rule: have/has + past participle (worked, been, done, seen). He/she/it has. Use for + duration, since + starting point. Contrast: “I worked as a social worker for many years” (finished) vs “I have gained valuable experience” (relevant now). Do not combine present perfect with a finished time such as “yesterday”.

Clues: ever, never, already, yet, since, for, over the years.

Important distinction: I am retired = your current situation; I have been retired for a year = the situation started one year ago and continues now. You can be retired from paid employment and still volunteer as an assessor.
🎙️ Speaking ladder: “I have been retired for a year.” → “I have gained useful skills.” → “My experience has helped me...”
06B / BUILDING STRONG ANSWERS

Grammar you need before the longer B1 models

Learn each pattern, then try its mini exercise before using it in your self-introduction.

1. Responsibilities: need to / have to + verb

Meaning: necessity or responsibility — je dois / il faut que je...

+ I need to listen carefully.

− I don't need to solve every problem alone.

? Do you have to work as part of a team?

After need to and have to, use the base verb. Don't have to = not necessary (not prohibited). Mustn't = prohibited.

2. Explaining work: involve + -ing / help + person + verb

Meaning: describe tasks — ce rôle consiste à... / cela m'a aidée à...

+ My role involves listening to people.

− It doesn't involve working alone all the time.

? Does your role involve teamwork?

Use involve + -ing (not involve to listen). Helped me develop or helped me to develop are both correct.

3. Giving reasons and results: because / so / due to

Meaning: cause and consequence — parce que / donc / en raison de.

Cause I moved groups because I improved.

Result I made progress, so my coach moved me.

Noun phrase Due to my progress, my coach moved me up.

Because + clause: because I made progress. Because of / due to + noun: because of my progress. Avoid “because of I improved.”

4. Recent achievements: past simple vs present perfect

Past simple = finished event: “My coach recently moved me into a higher-level running group.” Present perfect = present result: “I have improved my endurance.” French: mon entraîneur m'a fait changer de groupe / j'ai progressé et cela se voit aujourd'hui.

+ I have improved. − I haven't stopped running. ? Have you joined a new group? With a specific finished date, use the past simple.

5. Describing people, comparison and past events

Relative clause: “My coach, who knows my ability, moved me to a higher-level group.” Comparison: “I perform well for my age group.” Avoid unsupported rankings or saying you are better than everybody. Past simple: “I went to cheer on my son and other relatives at a race.”

+ My children take part in races. − I didn't run in that particular event. ? Did you go to support your family?

Only then use a B1 model: connect your background with because, which, who, although, whereas, but favour short, accurate answers over complicated memorised sentences.
