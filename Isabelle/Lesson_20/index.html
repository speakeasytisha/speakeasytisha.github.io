01 · Why this matters

When numbers become professional confidence

Your real-life situations

  • Speaking with a recruiter or headhunter.
  • Confirming a phone number, postcode or email address.
  • Discussing property prices, rents, deposits and service charges.
  • Reading Dutch-style addresses and postcodes aloud in English.
  • Clarifying information when the line is bad or the accent is difficult.

The professional habit

In the Netherlands, communication is often direct, practical and precise. On the phone, your goal is not to sound perfect. Your goal is to be clear, organised and reliable.

Let me read that back to make sure I have everything correctly.

02 · Dutch reality check

Formats you will see in the Netherlands

Phone

+31 6 12 34 56 78

International format. Drop the first zero after +31. A Dutch mobile number often starts with 06 domestically.

“plus thirty-one, six, twelve, thirty-four, fifty-six, seventy-eight”

Amsterdam landline

020 612 55 22

Amsterdam area code is commonly written with 020. Read it slowly and group the digits.

“zero two zero, six one two, fifty-five, twenty-two”

Postcode

1075 AT Amsterdam

Dutch postcodes use four digits + two letters. When confirming, spell the letters.

“one zero seven five, A for Amsterdam, T for Tilburg”

Property price

€ 895,000 k.k.

In Dutch property listings, k.k. means buyer’s costs. For new-build homes, you may see v.o.n..

“eight hundred and ninety-five thousand euros, buyer’s costs”

03 · Numbers foundation

Speak numbers clearly on the phone

Key rules

Digit by digitUse for phone numbers, postcodes, reference numbers and email confirmation.
Grouped numbersUse for telephone blocks: 06 12 34 56 78.
Large numbersUse for prices: €895,000 = eight hundred and ninety-five thousand euros.
Decimals and ratesUse for yields, fees and percentages: 2.5% = two point five percent.
Read-back habitAlways repeat critical data to confirm accuracy.

04 · Letters, spelling and email addresses

Sound professional when spelling in English

The problem letters

In British English, Z is usually zed. In American English, Z is usually zee. For the Netherlands and international business, either can be understood, but be consistent.

Useful spelling phrases

  • Could you spell that for me, please?
  • Is that B for Bravo or D for Delta?
  • That is M as in Martin, not N as in November.
  • There is a dot between your first name and last name.
  • The email address is info at am dot nl.
  • Let me repeat that back to you.

Spelling alphabet for real calls

Use a simple international spelling alphabet when the line is bad. You do not need to know all of it immediately, but these letters are very useful for names, postcodes and emails.
