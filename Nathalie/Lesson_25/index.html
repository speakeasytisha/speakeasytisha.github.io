One sound, several spellings

Part 2A • The priority contrast

/ɪ/ vs /iː/ — short I and long E

/ɪ/

Quick + relaxed

sit · live · ship · fit · fill · bit

  • Keep the vowel short.
  • Relax your jaw and lips.
  • Do not stretch it into “ee”.

Son bref et relâché. Ne l’allongez pas.

VS
/iː/

Long + tense

seat · leave · sheep · feet · feel · beat

  • Smile slightly.
  • Keep the tongue high.
  • Let the vowel last longer.

Son plus long et plus tendu. Étirez légèrement la voyelle.

Part 2B • Another I

/ɪ/ vs /aɪ/ — sit or site?

/aɪ/ is a moving vowel. Your mouth changes position during the sound. You can hear it in time, like, five, drive, island.

/aɪ/ est une diphtongue : votre bouche bouge pendant le son.

Part 2C • Your A family

/æ/, /eɪ/, /ʌ/ and /ɑː/

/æ/

Short A

cat · bad · man · plan · travel

Open your mouth wider. The sound sits between a French A and E.

/eɪ/

“Long A”

day · name · late · make · rain

This vowel moves. Do not freeze it on a single French vowel.

/ʌ/

Relaxed central vowel

cut · run · bus · love · country

Keep it short, central and relaxed.

/ɑː/

Open long vowel

car · start · calm · father

Open and lengthen the sound. Accent note: some words vary between UK and US English.

Part 2E • The hidden superstar

Schwa /ə/ — the most common relaxed vowel

/ə/

Schwa appears in unstressed syllables. It is short, neutral and relaxed.

Le schwa apparaît souvent dans les syllabes non accentuées. Le son est très court et relâché.

Why it matters: English does not give equal weight to every syllable. Strong syllables stand out; weak syllables often shrink toward schwa.

Part 4A • Written but not spoken

Silent letters — train your eye to ignore them

W

wrong · write · wrist · wrap

The W disappears before R.

K

know · knife · knee · knock

The K disappears before N at the beginning.

B

climb · comb · lamb · thumb

Final B often disappears after M.

L

walk · talk · half · calm

The L is silent in several frequent words.

T

listen · castle · Christmas

The T is silent in these common patterns.

GH

night · light · right · daughter

GH can be silent — but in laugh it becomes /f/.

Part 4B • High-impact consonants

H · TH · W/V · R · final consonants

💨

H — use air

Imagine gently breathing onto a mirror: hair, heat, hill.

👅

TH — tongue visible

/θ/ no vibration: think, three. /ð/ vibration: this, those.

👄

W vs V

W: rounded lips. V: top teeth touch bottom lip.

↩

English R

Pull your tongue slightly back. Do not use the back-of-throat French R.

🚪

Keep the final sound

English final consonants can carry meaning or grammar: work/worked, want/wanted, car/card.
