02 · READING STRATEGY

How to read without getting stuck.

Strong readers do not translate every word. They make decisions about what matters.

1

Preview

Read the title and first sentence. Predict the subject.

2

Skim

Read quickly for the general message. Ignore difficult details.

3

Scan

Look for names, numbers, causes, contrasts and examples.

4

Infer

Use the surrounding sentence to guess an unknown word.

5

React

Decide: Do I agree? Why? What is my example?

Open the “5-question reading compass”
  1. What is the text mainly about?
  2. What is the writer's key point?
  3. Which facts or examples support it?
  4. Which words can I understand from context?
  5. What do I think about the issue?

04 · GRAMMAR TOOL 1

Modals for opinions, advice and obligation.

These small verbs help you sound clear without sounding too absolute.

FORM

should / could / might / must + base verb

Positive: Children should have limits.

Negative: Platforms shouldn't collect too much data.

Question: Should parents decide?

No “to” after should, could, might or must.

MEANING

Choose the strength

couldone possible idea
shouldadvice / good idea
muststrong obligation
mightpossibility
have toexternal rule / necessity

05 · READING 1 · SOCIETY

Should social media have an age limit?

B1 · about 320 words · current France debate

France has been debating how far the law should go to protect children online — and where protection ends and personal freedom begins.

In 2026, French lawmakers approved a plan to stop children under 15 from using social media. Supporters said young people need stronger protection from addictive design, harmful content and pressure to stay online. They argued that families cannot solve the problem alone because social media platforms are designed to keep users' attention for as long as possible.

However, France's Constitutional Council blocked the measure in August. The judges did not say that protecting children was unnecessary. Instead, they decided that a broad ban could restrict freedom of expression and privacy too much, especially if age checks were not clearly organised. The government said it would work on a new version of the law.

The debate is difficult because both sides raise important questions. A strict age limit might reduce exposure to harmful content and give families a clear rule. On the other hand, teenagers also use social media to communicate, learn, create and take part in communities. Some critics therefore prefer rules that target the most harmful features of platforms instead of banning access completely.

There is no simple answer. The central question is how society can protect children without removing more freedom than necessary.

Adapted learning text. Current-topic inspiration: Reuters, 14 Aug 2026.

BEFORE YOU ANSWER

Find the contrast.

Look for: supporters said… / however… / on the other hand…

COMPREHENSION

Answer from the text.

The judges blocked it because they thought a general ban could limit freedom and privacy too much without clear safeguards.

06 · GRAMMAR TOOL 2

Present perfect vs past simple.

Use the tense to show whether a past event is connected to now or finished at a specific past time.

PRESENT PERFECT

have/has + past participle

Positive: Remote work has changed habits.

Negative: Some companies haven't changed.

Question: Has work become more flexible?

Use for experience, change, unfinished time, or a result connected to now.

PAST SIMPLE

past form / did + base verb

Positive: France introduced rules years ago.

Negative: Workers didn't answer.

Question: Did the policy work?

Use with a finished time: yesterday, in 2020, last year, two days ago.

SIGNALS

Useful clues

Present perfect: since, for, already, yet, ever, never, recently.

Past simple: last…, ago, yesterday, in + finished year.

Compare: I have lived here for 10 years. / I lived there in 2010.

INSTANT CHECK

Choose the correct tense.

07 · READING 2 · WORK & LIFE

Do we still know how to switch off?

B1 · about 330 words · work-life balance

Flexible work can give people more freedom, but it can also make the working day follow them home.

Across Europe, many employees are contacted for work outside their normal hours. A 2026 report described an “always-on” culture in which messages, emails and work apps can make it difficult to stop thinking about work. Remote and flexible workers are often contacted more frequently because the boundary between the office and home is less clear.

This does not mean flexible work is bad. For many people, it has improved daily life. They can avoid long journeys, organise family responsibilities more easily and sometimes choose the hours when they are most productive. The problem begins when flexibility becomes permanent availability.

Several European countries, including France, have introduced rules or agreements designed to protect a worker's right to rest. But a law is not enough if workplace culture still rewards people for replying late at night. Some workers also answer voluntarily because they worry that silence may make them appear less committed.

A practical solution may be a combination of rules and habits: clear expectations from managers, fewer unnecessary messages, and personal boundaries such as turning off work notifications after a certain hour. Technology created part of the problem, but it can also help create a healthier routine.

Adapted learning text. Current-topic inspiration: Euronews / Eurofound reporting, 3 Aug 2026.

READING CLUE

Cause → consequence → solution

Find one example of each. This structure is extremely useful in articles and essays.

COMPREHENSION

What does the text really say?

0/5
One advantage is that people can organise their day more easily. One disadvantage is that they may be contacted outside normal working hours.

08 · READING 3 · DIGITAL LIFE

AI for everyone — or only for the confident?

B1/B1+ · about 340 words · digital inclusion

Artificial intelligence is becoming an everyday tool, but people are not adopting it at the same speed.

In 2025, more than one third of internet users in the European Union used generative AI tools. The rate was much higher among younger adults than among people aged 55 to 74. This difference does not necessarily mean that older adults are less interested. Confidence, training, trust and the usefulness of a tool can all influence whether someone decides to try it.

AI can be practical in everyday life. It can help people organise information, understand a difficult document, practise a language, prepare questions before an appointment or compare options. For someone who is learning a new digital skill, the most important advantage may be the possibility of asking the same question several times without feeling embarrassed.

There are also reasons to be careful. AI can make mistakes, and users need to check important information. Privacy matters too: people should avoid sharing sensitive personal information if they do not understand how a service uses their data. Good digital skills therefore include both confidence and caution.

Digital inclusion is not simply about giving everyone access to a device. It also means helping people understand how to use new tools safely and independently. As more public services and everyday activities move online, that kind of support may become increasingly important.

Adapted learning text. Current-topic inspiration: Eurostat Digitalisation in Europe 2026 and European Commission Digital Decade 2026.

INFER MEANING

Do not reach for a dictionary first.

In “confidence and caution”, what does caution probably mean? Look at the examples after it.

COMPREHENSION + INFERENCE

Read between the lines.

0/5
Because people also need the skills and confidence to use digital tools safely, understand their limits and make independent decisions.

10 · READING 4 · QUALITY OF LIFE

Is quiet living always better living?

B1 · about 330 words · countryside vs city revisited

After an exam essay about countryside versus city life, here is a more complicated version of the question.

Many people imagine the countryside as peaceful and the city as noisy. There is some truth in that picture, but real life is more complicated. Recent discussion in France has shown how important quiet surroundings are to many people. Rural areas can offer more space, easier access to nature and fewer crowded streets. Housing can also be cheaper in some regions.

However, quiet does not automatically mean convenient. A village may have fewer doctors, shops, restaurants or public transport options. A car can become essential, and a beautiful house may require expensive renovation. Rural life can also be noisy in its own way: tractors, animals, church bells or nearby roads are part of the local environment.

Cities offer different advantages. Services are usually closer, public transport is more developed and cultural activities are easier to reach. On the negative side, people may face smaller homes, traffic, pollution and a faster pace of life.

The best place therefore depends on priorities. Someone who values space and nature may prefer a small town or village. Someone who wants easy access to healthcare, transport and activities may choose a city. Many people are now looking for a compromise: a medium-sized town that offers both services and a calmer daily rhythm.

Adapted learning text. Current-topic inspiration: recent French discussion on tranquillity, services and rural living, Aug 2026.

EXAM CONNECTION

Same subject. Better argument.

Notice the difference between a simple list of advantages and a balanced argument with however, therefore and depends on.

COMPREHENSION

Choose the evidence.

0/5
The writer suggests that a medium-sized town can offer a compromise between convenient services and a calmer lifestyle.

12 · FINAL MISSION

Read → decide → speak → write.

Choose one topic. Give your real opinion — not the “perfect” opinion.

SPEAKING · 90 SECONDS

Choose one question

Clear B1 model

In my view, employees should not be expected to answer normal work messages in the evening. The main reason is that people need real time to rest. For example, if you are always checking your phone, you never completely leave work. However, urgent situations can be different. Overall, I think companies should make the rules very clear.

Stretch B1+ model

I think the right to disconnect is important, but it should remain practical. A strict rule may not suit every job, especially when teams work across time zones. However, availability should be the exception rather than the norm. Clear expectations would protect employees while still allowing genuine emergencies to be handled.

WRITING · 120–160 WORDS

A short opinion article

Question: “Does technology make everyday life better, or simply busier?”

Intro · your positionReason 1 + exampleContrastConclusion
Words: 0Connectors: 0

Clear B1 model

I think technology makes everyday life better when we use it in a sensible way. It helps us communicate, find information and organise many practical tasks. For example, we can book tickets, speak to family members or learn something new very quickly. However, technology can also make life feel busier because messages and notifications are always arriving. Sometimes people feel that they have to answer immediately. In my view, the solution is not to reject technology but to control how we use it. We can turn off unnecessary notifications and choose moments when we do not use our phones. Overall, technology is useful, but we need good habits to make sure it serves us instead of controlling our time.

Stretch B1+ model

Technology has undoubtedly made many everyday tasks faster and more convenient, but convenience does not always create a calmer life. We can communicate instantly, access services online and use tools such as AI to understand complicated information. On the other hand, constant availability can create pressure and reduce the difference between work, leisure and rest. I therefore think the real issue is not whether technology is good or bad, but whether we use it deliberately. If people learn to set boundaries, check information carefully and protect their privacy, technology can improve independence without taking over their attention. In short, digital tools are most useful when we remain the ones making the decisions.
