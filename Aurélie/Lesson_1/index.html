Your town, your heritage

Stiring-Wendel grew around glassmaking and iron and steel industries. In 1857, it became an independent municipality. The historic Sainte-Marthe mine shaft is one of the town’s industrial landmarks. Its location near Germany makes cross-border connections part of the region’s character.

Language tool · in / near / on

in + city, region, country: I live in Stiring-Wendel.

near + place: It is near the German border.

in the + direction: It is in the northeast of France.

on + boundary: It is on the French–German border. (Use only if accurate for the place described.)

03

FIRST UNDERSTAND · THEN PRACTISE

Build an English sentence

Rule 1 · Subject + verb + information

WHO? I → ACTION manage → WHAT? fleet information.

English normally needs a subject. Say I work, not simply work. For your routine and your job, use the present simple.

Affirmative

I work with colleagues.
She works with clients.

Negative

I do not (don't) manage repairs.
She does not (doesn't) work here.

Question

Do you use English?
Does she use the software?

Remember: he/she/it + verb-s in affirmative sentences; after does, use the base verb. Be is different: I am / you are / she is; Are you…?

FR : Le présent simple sert à décrire les habitudes, les responsabilités et les faits. « Does she work? » (pas « does she works »).

Rule 2 · Add useful details

Adjectives describe a noun and usually come before it: an important report, a busy day. No plural -s on adjectives.

Adverbs tell us how, how often or when: I usually check records. I speak slowly. Frequency adverbs generally come before the main verb but after be: I am often busy.

Connectors join ideas: and (addition), but (contrast), because (reason), so (result).

FR : adjectif avant le nom ; adverbe de fréquence avant le verbe principal ; « because » = parce que, « so » = donc.

A PRACTICAL COMMUNICATION TOOL

Ask questions, not just answer them

Three useful question patterns

Do + subject + verb?

Do you have the vehicle number?
Do you need more information?

Wh-word + do + subject + verb?

Where do you find the report?
When do you need it?

Can / Could + subject + verb?

Can you check the file?
Could you repeat that, please?

Polite upgrade: Could you please send me the reference number? You can use this even when you need time to find the right words.

FR : auxiliaire avant le sujet dans les questions. « Could you…? » est plus poli que « Can you…? ».

Small tools that make a big difference

in a department / at a company / on a project; in the morning / on Monday / at 9 o'clock.

08

ONE NEW ELEMENT AT A TIME

From a simple sentence to a confident introduction

Each step teaches one small rule. Practise it immediately, then use it to improve your own introduction. Nothing new appears without an example first.

01 · complete sentence02 · clear detail03 · frequency04 · reason05 · sequence06 · polite question
STEP 1

Start with “I + verb” and “I am + job”

Use the present simple for facts and regular responsibilities. I work (action) / I am a Fleet Accountant (identity). Negative: I don't manage every request; with be: I am not a manager.

« I work » = je travaille ; « I am » = je suis. Le présent simple convient à votre présentation et vos tâches habituelles.

CLEAR

I work as a Fleet Accountant.

STRONGER

I work as a Fleet Accountant, and I manage fleet information.

STEP 2

Add one detail: what, where, or who

What: vehicle records. Where: at an international company / in our software. Who: with colleagues. Use at + company, in + system / city, with + people. Add an adjective before a noun: an important report; international colleagues.

Ajoutez une seule précision. L’adjectif se place généralement avant le nom et reste invariable.

CLEAR

I check records.

STRONGER

I check important vehicle records in our system.

STEP 3

Explain how often you do something

Usually = habituellement, often = souvent, sometimes = parfois. Put the frequency adverb before the main verb: I sometimes use English. Put it after be: I am often busy. For how an action happens, use an adverb such as carefully or slowly.

Avec un verbe d’action : « I usually check ». Avec « be » : « I am usually busy ». « Carefully » = avec attention.

CLEAR

I use English software.

STRONGER

I sometimes use English-language software, and I check the data carefully.

STEP 4

Connect ideas and give a reason

and adds information; but contrasts; because explains a reason; so shows the result. Compare: I need English. → I need English because I communicate with international colleagues. A connector makes the message clearer, not automatically more advanced.

« because » = parce que ; « so » = donc. Évitez d’ajouter plusieurs connecteurs dans une seule phrase longue.

CLEAR

I want to improve my English.

STRONGER

I want to improve my English because I sometimes speak with colleagues abroad.

STEP 5

Describe a short work process

Use sequencing words: First (d’abord), then (ensuite), after that (après cela), finally (enfin). Use the present simple to describe a typical process, not something that happened yesterday.

Chaque mot de séquence indique l’ordre des actions. La liste décrit un exemple de processus à adapter à votre travail.

CLEAR

I open the software and check the request.

STRONGER

First, I open the software. Then, I check the request. Finally, I update the information.

STEP 6

Ask a clear question when you need help

Do + you + base verb? → Do you have the reference? Where + do + you + verb? → Where do you find the report? With be, reverse verb and subject: Is the report ready? To sound polite, use Could you + base verb, please? → Could you repeat that, please?

Avec un verbe d’action : « Do you…? ». Avec « be » : « Is it…? ». « Could you…? » donne une demande polie.

CLEAR

Do you have the vehicle reference?

STRONGER

Could you confirm the vehicle reference, please, so I can check the correct record?

YOUR REAL ENGLISH · FROM GOOD TO EXCELLENT

Your professional introduction · step by step

Start with what you already said, develop one skill at a time, then present your role naturally to colleagues, clients and suppliers.

YOUR STARTING POINT · already a great foundation
I work as a fleet accountant. My role involves speaking with colleagues, updating and downloading reports. I regularly check invoices. I also answer the phone and reply to e-mails. I also have to speak with our clients, suppliers and drivers. I handle the full procedure from ordering the vehicle to returning the vehicle.

Vous savez déjà expliquer vos responsabilités. Ici, nous allons relier les idées, enrichir le vocabulaire et améliorer la fluidité, sans vous concentrer sur les erreurs.

STAGE 1

Introduce your role

I work as a Fleet Accountant. Name your position clearly, then add one central responsibility.

I work as + métier ; I am responsible for + nom ou verbe en -ing.

CLEAR

I work as a Fleet Accountant.

DEVELOPING

I work as a Fleet Accountant, and I'm responsible for keeping vehicle records up to date.

STAGE 2

Make your responsibilities flow

Use and / also / in addition / as well as to add information. Avoid repeating I also in every sentence.

My role involves checking invoices and updating records.

Après involves et responsible for, utilisez la forme -ing : checking, updating, communicating.

Useful pattern: In addition to checking invoices, I also update vehicle records.

STAGE 3

Describe frequency and people

Every day / on a daily basis / regularly / when necessary show how often you do something. Add who you communicate with and why.

On a daily basis, I communicate with colleagues, clients, suppliers and drivers to coordinate vehicle-related information.

« On a daily basis » est une expression plus développée pour parler d'une routine professionnelle.

STAGE 4

Explain a process in order

First → Next → Then → Finally. For a concise presentation, use from … through to … to describe a complete process.

First, I follow the vehicle order. Then, I update the records and check the information. Finally, I help manage the vehicle's return.

More concise: I help coordinate the process from the initial vehicle order through to its return.

STAGE 5

Add professional impact

Give a reason with because or a purpose with to + verb. Use which helps... to show the result of your work.

I check invoices and update records carefully, which helps keep our information accurate and up to date.

« which helps » explique l'utilité de votre travail ; la phrase est plus professionnelle sans devenir compliquée.
