COMMUNICATION 01

Small talk that opens doors

Practise natural greetings, safe topics, follow-up questions and polite transitions.

TEACH · BEFORE PRACTICE

The four moves of a natural opening

1. Greet

Good morning. Nice to meet you.

Bonjour. Enchantée.

2. Ask

How has your week been?

Comment se passe votre semaine ?

3. Respond + detail

Quite busy, thanks. We have a new project.

Plutôt chargée, merci. Nous avons un nouveau projet.

4. Return / transition

And you? Shall we get started?

Et vous ? On commence ?

Natural rule: Answer + one detail + a question back. Avoid a single “Fine, thanks” every time.

Mini-dialogue · meeting an international colleague

Colleague: Hi Aurélie, how has your morning been?

You: Pretty good, thanks. I’ve been checking some reports. How about yours?

Colleague: Busy, but productive!

You: Glad to hear it. Shall we look at the latest fleet figures?

Present your company with confidence

REAL COMPANY INFORMATION

Learn the company before describing it

Fleets International Enterprises (FIE) is an independent fleet and mobility management services provider headquartered in Germany, with an international presence. It supports organisations with vehicle fleets and business mobility, including fleet management, invoice control, contract management, global reporting and strategic support. Its website describes dedicated account management and teams supporting operations, data/reporting, implementation and IT.

Source: FIE official website · About FIE. This is a learning summary, not a company-approved sales statement.

Une entreprise de gestion de flotte et de mobilité accompagne ses clients dans la gestion des véhicules, des données, des coûts et des processus associés.

Grammar · present simple for facts vs present continuous for current activity

Present simple · facts/routines

FIE provides fleet services. It supports international clients.

Negative: It does not manufacture cars.

Question: Does it provide reporting?

Present continuous · now/temporary

We are preparing a report today.

Negative: We are not updating that record now.

Question: Are you checking the invoice?

He/she/it + verbe -s au présent simple. À la forme négative ou interrogative : does / doesn’t + base verbale. Présent continu : am/is/are + -ing.

Company vocabulary: choose the right words

Three client-facing introductions

A2 · 20 seconds

We are an international fleet management company. We help companies manage their vehicles and reports.

B1 · 35 seconds

FIE is an independent fleet and mobility management company based in Germany. We support international clients with vehicle management, reporting and invoice control.

B1+ · 50 seconds

FIE provides independent fleet and mobility management services for international organisations. We help clients coordinate vehicle-related processes, manage information and gain a clearer view of their fleet through reporting and account management.

WHO WE ARE → WHAT WE OFFER → WHO WE HELP → WHY IT MATTERS. Example: We are an international fleet services provider. We help businesses manage vehicles, reports and processes so they have more accurate information.

Grammar · explaining relationships with who / which / that

The account manager is the person who coordinates client communication.
The report is a document that shows fleet data.
The platform is a tool which helps us manage records.

Who → personnes ; which → choses ; that → personnes ou choses (dans ces propositions relatives déterminatives).

Useful patterns

Our team works closely with the operations department.

I’m responsible for checking invoices, while another team handles technical requests.

COMMUNICATION 04 · EXPANDED WORKSHOP

Describe the atmosphere and share your views

Learn the words, build accurate sentences, connect your ideas and suggest improvements diplomatically.

YOUR LEARNING PATH

Describe → Explain → Contrast → Add an example → Suggest → Give a balanced opinion.

The workplace examples are models, not statements about your actual colleagues or procedures. Adapt them to your experience.

LESSON 01 / 08

01 · Atmosphere adjectives — describing the workplace

When and why · Rule

Adjectives describe a noun or follow a linking verb such as be, feel, seem. They do not change for plural nouns.

How to build it · Model

Before a noun: We have a supportive team. After be: The team is supportive.

Remember · Common mistake

Adjective order: a + adjective + noun; subject + be + adjective. Do not say “a team supportive”.

Vocabulary & expressions · English / Français

supportive = solidaire / bienveillant; collaborative = collaboratif; friendly = accueillant; welcoming = chaleureux; professional = professionnel; organised = organisé; structured = structuré; flexible = flexible; busy = chargé; fast-paced = au rythme soutenu; demanding = exigeant; positive = positif; international = international; efficient = efficace.

At work · Examples

The atmosphere is friendly and professional. / We work in a fast-paced environment. / My colleagues are helpful when I need to check an invoice.

LESSON 02 / 08

02 · Talking about preferences — enjoy, like, appreciate

When and why · Rule

Use enjoy / like / appreciate + noun or verb-ing to say what you enjoy. Would like + to + base verb expresses a wish, not a current preference.

How to build it · Model

I enjoy working with colleagues. / I appreciate the variety of tasks. / I would like to improve my English.

Remember · Common mistake

After enjoy, use -ing, NOT “to work”. After would like, use to + verb.

Vocabulary & expressions · English / Français

I enjoy… = J’aime…; I particularly appreciate… = J’apprécie particulièrement…; What I like most is… = Ce que j’aime le plus, c’est…; I find it rewarding to… = Je trouve gratifiant de…; I prefer… to… = Je préfère… à…

At work · Examples

I enjoy checking that our records are accurate. / What I appreciate most is the opportunity to work with international colleagues.

LESSON 03 / 08

03 · Giving a reason — because / because of

When and why · Rule

Because (parce que) introduces a complete clause: subject + verb. Because of (à cause de / en raison de) introduces a noun or -ing phrase.

How to build it · Model

I like my role because I work with different teams. / The report was delayed because of a system issue.

Remember · Common mistake

Because + subject + verb; because of + noun. Avoid “because of the system was slow”.

Vocabulary & expressions · English / Français

because = parce que; because of = à cause de / en raison de; since = puisque / étant donné que; as = comme / puisque (context-dependent); the reason is that… = la raison est que…

At work · Examples

I appreciate the process because it helps us avoid errors. / Because of the number of invoices, the checks take time.

LESSON 04 / 08

04 · Showing contrast — although / even though / however / but

When and why · Rule

Although (bien que / même si) joins two contrasting clauses in ONE sentence. However (cependant / toutefois) often introduces a new sentence, followed by a comma. But (mais) links contrasting ideas more simply.

How to build it · Model

Although the process is detailed, it is useful. / The process is useful. However, it takes time. / It is useful, but slow.

Remember · Common mistake

Although + subject + verb, main clause. Do not write “Although … but …” together for the same contrast.

Vocabulary & expressions · English / Français

although = bien que / même si; even though = même si (plus appuyé); however = cependant / toutefois; but = mais; nevertheless = néanmoins; on the other hand = d’un autre côté; whereas = tandis que / alors que.

At work · Examples

Although our work is sometimes demanding, the team is supportive. / The reporting system is reliable. However, some steps could be simpler.

LESSON 05 / 08

05 · Adding ideas and sequencing — also / in addition / for example / therefore

When and why · Rule

Connectors guide your listener: also adds information; in addition starts a further point; for example gives evidence; therefore / as a result introduces a consequence.

How to build it · Model

I also update vehicle records. / In addition, I check reports. / For example, I compare invoice details. / The information was incomplete. As a result, I contacted the team.

Remember · Common mistake

Also normally comes before the main verb but after be: “I also check” / “I am also responsible”. Put a comma after sentence-opening connectors.

Vocabulary & expressions · English / Français

also = aussi / également; in addition = de plus; furthermore = en outre; for example = par exemple; in particular = en particulier; therefore = par conséquent; as a result = en conséquence; first / then / finally = d’abord / ensuite / enfin.

At work · Examples

I check invoices and I also update records. In addition, I communicate with other departments. For example, I contact the reporting team when figures need clarification.

LESSON 06 / 08

06 · Polite possibilities — could / might / would

When and why · Rule

Could + base verb suggests a possible action. Might + base verb expresses a possibility. Would + base verb is useful in polite conditional suggestions.

How to build it · Model

We could create a checklist. / It might help the team. / It would be useful to review the procedure.

Remember · Common mistake

Modal + base verb: “could improve”, not “could to improve” or “could improves”.

Vocabulary & expressions · English / Français

could = pourrait / pourrions; might = pourrait / il se pourrait; would = conditionnel; perhaps = peut-être; it might be useful to… = il pourrait être utile de…

At work · Examples

Perhaps we could review the invoice checklist together. That might reduce repeated checks.

LESSON 07 / 08

07 · Making suggestions — should / shouldn’t / why not?

When and why · Rule

Should + base verb gives advice (devrait). Shouldn’t + base verb advises against an action (ne devrait pas). They can sound stronger than could in workplace discussions. Why not + base verb? is a friendly informal suggestion; Why don’t we + base verb? is collaborative.

How to build it · Model

We should check the figures before sending them. / We shouldn’t share unverified information. / Why not create a shared guide? / Why don’t we review the steps together?

Remember · Common mistake

No “to” after should or shouldn’t. “Why not + base verb?” not “Why not to create?” In formal feedback, “Perhaps we could…” is often softer.

Vocabulary & expressions · English / Français

should = devrait / devrions; shouldn’t = ne devrait pas; why not…? = pourquoi ne pas… ?; why don’t we…? = pourquoi ne pas… ensemble ?; how about + -ing? = et si on… ?; let’s + verb = faisons…

At work · Examples

We should keep the information accurate. We shouldn’t rush the final check. Why don’t we create a clearer procedure?

LESSON 08 / 08

08 · Expressing a balanced opinion

When and why · Rule

Build a professional response in four steps: opinion → reason → balanced contrast → constructive suggestion. Avoid presenting assumptions about the company as established facts.

How to build it · Model

In my view, the team works well together because colleagues share information. Although some checks take time, they help us maintain accuracy. Perhaps we could simplify one step.

Remember · Common mistake

“I think that…” is a complete introduction; “According to me” is less natural than “In my opinion” in this context.

Vocabulary & expressions · English / Français

in my opinion = à mon avis; from my point of view = de mon point de vue; I feel that… = j’ai le sentiment que…; one advantage is… = un avantage est…; one challenge is… = une difficulté est…; overall = dans l’ensemble; one possible improvement would be… = une amélioration possible serait…

At work · Examples

Overall, I enjoy the variety of responsibilities because I work with several teams. Although some reporting tasks are time-consuming, they are important. One possible improvement would be a shared checklist.

Speak naturally · Your complete answer

Use the model below to create a clear response. Each sentence has a job.

1 · Opinion

Overall, I find the atmosphere collaborative.

2 · Reason & example

I enjoy working with colleagues because we share information. For example, I can ask the reporting team for clarification.

3 · Contrast

Although some checks take time, they help us maintain accurate records.

4 · Suggestion

Perhaps we could create a shared checklist. It might help everyone work more efficiently.

A2

My team is friendly. I enjoy working with colleagues. Sometimes we are busy. We could use a checklist.

B1

I find the atmosphere supportive because my colleagues help each other. I also enjoy the variety of tasks. Although the work is sometimes busy, it is interesting. Perhaps we could make the reporting steps clearer.

B1+

Overall, I appreciate the collaborative atmosphere and the variety of responsibilities. For example, I regularly coordinate with colleagues to verify invoice information. Although certain procedures can be time-consuming, they are important for accuracy. One possible improvement would be to introduce a shared checklist, which might make communication more efficient.
