STEP 02 · NEWS FLASH · FACT-CHECKED CONTEXT

🗞️ France: what has been happening?

This is a short, simplified English news briefing, not a full newspaper article. The facts below concern the national mobilisations of September–November 2026. Your personal experience may have been different.

THE WEEK IN FRANCE

Workers, students and citizens have been protesting over several weeks

Demonstrations have focused on pay, the cost of living, public services, jobs, and resources for schools. Organisers and authorities sometimes describe the size and effects of demonstrations differently.

01💶

Pay & living costs

Unions called for higher wages and better protection of purchasing power. Everyday costs were a central concern.

salaires · pouvoir d’achat · coût de la vie

“People are asking for higher wages.”

02🏫

Education & public services

Students, teachers and other public-service workers raised concerns about funding, staffing and conditions.

éducation · moyens · services publics

“Some schools need more resources.”

03🚆

Transport & everyday life

Rail strikes and demonstrations disrupted some travel. Passengers and shopkeepers could be affected even if they did not take part.

perturbations · trains · commerçants

“Some trains were cancelled.”

04🤝

Dialogue & different views

Unions asked the government and employers to negotiate. Some people supported the protests; others worried about disruption or public safety.

négocier · revendications · sécurité

“People have different opinions.”

📅 29 SeptemberPublic-sector action
📅 17 / 24 / 31 OctoberMore mobilisations
📅 5 NovemberNational strike & protests
🧠 Your taskExplain the reasons in your own English

⚖️ Compare sources, not just numbers

For 5 November, the Interior Ministry reported approximately 575,000 participants. Unions reported more than 1.5 million. These are different organisations’ figures, not one agreed number. You can say: “According to the ministry… According to the unions…”

🛍️ Incidents are not the whole story

Authorities also reported violence and risks of looting around some demonstrations. Most reported marches were broadly peaceful. Shoplifting, vandalism and looting should be discussed as specific acts, not as behaviour shared by all protesters or young people.

STEP 04 · THE GRAMMAR NEWS DESK

🧠 Which tense helps you tell the story?

Learn each form separately: when to use it → how to form it → positive / negative / question → key words → exceptions → model answers. Read the models before opening the exercises.

YesterdayFor weeksNowAt that momentGeneral factsNext stepsReasons & opinions
01
YOUR MAIN TENSE

🕰️ Past Simple — what happened yesterday?

finished past action

Use: for a finished action at a finished time, especially yesterday, last week, on 5 November, two days ago.

✅ PositiveI attended a protest.People marched peacefully.I saw many banners.subject + past form (regular -ed or irregular)
🚫 NegativeI did not (didn’t) leave early.They didn’t agree.did not + base verb
❓ QuestionDid you take part?Why did you go?Did + subject + base verb?
⚠️ Important exceptions

go → went, see → saw, feel → felt, hear → heard, take → took. After did, always use the base verb: Did you go? (not Did you went?). With be, use was/were: Was it peaceful? (not Did it was peaceful?).

🗣️ Your A2 model

“Yesterday, I joined a demonstration. I saw many people, and I heard some slogans. The atmosphere was lively.”

02
NEW USEFUL CONNECTION

⏳ Present Perfect — what has happened recently?

past connected to now

Use: for a recent development or a period continuing up to now. Useful with recently, over the past few weeks, since September, for a month, so far.

✅ PositivePeople have protested recently.The issue has attracted attention.have/has + past participle
🚫 NegativeThey haven’t reached an agreement.have/has not + past participle
❓ QuestionHave you followed the news?Have/Has + subject + past participle?
⚠️ Past Simple or Present Perfect?

Yesterday is a finished time → “I went to a protest yesterday.” Over the past few weeks connects the past to now → “There have been several demonstrations.” Do not normally say I have gone to a protest yesterday. For = duration (for three weeks); since = starting point (since October).

🌱 Optional B1 stretch: Present Perfect Continuous

“People have been protesting for several weeks.” = an activity started in the past and has continued or recently continued. Form: have/has been + verb-ing. Learn this after you are comfortable with Present Perfect.

🗣️ Your A2+ model

“There have been several protests in France recently. People have asked for better wages and public services.”

03
WHAT IS HAPPENING

📍 Present Continuous — what is happening now?

now / temporary

Use: for action in progress now or around the present period. Time clues: now, at the moment, today, this week, currently.

✅ PositivePeople are discussing the news.am / is / are + verb-ing
🚫 NegativeThey aren’t marching now.am/is/are + not + verb-ing
❓ QuestionAre people still protesting?Am/Is/Are + subject + verb-ing?
⚠️ Spelling and special cases

make → making (drop silent -e); run → running (double consonant); lie → lying. Some state verbs are generally not used in the continuous: know, believe, want. Say “I believe people need change”, not I am believing….

🗣️ Your model

“People are talking about the demonstrations on the news today.”

04
SET THE SCENE

🎬 Past Continuous — what was happening when…?

background in the past

Use: to describe an action in progress at a moment in the past, or the background when another event happened.

✅ PositivePeople were chanting.I was listening to a speaker.was/were + verb-ing
🚫 NegativeThe crowd wasn’t moving.was/were not + verb-ing
❓ QuestionWhat were people doing?Was/Were + subject + verb-ing?
⚠️ Past Simple interrupts the background

“People were chanting when the march started.” Longer background: were chanting; completed event: started. While often introduces the continuing action; when often introduces the shorter event. This is a helpful pattern, not an absolute rule.

🗣️ Your A2+ model

“People were carrying banners, and some were singing. I was listening when the march began.”

05
GENERAL FACTS & OPINIONS

💬 Present Simple — what do you believe or know?

facts / views / habits

Use: to express an opinion, describe general facts or explain what people regularly do.

✅ PositiveI think wages are important.She works in a school.I/you/we/they + base verb; he/she/it + -s
🚫 NegativeI don’t agree with that idea.He doesn’t agree.don’t/doesn’t + base verb
❓ QuestionWhy do people protest?Does she support the strike?Do/Does + subject + base verb?
⚠️ Third person and "be"

She wants change, but Does she want change? (no -s after does). With be, ask “Are you concerned?” rather than Do you are concerned?.

🗣️ Your model

“I think public services are important because everyone needs schools and hospitals.”

06
LOOKING AHEAD

🔮 Going to / will — what could happen next?

plans / decisions

Use: going to for an intention or plan already decided. Use will for a spontaneous decision, offer or promise. Predictions can also use will, but keep that as a stretch.

✅ PositiveWe’re going to discuss this.I’ll ask a question.am/is/are going to + base; will + base
🚫 NegativeI’m not going to join tomorrow.I won’t forget the experience.not going to / won’t + base
❓ QuestionAre you going to go again?Will you call me later?Are/Is + subject + going to…? / Will + subject…?
⚠️ Remember the base verb

Say “I’m going to go”, not I’m going to went. Say “I will explain”, not I will explaining. With fixed arrangements, Present Continuous can also express the future: “We’re meeting on Friday at 7.”

🗣️ Your model

“Next week, I’m going to follow the news. I’ll ask questions if I don’t understand.”

07
MAKE YOUR ANSWERS STRONGER

🔗 Reasons, contrasts & calm opinions

clearer speaking
because + a full idea

“I went because I care about salaries.”

parce que + sujet + verbe
because of + noun

“The train was late because of the strike.”

à cause de + nom
but / however

“I support peaceful protests, but I worry about violence.”

mais / cependant
although (B1 stretch)

“Although the street was crowded, the atmosphere was calm.”

bien que / même si
⚠️ Common mistakes

“because people need change” ✅ / “because of the rising prices” ✅. Do not say because of people need change. In my opinion introduces a personal view; according to the report introduces an attributed claim. You can disagree politely without attacking anyone.

🗣️ Your A2+ model

“In my opinion, people should be able to protest peacefully. I understand their concerns, but I also understand why some shopkeepers feel worried.”

STEP 06 · LISTEN AND UNDERSTAND

🎧 Two short news dialogues

Listen once for the big idea, then listen again for the details. The dialogues are fictional teaching scripts based on realistic situations — not recordings of real protesters.

AUDIO REPORT 01 · PEOPLE & REASONS

🎙️ At the demonstration

One reporter talks with someone who attended the march.

Reporter: Why did you join the demonstration yesterday?

Participant: I went because I am worried about the cost of living. I want better wages and stronger public services.

Reporter: What was the atmosphere like?

Participant: At first, the crowd was calm. People were carrying banners and chanting. I felt hopeful, but the streets were very busy.

AUDIO REPORT 02 · TWO POINTS OF VIEW

🛍️ The local shopkeeper

A reporter asks a shopkeeper about the impact on the town.

Reporter: How did the demonstration affect your shop?

Shopkeeper: I understand why people want higher wages. But we closed early because the streets were blocked.

Reporter: Was your shop damaged?

Shopkeeper: No, it wasn’t. I heard reports of incidents in other places, but nothing happened here.

Reporter: What do you hope will happen next?

Shopkeeper: I hope everyone can discuss the problems peacefully.

STEP 07 · CONTROL THE CONVERSATION

🎙️ Become the interviewer

Choose a role for your teacher. You ask questions, listen to the answers, react naturally and ask a follow-up. All questions and model answers are visible.

💬 Keep talking naturally

  • “Really? What happened next?”
  • “Why do you think that?”
  • “Could you explain that?”
  • “How did you feel?”
  • “What do you hope will happen?”
  • “Thank you for speaking with me.”
📌 Question tip: Did → past. Do / does → habits/opinions. Have / has → recent experience. Are…going to → future plan.

🪜 Three model answers for your own experience

A2 · SAFE

“Yesterday, I went to a demonstration. I was there because I care about wages. I saw many people and banners. The atmosphere was lively.”

A2+ · TARGET

“Yesterday, I took part in a demonstration because I wanted to support better working conditions. People were chanting and carrying signs. I felt hopeful, although it was crowded.”

B1 · STRETCH

“Over the past few weeks, people have been expressing concerns about wages and public services. I joined yesterday’s demonstration to make my views heard. In my experience, the atmosphere was mostly peaceful, but I know that other people may have had different experiences.”

These are language models, not statements about what you actually saw or believe. Adapt them to your own experience.
