    Step 3 · The key to all conjugation

    Pronoun power: who is doing the action?

    Before you choose a verb form, always ask yourself: who is the subject? In English, he / she / it is the “special” group. It changes the verb in the present simple, the present continuous and the present perfect.

    Avant de choisir la forme du verbe, demandez-vous toujours : qui est le sujet ? He / she / it est le groupe « spécial ».

    👤 One person = he / she

    my son → hemy daughter → shemy neighbour → he / sheClaire → she

    Une personne = he (il) / she (elle).

    🐶 One thing / animal = it

    the house → itthe dog → itthe weather → itthe campervan → it

    Une chose ou un animal = it.

    👨‍👩‍👧 Many = we / they

    my children → theymy family and I → wethe neighbours → theymy friends → they

    Plusieurs personnes ou choses = we (nous) / they (ils, elles).

    ⚠️ “My family” is one group → it / she-form: My family lives near the sea. But “my children” is plural → My children live in Nantes.
    « My family » = un groupe (verbe avec -s). « My children » = pluriel (pas de -s).

    🏡 Present simple: habits and facts

    Use it for routines, habits, general truths and permanent situations.

    Pour les habitudes, les routines, les faits et les situations permanentes.

    Keywords: every day, usually, always, often, sometimes, never, on Mondays, at the weekend, in general

    ➕ The he / she / it rule

    he / she / it + verb-s
    • work → she works
    • watch, wash, go, do, fix → +es (watches, goes)
    • study, try → ies (studies) · play → plays
    • have → has

    ➖ ❓ Negatives and questions

    I / you / we / they don’t + verb · Do you…?

    ⚠️ The -s moves to does: She doesn’t works. Does she works?

    🧱 Structure

    I am + verb-ing
    he / she / it is + verb-ing
    you / we / they are + verb-ing

    Le verbe « be » change selon le sujet, le verbe prend -ing.

    ✏️ -ing spelling

    • cook → cooking
    • make, write → making, writing (lose the -e)
    • sit, swim, run → sitting, swimming, running
    • lie → lying
    Subject➕ Affirmative➖ Negative❓ Question
    II’m cooking.I’m not cooking.Am I cooking?

    ⚠️ Some verbs are almost never in -ing: like, love, hate, want, need, know, understand, believe. Say “I know”, not “I’m knowing”.

    🕰️ Past simple: finished actions

    Use it for finished actions at a finished time.

    Pour une action terminée à un moment terminé.

    Keywords: yesterday, last week, last month, two days ago, in 2020, when I was young, during the trip

    😌 Good news!

    The past simple is the same for all pronouns: I worked, she worked, they worked.

    🧭 Present perfect: experience & result now

    Use it for life experience (no date) and for a past action with a result now.

    Pour l’expérience de vie (sans date) ou un résultat visible maintenant.

    Keywords: ever, never, already, just, yet, before, since, for

    🔔 he / she / it again!

    I / you / we / they have + past participle
    he / she / it has + past participle

    She has never been to Scotland. Has he ever driven a campervan?

    ⚠️ Present perfect or past simple?

    A finished time (yesterday, last month, in 2019) → past simple.

    No time given, life experience → present perfect.

    ✅ I have been to Brittany. ✅ I went to Brittany last month.

    ❌ Last month, I have been…

    Step 5 · New tool

    Phrasal verbs for everyday life

    A phrasal verb = verb + small word (up, out, back, off, after…). The small word changes the meaning. English speakers use them all the time, so understanding them helps you a lot in listening.

    Un phrasal verb = verbe + particule. La particule change le sens. Très fréquent à l’oral.

    1 · The meaning changes

    look = regarder

    look after = s’occuper de

    look for = chercher

    look forward to = avoir hâte de

    2 · Conjugate only the verb

    The small word never changes. The verb follows all the normal rules:

    • She gets up at 7. (he/she/it -s)
    • She got up late yesterday.
    • She’s getting up now.
    • Does she get up early?

    3 · Where does “it” go?

    With many phrasal verbs, a pronoun goes in the middle:

    ✅ Turn off the TV. ✅ Turn the TV off.

    ✅ Turn it off. ❌ Turn off it.

    Avec un pronom (it, them, her…), il se place au milieu.
