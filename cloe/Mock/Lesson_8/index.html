What you need to know

Do not use the same strategy for every question. First recognise the domain: word meaning, grammar structure, natural expression, written evidence, or spoken evidence. Then apply the right technique.

VOCABULARYMeaning + collocation

Look around the gap. Which word naturally belongs with the surrounding words?

GRAMMARSignal + structure

Find the time marker, subject, auxiliary, preposition or sentence pattern.

EXPRESSIONSSituation + register

Ask what the speaker is trying to do: request, clarify, apologise, disagree, confirm…

READINGQuestion + evidence

Read the command, scan for proof, and avoid answers that are merely plausible.

LISTENINGTarget + change

Know what to catch before you play: number, reason, action, correction, opinion or condition.

One exam routine for every question.

Use this six-step routine to stay calm when the task type changes.

time marker · tone · keyword · question word

meaning? form? purpose? detail? action?

Does it fit naturally and completely?
C

Look for one last trap.

negative · tense · false friend · changed detail
H

Hit & move

Answer and release the question.

Do not spend the next question thinking about the last one.
SWITCHING EXAMPLE

Three questions. Three different brains.

1 · “We need to ___ the deadline.”
Vocabulary brain → collocation → meet the deadline.

2 · “By Friday, we ___ the report.”
Grammar brain → by + future completion → will have finished.

3 · “I understand your point, ___ I have some concerns.”
Expression brain → polite disagreement → but.

En français : avant de chercher la réponse, demandez-vous toujours « quelle compétence cette question teste-t-elle ? ». Cela évite de traduire mot à mot et réduit les erreurs d'inattention.
