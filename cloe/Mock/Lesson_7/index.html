02 · S.P.E.A.K. METHOD · 8 MIN

Five moves to prevent short, blocked answers.

Use the same structure whether you are introducing yourself, handling a role-play or discussing a professional topic.

S

Situation

Understand exactly what is happening.

Who? Why? What changed? What does the examiner need?
P

Point

Answer the question directly first.

Give your main message before adding detail.
E

Expand

Add a reason, detail or consequence.

because · so · which means · the main reason is…
A

Add

Add an example, action or alternative.

For example… · One option would be… · In my experience…
K

Keep moving

Return the conversation to the other person.

Ask a question, confirm, clarify or invite a response.
COACH THINKING · FROM SHORT TO STRONG

Question: “What do you do?”

Too short: “I work in customer service.”

S.P.E.A.K.: “I work in customer service for a medium-sized company. I mainly deal with client requests and solve delivery or billing problems. For example, I often coordinate with our logistics team when an order is delayed. I also follow up with customers to make sure the issue has been resolved.”

Need repetition?

“Could you repeat the last part, please?”

Need clarification?

“Do you mean…?” / “If I understand correctly…”

Need thinking time?

“That’s an interesting point. I’d say…”

Need to repair?

“Let me rephrase that.” / “What I mean is…”

S.P.E.A.K. IN 20 SECONDS

Situation → Point → Expand → Add → Keep moving

Situation: understand the role, problem and objective.

Point: give a direct answer or response.

Expand: explain why / what happened / what matters.

Add: give an example, action, option or consequence.

Keep moving: ask, confirm, clarify or invite the other person to react.
