02 · R.E.A.C.T. METHOD · 9 MIN

Five checks before you click.

Translation alone is risky. Use context to decide what the speaker is trying to do and how formal the phrase should be.

R

Read

Read the whole situation, including who is speaking to whom.

client → supplier · colleague → colleague · manager → team
E

Effect

Identify the communication goal: request, clarify, reassure, disagree, follow up…

What should this phrase do?
A

Appropriacy

Choose the right level of politeness and professionalism.

“Send it.” ✗ → “Could you send it, please?” ✓
C

Chunk

Look for a natural fixed chunk or collocation rather than translating word by word.

keep me posted · bear in mind · get back to
T

Test

Read the complete sentence once. Does it sound natural and match the intention?

Meaning + register + grammar must all work.
COACH THINKING · MODEL

A client gives a date you did not hear clearly. What do you say?

RRead: professional call with a client.
EEffect: ask for repetition.
AAppropriacy: polite, not abrupt.
CChunk: “I didn’t quite catch that.”
TTest: “I’m sorry, I didn’t quite catch that. Could you repeat the date?”
TRAP 01

Literal French

Prefer a real English chunk: I’ll get back to you, not a word-for-word translation of “je reviens vers vous”.

TRAP 02

Correct but too direct

“I disagree” may be grammatically correct but I see your point, but… can be more appropriate professionally.

TRAP 03

Wrong collocation

English chunks are fixed: meet a deadline, raise an issue, take responsibility.

TRAP 04

Similar meaning, wrong function

Would you mind…? requests action; Would you like…? offers something.

EXPRESSION SUCCESS STRATEGY

How to choose the phrase without translating every word

  1. Identify the relationship.A client, colleague, manager and close teammate may require different levels of formality.
  2. Name the function.Are you requesting, clarifying, disagreeing, suggesting, apologising, following up or closing?
  3. Think in chunks.Remember complete phrases such as “Would you mind…?”, “Just to clarify…”, “I’ll get back to you…” and “I see your point, but…”.
  4. Check register.The most direct phrase is not always the most appropriate professional answer.
  5. Read it aloud in your head.If the complete exchange sounds natural and achieves the goal, validate and move on.
