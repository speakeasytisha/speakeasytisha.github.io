The 3-sentence KPI formula

04 · BLOCK 2 · TODAY'S ACCOMPLISHMENT

Turn sticky notes into clear sentences.

Sticky notes are short and often have no verb. When you speak, add we + a verb in the past simple.

Les post-it sont courts, souvent sans verbe. À l'oral, ajoutez « we » + un verbe au prétérit.

Quick rule · past simple for today's finished actions

regular: close → closed · fix → fixed · train → trained · reduce → reduced

irregular: do → did · make → made · get → got · find → found · send → sent

Pronunciation tip: closed = /kləʊzd/ (1 syllable) · fixed = /fɪkst/ · trained = /treɪnd/ — don't add an extra “-ed” syllable.

Attention à la prononciation : « closed » = une seule syllabe.

Sticky note → spoken sentence

3 NNC items closed ✔
New operator trained – inspection
Conveyor jam fixed 10:30
Backlog cleared – initial handling

Choose the correct verb form

1. Today we ___ the rework on batch 12.

2. This morning we ___ the drawings to engineering.

3. We ___ to reduce scrap by 50 pieces.

4. Which is the most useful way to present 6 sticky notes?

06 · BLOCK 4 · SUPPORT NEEDED

Ask for help — clearly and politely.

American colleagues expect a request that is direct (what, from whom, by when) and polite. Too vague = nothing happens. Too blunt = it sounds like an order.

Les collègues américains attendent une demande directe (quoi, de qui, pour quand) et polie. Trop vague : rien ne bouge. Trop sec : cela ressemble à un ordre.

CLEARWe need support from [team] to [verb]…

“…from IT to repair two scanners.”

POLITECould we get… ? / It would help if…

“Could we get an extra inspector on Thursday?”

DECISIONWe need a decision on…

“…on which batch to prioritise.”

NOTHINGNo support needed today.

Always say it — never skip the block.

Choose the best request for a daily meeting

1. Two scanners are broken.

2. You need an extra inspector on Thursday.

3. Engineering has not sent a drawing for three days.

4. Everything is fine today.

08 · VOICE & DELIVERY

Silence is your most powerful word.

Great presenters do three things with their voice: they pause, they stress the key word, and they let their voice fall at the end of a statement. That is what makes a message sound confident and memorable.

Les grands présentateurs font trois choses : ils marquent des pauses, ils accentuent le mot clé, et leur voix descend en fin de phrase. C'est ce qui rend un message assuré et mémorable.

/ short pause (½ sec) // long pause (1–2 sec) BIG stress this word ↘ voice goes down 👀 look at the audience 👉 point at the slide 🙂 smile

Same sentence · two deliveries

In four months / we've cut processing time / from around ten / to just over SIX. // That's REAL progress. ↘

Listen to both. Which one gives the audience time to understand — and to feel that it matters?

Écoutez les deux versions. Laquelle laisse au public le temps de comprendre — et de ressentir l'importance du message ?

1

Pause in chunks

Pause after each group of meaning — never in the middle of one. Pause before a key number, and after a key message.

Pause entre les groupes de sens, jamais au milieu. Pause avant un chiffre clé, et après un message clé.

2

Stress one word

In each sentence, choose one word that carries the message. Say it a little louder, longer and higher.

Dans chaque phrase, choisissez un seul mot porteur du message et appuyez-le.

3

Land the ending ↘

For a statement, let your voice go down at the end. It sounds sure and finished. Then pause.

Pour une affirmation, laissez la voix descendre à la fin : cela sonne sûr et terminé.

09 · CONNECT & CONVINCE

Facts inform. Stories convince.

You already have the strongest possible material: real results and real expertise. These six techniques turn your data into a message people remember — and act on.

Vous avez déjà le meilleur matériau possible : des résultats réels et une vraie expertise. Ces six techniques transforment vos données en un message dont on se souvient — et qui fait agir.

1 · HOOK

Start with one number

“Let me start with one number: six.”

Commencez par un seul chiffre fort.

2 · WE

Talk about people

“The team cut processing time” — not “processing time was reduced”.

Parlez des personnes, pas seulement des chiffres.

3 · SO WHAT?

Say why it matters

“What does that mean? // It means parts move faster to the customer.”

Dites toujours pourquoi c'est important.

4 · RULE OF THREE

Group ideas in threes

“Where we are, what we've learned, and where we're going.”

Le cerveau retient facilement trois éléments.

5 · CONTRAST

Before → after

“In June, rework was 76. / Today, it's 37.”

Le contraste avant/après rend le progrès visible.

6 · EMOTION

Show how you feel

“I'm genuinely proud of this.” · “I'm excited about what's next.”

Le public américain attend de l'enthousiasme et de l'engagement personnel.

10 · PRESENCE & CUES

Your body says it before you do.

Six simple habits that make an audience trust you. You will find their cue icons in the performance scripts.

Six habitudes simples qui inspirent confiance au public. Leurs icônes apparaissent dans les scripts de présentation.

🧍

Plant & breathe

Stand still, feet apart. Take one breath before the first word.

Ancrez-vous, respirez avant le premier mot.

👀

One sentence, one person

Look at one person for a full sentence, then move to another.

Une phrase = une personne regardée.

👉

Point, turn, talk

Point at the chart, turn back to the audience, then speak.

Montrez le graphique, revenez vers le public, puis parlez.

🤲

Open hands

Hands visible, at waist height. Use them to count “one, two, three”.

Mains visibles ; comptez les points sur vos doigts.

🙂

Smile on good news

Your face should match your words: good results = a real smile.

Votre visage doit refléter vos mots.

⏸️

Pause & look

After the key message: stop, look at the room, count to two.

Après le message clé : arrêtez-vous, regardez la salle, comptez jusqu'à deux.

14 · BEFORE, DURING THE BREAKS, AFTER

The conversation around the presentation.

With American colleagues, relationships are often built in the five minutes before the meeting, at the coffee break, and at lunch. The recipe is simple: answer + one detail + a question back.

Avec des collègues américains, la relation se construit souvent dans les cinq minutes avant la réunion, à la pause café et au déjeuner. La recette : réponse + un détail + une question en retour.

ANSWER“Pretty good, thanks!”

Short and positive.

+ ONE DETAIL“A little jet-lagged, but excited to be here.”

Gives them something to react to.

+ QUESTION BACK“How about you? Busy week?”

Keeps the conversation alive.

15 · AFTER THE UPDATE

Short questions, short answers.

In a daily meeting, answers are short: answer → one reason → next step. If you don't know, say so and promise a follow-up.

En réunion quotidienne, les réponses sont courtes : réponse → une raison → prochaine étape. Si vous ne savez pas, dites-le et promettez un retour.

Q1

“Why did you miss the target today?”

Model

“Mainly because of the scanner issue this morning — we lost about an hour. It's fixed now, so we expect to be back on track tomorrow.”

Q2

“What's the root cause?”

Model

“We don't know yet. We're looking into it with the workshop, and I'll give you an update at tomorrow's meeting.”

Q3

“When will it be closed?”

Model

“We're aiming for Thursday, provided we get the drawing from engineering by tomorrow noon.”

Q4

“What do you need from me?”

Model

“A quick decision on the rework priority would really help — ideally today, so the team can plan tomorrow's shift.”

Q5

“Sorry, can you repeat the scrap number?”

Model

“Sure. Scrap was one hundred and fifty pieces today — one-five-zero — which is in line with the estimate.”

Q6

(You didn't understand the question.)

Model

“Sorry, could you say that again? / Just to make sure I understand — are you asking about scrap or about picks per hour?”

Useful buying-time phrases: “Good question.”“Let me check and come back to you.”“Just to clarify…”“I'll follow up after the meeting.”
