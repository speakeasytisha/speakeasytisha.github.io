Four blocks.
One story people remember.

During the October work week, you will use the same four-part structure every day. The goal is not to read a board. The goal is to give the room a clear message: where the team stands, what changed today, what comes next and what support will help.

02 · CAPTURE ATTENTION FIRST

Give the conclusion before the detail.

A short daily update becomes much more engaging when the first sentence tells the room the story. The charts then become proof, not the presentation.

Un point quotidien est beaucoup plus engageant si la première phrase donne immédiatement le message. Les graphiques deviennent alors une preuve, pas le discours.

FACT-BY-FACT

Harder to follow

“Picks per hour were 25, then 25, then 30, then 40, then 50…”

The audience has to decide what the numbers mean.
STORY-FIRST

Easier to remember

“The main story today is that productivity has moved much closer to target. Let me show you the two numbers behind that.”

You give the meaning first, then support it.
POSITIVE MOVEMENT“The main story today is that…”

…performance moved in the right direction.

ON TARGET“The key point today is that…”

…we reached / held the target.

MIXED RESULT“We made progress on X, but…”

…Y remains the main focus.

BLOCKER“The main issue today is…”

…and the support we need is very specific.

Build your opening headline

Frame
Message
Clear model

“The main story today is that our productivity indicator is moving much closer to target. I’ll show you where we are, what the team accomplished today, what we’ll focus on tomorrow and the support that would help us keep the momentum.”

Stronger / more natural model

“Today’s update is encouraging: we’ve moved the KPI in the right direction and we’ve learned something useful about the next constraint. I’ll keep this short — where we are now, what changed today, what we’re doing tomorrow and where we could use support.”

03 · BLOCK 1 · WHERE ARE WE?

Present KPIs as a trend + meaning.

The audience rarely needs every point on the graph. Give the direction, one or two useful numbers, the comparison with target and the meaning.

THE 4-MOVE KPI STORY

  1. Headline: “Productivity is improving.”
  2. Evidence: “We moved from 25 to 50 picks per hour.”
  3. Target: “That puts us at the current target, although the stretch goal is higher.”
  4. Meaning: “The priority now is to make that performance repeatable.”
move closer to targetreach the targetremain below targethold steadyimprove steadilylevel offclose the gapmaintain the gainshow a clear trendbe on track

GRAMMAR CHOICE

Present simple vs present perfect

Present simple: describe what the chart shows now.
“The chart shows a steady improvement.”

Present perfect: describe progress from an earlier point up to now.
“We have moved from 25 to 50 picks per hour.”

Useful contrast:
“We’ve reached the target, but we still need to prove that the result is stable.”

“Scrap has fallen significantly, while productivity has improved.”

Guided KPI language

1. Productivity ___ significantly since the start of the week.

2. The chart ___ a steady increase in picks per hour.

3. Which line tells a stronger story than simply saying “We are at 50”?

4. Choose the natural trend sentence.

04 · BLOCK 2 · WHAT CHANGED TODAY?

Turn activities into accomplishments.

“We had a meeting” is an activity. “We aligned on the root-cause categories” is an accomplishment. Give the result of the work, not only the work itself.

“Nous avons eu une réunion” décrit une activité. “Nous nous sommes alignés sur les catégories de causes” décrit un résultat.

ACTIVITY → RESULT → MEANING

Activity: “We reviewed ten cases.”

Result: “We identified two recurring delay patterns.”

Meaning: “That gives us a much clearer focus for tomorrow.”

Natural daily structure:
“Today we completed X, which allowed us to Y. The useful takeaway is Z.”

COMPLETE

completed · finished · closed out · validated

DISCOVER

identified · confirmed · found · uncovered

ALIGN

agreed on · aligned on · clarified · prioritised

IMPROVE

reduced · simplified · removed · stabilised

PAST SIMPLE

Finished action + finished time

“We reviewed the cases this morning.”

“We identified two issues during the workshop.”

PRESENT PERFECT
Use it to emphasise the result that matters now:
“We have identified two recurring patterns, so tomorrow we can focus the analysis.”

Conjugation & meaning

1. We ___ the first test this morning.

2. We ___ two recurring delay patterns, so we now have a clearer focus.

3. The process ___ since the first trial.

4. Choose the correct sentence.

5. Which version turns an activity into a meaningful accomplishment?

05 · BLOCK 3 · WHAT HAPPENS NEXT?

Turn tomorrow’s list into a priority.

Do not read every sticky note. Tell the room what the team will focus on first, why that matters and what success will look like.

PRIORITY → ACTION → PURPOSE

Priority: “Tomorrow, the first priority is to validate the main assumption.”

Action: “We’ll run two targeted checks.”

Purpose: “That should tell us whether the change is robust enough to standardise.”

WILL

Clear commitment

“We’ll test the new sequence tomorrow.”

Useful for a decision / commitment.
BE GOING TO

Planned action

“We’re going to review the three highest-delay cases.”

Useful when the plan is already organised.
THE PRIORITY IS TO

Executive focus

“The priority is to validate the change before we scale it.”

Useful when you want to avoid a task list.

06 · BLOCK 4 · WHAT DO YOU NEED FROM THE ROOM?

Make support requests specific and actionable.

“We need support” is difficult to act on. Name the person, information, decision or resource that would unlock the next step.

CONTEXT → ASK → IMPACT

Context: “To complete tomorrow’s test…”

Ask: “…we need confirmation from quality before 10 a.m.”

Impact: “That will let us keep the test sequence on schedule.”

DIRECT

“We need confirmation on…”

COLLABORATIVE

“What would help us most is…”

SOFTER

“We could use some support with…”

CONDITIONAL

“If we can get X by 10 a.m., we can complete Y.”

07 · SOUND NATURAL BETWEEN THE BLOCKS

Transitions create the feeling of one conversation.

Use several versions so the daily update does not sound memorised. The function stays the same even when the wording changes.

Variez les formulations tout en gardant la même fonction : cela rendra le point quotidien plus naturel.

OPEN → KPI

“Let me start with where we are on the two main indicators.”

“First, a quick look at the numbers that matter.”

“To set the scene, here’s where the KPI stands today.”

KPI → TODAY

“So what helped move that number today?”

“Behind that result, the team completed three useful actions.”

“That brings me to what we accomplished today.”

TODAY → TOMORROW

“There is one area where support would help us move faster.”

Finish cleanly

“So the takeaway is simple…”

“That’s where we are today. What questions do you have?”

“That’s the update. Is there anything you’d like me to clarify?”

09 · MAKE THE DELIVERY SOUND ALIVE

Use your voice to show what matters.

A natural update has contrast: short headline, small pause, evidence, another pause, then the implication. You do not need to speak faster. You need to make the hierarchy audible.

Pour paraître naturel, faites entendre la hiérarchie : message, pause, preuve, pause, conséquence.

1

Headline

Stress the message.
“The MAIN STORY today is that productivity is now AT TARGET.”

2

Pause

Give the room one beat to absorb the message before the numbers.

3

Evidence

Say only the useful figures. Slow down slightly on the numbers.

4

Meaning

Change tone slightly: “What that means for tomorrow is…”

Version that sounds like a report

“Picks per hour are 50. Scrap is 129. Today we completed three actions. Tomorrow we have four actions. We need support.”

Version that sounds like a story

“The main story today is that productivity has reached the current target, while scrap has also moved in the right direction. Behind that improvement, we completed three actions that clarified the next constraint. So tomorrow, the priority is to validate that finding. To stay on schedule, we need one confirmation from quality before the first test.”

CHUNKING PRACTICE

Listen for thought groups, not individual words.

“The main story today / is that productivity has reached the target. // Behind that result / the team completed three actions / and identified one remaining constraint. // So tomorrow / the priority is to validate that point. // To stay on schedule / we need one decision from the room.”
