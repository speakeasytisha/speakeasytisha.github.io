Étape 1 · Retrieval practice

Start with what you already know.

Before adding new language, reactivate the tools from Lesson 1. The goal is automatic recall, not memorisation.

Avant d'ajouter du nouveau vocabulaire, réactivez les outils de la Leçon 1. L'objectif est de créer des automatismes, pas de réciter.

1

Buy time

You need two seconds to think.

“That’s a good question. Let me think for a second.”

2

Reformulate

Your first sentence is not clear.

“What I mean is…” / “Let me put it another way.”

3

Highlight the point

You want the listener to remember one idea.

“The main point is…” / “What matters most is…”

30-second speaking activation

Tell me one thing you remember from Lesson 1 and one situation where you could use it at work.

Expliquez une chose que vous retenez de la Leçon 1 et une situation professionnelle dans laquelle vous pourriez l'utiliser.

Étape 3 · Grammar that changes your message

Choose the tense according to the time frame.

Pour présenter un projet ou un graphique, Thomas n'a pas besoin de réciter tous les temps. Il doit surtout savoir choisir rapidement le bon temps selon l'information qu'il donne.

FACT / ROUTINE

Present simple

Use it for stable facts, routines and what a graph represents.

Subject + base verb / -s

“This chart shows our monthly output.”

Ce graphique montre notre production mensuelle.

Typical keywords / clues
usuallyoftenalwaysnormallygenerallyevery weekeach month
Graph clues: shows · represents · indicates
NOW / CURRENT TREND

Present continuous

Use it for something happening now or developing around now.

am / is / are + verb-ing

“Demand is increasing at the moment.”

La demande est en train d'augmenter actuellement.

Typical keywords / clues
nowright nowat the momentcurrentlythis weekthese days
Look for a change developing around now.
FINISHED PAST

Past simple

Use it with a completed period or a specific past date.

verb + -ed / irregular form

“Output fell slightly in March.”

La production a légèrement baissé en mars.

Typical keywords / clues
yesterdaylast weeklast monthin Marchin 2025two days agowhen…
Finished time = past simple.
PAST → NOW

Present perfect

Use it for change or progress from a past point up to now.

have / has + past participle

“We have improved our output by 18%.”

Nous avons amélioré notre production de 18 % jusqu'à présent.

Typical keywords / clues
sinceforso farup to nowrecentlyalreadyyetover the last…
Past + connection to now = present perfect.
Important: keywords are clues, not magic rules.

Always ask: Is the time finished? Is it happening now? Does it connect the past to now?

Posez-vous toujours la question : la période est-elle terminée ? Est-ce en cours maintenant ? Y a-t-il un lien entre le passé et maintenant ?

FINISHED TIMElast month / in March / yesterday→ Past simple
PAST → NOWsince January / so far / over the last six months→ Present perfect

Speaking rule

Look at the graph → choose 3 cue words → look back at the listener. The goal is communication, not reading.

Étape 7 · Interaction

You do not need an instant perfect answer. You need a structure.

Pour Thomas, l'objectif est de réduire les hésitations lorsqu'une question imprévue arrive. Chaque réponse suit : acknowledge → think → answer → check.

WHY?

“Why did the figures drop in March?”

“There were two main reasons. First,… Second,… Overall,…”
Supported · A2+/B1-

“The figures dropped because we had a supply problem and a delay. The problem was temporary, and the results improved in April.”

Natural · B1

“There were two main reasons. First, we had a temporary supply issue. Second, one stage of the process took longer than expected. Overall, the impact was limited because performance recovered in April.”

Stronger · B1+

“There were two main factors behind the March dip. First, we experienced a temporary supply issue, and second, one validation stage took longer than planned. Fortunately, neither issue was structural, so the figures recovered quickly once those constraints were resolved.”

NEXT STEP?

“What happens next?”

“The next step is to… After that, we’ll…”
Supported · A2+/B1-

“Next, we need to check the remaining items. Then we will update the figures and prepare the next review.”

Natural · B1

“The next step is to validate the remaining items. After that, we’ll update the figures and prepare the final version for the next review.”

Stronger · B1+

“Our immediate priority is to validate the outstanding items. Once that is done, we’ll consolidate the revised figures, confirm the final assumptions and circulate an updated version before the next review.”

CLARIFY

“Can you explain what you mean by ‘recovery’?”

“Of course. What I mean is…”
Supported · A2+/B1-

“Recovery means the figures went up again after the drop.”

Natural · B1

“Of course. What I mean is that the figures improved again after the temporary drop. In March they fell slightly, but they increased again in April.”

Stronger · B1+

“Of course. By ‘recovery’, I mean that performance returned to a positive trend after the temporary March decline. The index moved from 47 in March to 63 in April and continued to rise afterwards.”

DON'T KNOW

“Do you have the exact figure for July?”

“I don’t have the exact figure in front of me, but…”
Supported · A2+/B1-

“I don’t have the exact figure now. I can check it and send it after the meeting.”

Natural · B1

“I don’t have the exact figure in front of me, but I can check it and send it to you after the meeting.”

Stronger · B1+

“I don’t want to give you an inaccurate number, so I’d prefer to verify it. I can check the July figure immediately after the meeting and send you the confirmed value.”
