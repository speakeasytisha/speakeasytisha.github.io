01 · DO NOT MIX THE OLD AND NEW STORY

The critical change is P90.

The updated deck states a P90 NNC lead time of 288 hours / 12 days with an objective of 48 hours / 2 days. Do not call this an “average” when you present the updated slide.

La nouvelle diapositive parle du délai P90 et non d'une moyenne. Cette distinction est essentielle à l'oral.

CURRENT288 h12 days · P90 NNC lead time
→
OBJECTIVE48 h2 days
Meaning first.

In standard statistical usage, P90 means the 90th percentile. If that is also your company's approved definition, you can explain it as: “90% of cases are at or below this lead time.” If your internal definition differs, use the approved wording.

Quick update check

1. Which opening is accurate for the updated KPI?

Look at the exact KPI label, not only the number.

2. Which sentence gives the target clearly?

Use from + starting value + to + target value.

3. What consequence does the problem statement highlight?

Focus on the operational consequence, not the presentation format.

06 · PROJECT TIMELINE

Use the right tense for completed and upcoming milestones.

The deck gives five milestones: kick-off, define, measure/analyse, improve/Shinweek, and control at +30 / +60 / +90 days.

30 Jul 2026Definecompleted milestone

TENSE MAP

Past → scheduled → future follow-up

Completed: “The kick-off took place on 26 June.”

Scheduled: “Measure and Analyse are scheduled for 30 September.”

Future: “We will follow up at +30, +60 and +90 days.”

Avoid: “We do the kick-off the 26 June.”

Prefer: “The kick-off took place on 26 June.”

Avoid: “We are improve in 16 October.”

Prefer: “The Improve / Shinweek phase is scheduled for 16 October.”

Timeline language

1. The kick-off ___ 26 June 2026.

Completed date → past simple + on.

2. The Improve / Shinweek phase ___ 16 October.

Planned milestone → is scheduled for.

3. Which closing is most natural?

Use a future verb and say what will be reviewed.

11 · UNEXPECTED QUESTIONS

Answer what you know. Protect what you do not know.

Use: acknowledge → answer from the slide → state the limit → next step.

Q1

“What exactly does P90 mean?”

Model

“In standard statistical terms, P90 is the 90th percentile, so 90 percent of cases are at or below that value. I would use our approved internal definition in the meeting if the company calculates or labels it differently.”

Q2

“Why is the target specifically 48 hours?”

Model

“The slide sets the target at 48 hours. It does not give the rationale for selecting that number, so I would avoid inventing one. I can confirm the project rationale separately if needed.”

Q3

“Why are GE Engineering analysis cases out of scope?”

Model

“The deck explicitly excludes GE Engineering analysis / CSC cases and indicates that they represent around two percent. The slide does not state the detailed reason for the exclusion, so I would give only the approved project rationale rather than speculate.”

Q4

“Where is the biggest bottleneck today?”

Model

“The current-state map gives us visibility of the handoffs and shows where measurements are known or still missing. I would wait for the complete timing data before naming the main bottleneck. The next step is to close those measurement gaps and quantify the delay by step.”

Q5

“How will you know the Shinweek worked?”

Model

“The target slide gives us the primary KPI: P90 NNC lead time, starting at 288 hours with a target of 48 hours. It also includes Day 1 to Day 4, result and percentage-change fields, so the improvement can be tracked during the event and then controlled at +30, +60 and +90 days.”

Q6

“What is your main message in one sentence?”

Model

“We need to reduce the P90 NNC lead time from 12 days to 2 days by understanding the end-to-end process, measuring where time is lost and focusing improvement on the largest verified contributors.”
