01 · RETRIEVAL

Describe first. Interpret second.

Your first job is still to report what the graph actually shows. Keep description separate from explanation.

Première étape : décrire uniquement ce que montre le graphique. L'interprétation vient ensuite.

Illustrative training dataComponent temperature · °C

Training scenario only — figures are illustrative and do not represent programme data.

30-SECOND ORAL WARM-UP

What do you see?

Use at least three graph phrases: increase, gradually, from…to…, overall, approximately.

B1 Support model

The temperature increases from about 705°C at T1 to about 742°C at T5. The increase is gradual at first and becomes stronger after T3.

B2 Stretch model

Overall, the graph shows a sustained upward trend. The increase is relatively gradual through the first three test points, before becoming more pronounced between T3 and T5, where the value reaches just over 740°C.

04 · LANGUAGE CONTROL

Explain relationships without mixing the structures.

Repère utile : because + phrase complète ; because of / due to + nom ; result in = produire une conséquence ; result from = provenir d'une cause.

CAUSE

because of + noun

“The temperature increased because of the operating condition.”

CAUSE

because + clause

“The temperature increased because the operating condition changed.”

RESULT

lead to / result in + noun

“Higher loading may lead to faster degradation.”

SOURCE

result from + noun

“The variation may result from measurement uncertainty.”

Auto-graded · 10 pointsChoose the precise structure

1. The increase may be ___ the revised operating condition.

2. The value changed because ___ the flow rate increased.

3. Higher thermal loading could ___ faster degradation.

4. The variation may ___ sensor tolerance.

5. The geometry change ___ a small efficiency gain.

6. One possible explanation ___ the new test condition.

7. The pressure loss decreased ___ the design update.

8. We cannot say the update caused the result ___ we only have one test point.

9. The higher value may be ___ measurement uncertainty.

10. If confirmed, the change could ___ a reduction in margin.

05 · GRAMMAR THAT CHANGES THE ENGINEERING MESSAGE

Do not sound more certain than the evidence.

Modals are not decoration. They tell your audience how strong the evidence is.

Le choix du modal change le niveau de certitude : may/might/could = possibilité ; is likely to = probabilité ; must = obligation forte.

LOW / POSSIBLEmight / may / could

“This may be linked to the operating condition.”

PROBABLEis likely to / probably

“The change is likely to affect the margin.”

STRONG EVIDENCEshows / confirms

“The repeated tests confirm the trend.”

RECOMMENDATIONshould / need to / must

“We should investigate.” · “We must remain within the limit.”

Auto-graded · 10 pointsChoose the safest professional wording

1. You have one abnormal test result. Which statement is best?

2. Several repeated tests show the same trend.

3. You recommend further investigation, but it is not a safety requirement.

4. A hard operating limit cannot be exceeded.

5. You think a hypothesis is plausible but unconfirmed.

6. The evidence suggests a future risk, but no failure has occurred.

7. You do not have enough evidence for a final conclusion.

8. You expect a result based on current evidence.

9. You want to avoid speculation.

10. The next test is necessary to verify repeatability.

06 · PRECISION LAB

Separate what you know, think and need to confirm.

Distinguez toujours ce qui est mesuré, ce qui est une hypothèse et ce qu'il reste à vérifier.

WHAT WE KNOW

Evidence

Use measured facts and observed results.

The test shows… / We observed…
WHAT WE THINK

Hypothesis

Use cautious interpretation.

This may suggest… / One possible explanation is…
WHAT WE NEED

Verification

State the missing evidence or next test.

We need to confirm… / Further testing is required…

Classify each statement: Evidence, Hypothesis or Verification.

1. The latest test shows a 6°C increase.
2. The increase may be related to the revised condition.
3. We need another run to determine whether the result is repeatable.
4. One possible explanation is measurement uncertainty.
5. Configuration B has lower pressure loss in all three runs.

10 · HANDLE THE QUESTION, NOT THE PRESSURE

Keep control of technical Q&A.

En questions-réponses, vous pouvez ralentir, reformuler la question et revenir aux données vérifiées au lieu d'improviser un chiffre.

CLARIFY“If I understand your question correctly…”

Check what the person is really asking.

BUY TIME“That’s a good question. Let me think for a moment.”

Two seconds can improve your answer.

ANCHOR“Based on the current data…”

Return to evidence.

LIMIT“We have not investigated that point yet.”

Say what is outside the current scope.

FOLLOW-UP“I can check that and come back to you.”

Close the gap professionally.

CHALLENGE“That’s a valid concern. What we are seeing so far is…”

Acknowledge, then respond.

FULL MODEL · ADAPTABLE TRAINING SCRIPT

Two levels for the same 5-minute structure.

Use the structure, not the confidential details.

B1 Support · 5-minute model

Opening. Good morning, and thank you for joining. Today I’d like to give you a short update on the latest technical results, explain the main point we are watching, and finish with the actions we propose for the next step. I will first give you the context, then show the key result, and finally explain why it matters for the programme.

Context and data. We have completed a new test sequence to compare the current baseline with an updated operating condition. The objective was to check whether the change gives us the expected performance benefit while keeping the other important parameters within an acceptable range. Overall, the main performance indicator is moving in the expected direction, and the result is encouraging. We also see lower pressure loss than in the baseline condition. However, one parameter needs more attention. The monitored component temperature is higher than the baseline, especially in the final part of the sequence. The increase is progressive rather than sudden, and the difference becomes more visible at the higher test points.

Interpretation and impact. At the moment, we do not know the exact cause. One possible explanation is the revised operating condition, but we need more evidence before we can confirm this. It is also possible that part of the difference comes from normal test variation or measurement uncertainty. What we can say with confidence is that the temperature trend is different from the baseline and that it needs to be understood. If the increase is repeatable, it could reduce the available durability margin. So this is not a final conclusion and it does not mean that the configuration is unacceptable. It means that we have identified a technical point that must be checked before we make the final decision.

Next steps. Our next step is to repeat the test under controlled conditions and review the related parameters. We also want to compare the result with the previous configuration under equivalent conditions so that we can separate the effect of the operating condition from normal test variation. In addition, we will check whether the same trend appears in the other thermal indicators. If the result is confirmed, we will then assess the impact on durability margin and decide whether an optimisation is necessary. If the result is not repeated, we will review the measurement and test conditions before taking any design action.

Close. To summarise, the overall performance result is encouraging, but we have one technical point to confirm before making a final decision. The priority now is to verify the temperature trend, understand the cause and quantify the impact. We have a clear verification plan, and the next test should give us the evidence we need to move forward. Thank you. I’m happy to take your questions.

B2 Stretch · 5-minute model

Opening. Good morning, everyone. I’d like to use the next few minutes to walk you through the latest technical results, highlight one point that deserves particular attention, and explain how we propose to address it. I’ll start with the objective of the test sequence, then focus on the main result and its implications, and finish with the verification plan and the decision we expect to make afterwards.

Context and data. The latest test sequence was designed to compare the baseline condition with an updated operating point and to determine whether the expected performance benefit could be achieved without creating an unacceptable penalty elsewhere. From a performance perspective, the results are broadly encouraging: the target indicator shows a measurable improvement, pressure loss is lower than the baseline, and the direction is consistent across the completed runs. The main point I’d like to highlight, however, is the component temperature. Relative to the baseline, we see a progressive increase, with the largest deviation occurring toward the end of the sequence. The change is not isolated to a single sample, but the current data set is still too limited for us to describe the effect as fully established.

Interpretation and impact. At this stage, the data suggests a relationship with the revised operating condition, but I would be cautious about describing that as a confirmed cause. We still need to separate a genuine configuration effect from normal test-to-test variation and measurement uncertainty. We also need to check whether the correlated thermal parameters move in the same direction. If the trend is confirmed, the potential impact is primarily on durability margin rather than immediate performance. In other words, we may be gaining performance while consuming margin elsewhere, and that trade-off needs to be understood before final selection. The result therefore changes the level of confidence in the configuration, but it does not yet justify a design change by itself.

Next steps. For that reason, I recommend three actions. First, reproduce the condition in the next controlled run to confirm repeatability. Second, review the correlated thermal and aerodynamic parameters to identify whether they support the same hypothesis and to determine whether the temperature increase is local or part of a broader shift. Third, compare the result directly with the baseline and the previous configuration under equivalent conditions. In parallel, we should review the remaining durability evidence so that the performance benefit can be evaluated against the available margin rather than in isolation. If the next run confirms the trend, we will quantify the impact and assess whether optimisation is required. If it does not, we will focus on test variability and measurement effects before changing the design.

Decision path. The important point is that the next decision is evidence-based. We are not selecting or rejecting the configuration today. We are defining the additional evidence needed to close the uncertainty. Once that evidence is available, we should be able to make a much more robust trade-off between performance, thermal behaviour and durability.

Close. To summarise, the current data supports the intended performance direction, but the temperature trend remains an open technical point. We understand what needs to be verified, we have a clear plan for the next test, and that work should allow us to reduce the uncertainty significantly before the next programme decision. Thank you. I’m happy to discuss the details and take any questions.
