01 · Retrieval first

Speak before you study.

You have 60 seconds. Do not write full sentences first. Use only cue words.

Vous avez 60 secondes. N'écrivez pas de phrases complètes d'abord. Utilisez uniquement des mots-clés.

60-second engineering update

“What are you working on at the moment?”

Include: programme → component → current priority → challenge → next step.

01:00
Compare after speaking

Three levels of support

Le but est de comparer votre structure, pas de mémoriser le modèle.

Supported · B1-

“I’m working on a high-pressure turbine nozzle programme. My team is reviewing technical data and open points. Our priority is to stay on schedule. We still have some issues to close. The next step is to prepare the next review.”

Natural · B1/B1+

“I’m currently working on a programme involving the high-pressure turbine nozzle. My team is coordinating technical activities and reviewing the latest design data. One of our main priorities is to close the remaining technical points without affecting the schedule. The next step is to consolidate the data before the international review.”

Stronger · B2 bridge

“At the moment, I’m leading work on a high-pressure turbine nozzle programme. I’m coordinating several technical workstreams, reviewing design maturity and making sure open issues are resolved before they become schedule risks. Our immediate priority is to align the latest technical definition with the validation plan so we can go into the next international review with a clear status and a realistic action plan.”

04 · Next grammar step

Use the passive when the process matters more than the person.

Engineering updates often focus on the component, the test, the drawing or the issue — not on who performed the action.

Dans les mises à jour techniques, on met souvent l'accent sur le composant, le test, le plan ou le problème — pas sur la personne qui effectue l'action.

FACT / PROCESS

Present passive

is / are + past participle

“The flow is directed through the nozzle guide vanes.”

Le flux est dirigé à travers les aubes directrices.

NOW

Present continuous passive

is / are being + past participle

“The latest drawing is being reviewed.”

Le dernier plan est en cours de révision.

PROGRESS

Present perfect passive

has / have been + past participle

“Three action items have been closed.”

Trois actions ont été clôturées.

FINISHED

Past passive

was / were + past participle

“The issue was identified during the review.”

Le problème a été identifié pendant la revue.

Quick decision rule

Use active voice when the owner matters: “The Mexico team will run the test.” Use passive voice when the result/process matters: “The test will be run in Mexico.”

Utilisez la voix active quand le responsable est important. Utilisez la voix passive quand le résultat ou le processus est plus important.

05 · Technical speaking

Explain the HPT nozzle at two levels of detail.

Strong international engineers adjust the explanation to the audience. Precision is useful; unnecessary jargon is not.

Un bon ingénieur international adapte son explication à l'interlocuteur. La précision est utile ; le jargon inutile ne l'est pas.

→
2HPT nozzle / nozzle guide vanesstationary vanes guide the hot gas flow
→
3High-pressure turbinerotor blades extract energy

Simplified training explanation. Use only the level of technical detail that is appropriate and accurate for the real programme.

Audience A · Project colleague

Target: 45 seconds. Explain where the component is, what it does and why it matters.

located downstream ofstationary vanesguide the flowhigh temperatureperformance / durability

“The high-pressure turbine nozzle is located just downstream of the combustion chamber. It is made up of stationary nozzle guide vanes that control the direction of the hot gas flow before it reaches the first turbine rotor stage. Because the component operates in a very high-temperature environment, its geometry, cooling and material condition are important for both performance and durability.”

Audience B · Senior non-specialist

Target: 30 seconds. Keep the function, remove the unnecessary detail.

part of the hot sectioncontrols gas flowhelps turbine work efficientlyhigh-temperature environment

“It’s a stationary component in the hot section of the engine. Its main role is to control how the hot gas is directed into the high-pressure turbine. In simple terms, it helps the turbine receive the flow in the right direction and at the right conditions, so it has a direct impact on efficiency and durability.”

Fluency challenge

Explain it once with the technical term. Then explain it again without saying “nozzle guide vane”. Describe the function instead.

06 · October presentation skill

Don’t read a graph. Tell its story.

A professional graph commentary answers five questions: What are we looking at? What is the overall trend? What changed? Why does it matter? What do you need from the audience?

07 · American English under pressure

Listen for the message behind the details.

This one is intentionally harder. First listen for gist. Second listen for actions, dates and implications. Use the transcript only after your second attempt.

Cet exercice est volontairement plus difficile. Première écoute : idée générale. Deuxième écoute : actions, dates et implications. Le transcript vient après.

US project update · Two passes

Capture decisions, conditions and risk — not every word.

“Quick update before we move on. We closed three of the five open action items from last week, but the thermal review has slipped from Tuesday to Thursday because the revised analysis came in late. The Mexico team can still support the validation run, provided they receive the updated drawing package by three p.m. Central tomorrow. One more thing: the latest supplier data shows a one-point-eight percent variation, which is not a stop-work issue, but I do want engineering to confirm the trend before Friday. For the kickoff deck, please don’t add another ten slides. Keep the main chart, show the movement since June, and make the decision we need from leadership explicit. If anyone sees a risk to that plan, flag it today rather than waiting for the Thursday review.”

Full model answer · approx. 5 minutes

A complete October kickoff model

Ce modèle est volontairement plus développé. Il sert à observer la structure, les transitions et le langage des graphiques — pas à être appris par cœur.

Open the full model answer
0:00–0:30

Opening & purpose

“Good morning everyone, and thank you for joining. Today I’d like to give you a concise update on the high-pressure turbine nozzle programme. I’ll start with a quick reminder of the programme context, then I’ll explain the main technical focus, walk you through the latest readiness chart, and finish with the remaining risks, actions and the alignment we need from the group.”

0:30–1:15

Programme context

“The programme focuses on the high-pressure turbine nozzle, which is part of the hot section of the engine and is located downstream of the combustion chamber. The nozzle guide vanes are stationary components that control the direction of the hot gas flow before it reaches the high-pressure turbine rotor. My role is to coordinate the technical workstreams, make sure the teams stay aligned, and help close open points before they become schedule or validation risks. We are working across several sites, so clear communication and action ownership are especially important.”

1:15–2:10

Technical focus

“From a technical perspective, the key point is that this component operates in a very demanding thermal environment. That means the geometry, material condition and cooling strategy all have to remain consistent with the technical requirements. At the moment, the latest drawing package is being reviewed and the remaining analysis points are being consolidated. Several actions have already been closed, but we still need to confirm the final definition before the next validation step. I don’t want to go too far into the design detail here, but the important message is that the technical work is progressing and the remaining items are well identified.”

2:10–3:20

Chart story

“If you look at this chart, you can see how programme readiness has evolved since June. Overall, the trend is clearly positive. Readiness increased from 61 percent in June to 68 percent in July and 75 percent in August. By September, we had reached 82 percent, which was still three percentage points below our 85 percent target. Since then, we have closed several open actions and the October figure has moved to 89 percent. So the key takeaway is that the gap has now been closed and we are slightly above target. I would not say that this means all the risk has disappeared, but it does show that the actions taken over the last few weeks are having the expected effect.”

3:20–4:20

Risks, actions & global collaboration

“We still have a small number of technical actions to close. The main risk is not simply the number of open items; it is the dependency between the updated drawing package, the remaining analysis and the validation schedule. The next step is to close the outstanding analysis points, release the updated package and make sure each site has the same technical baseline. The Mexico team can support the validation activity, but they need the latest package on time. We also need to keep the U.S. and India teams aligned on the review sequence and on who owns each action. To avoid misunderstandings, every critical action should have a named owner, a clear due date and an explicit dependency.”

4:20–5:00

Close & decision request

“To sum up, the programme is moving in the right direction. The technical definition is becoming more mature, readiness has improved steadily, and we are now slightly above the October target. The remaining risks are manageable, provided we keep the current actions on schedule and maintain good coordination between the different sites. What I need from the group today is confirmation that we keep the current review plan and that the remaining priorities are aligned across all teams. If everyone agrees, we’ll continue with that plan and circulate the updated action list after the meeting. Thank you. I’m happy to take your questions.”
