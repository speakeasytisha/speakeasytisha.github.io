BEFORE YOU CHOOSE · WEATHER IN NEW YORK

What's the weather going to be like?

Learn the language of weather before deciding which attraction fits the day. This lesson uses practice forecasts, not live weather data. For real travel dates, consult an updated forecast.

Three different questions
  • What's the weather like? = Quel temps fait-il ? (general conditions)
  • What's the forecast for Wednesday? = Quelles sont les prévisions pour mercredi ? (future)
  • What will the temperature be? = Quelle température fera-t-il ? (degrees)

Plan by location and time — not just by day

Each attraction shows its neighbourhood, nearby sights, approximate time needed and suggested time of day. Pair convenient locations (for example Grand Central + SUMMIT; or the High Line + Chelsea Market + Little Island). Visiting durations are planning estimates; actual tickets, operating hours and transport should be checked on official websites.

Keep in mind: the Statue of Liberty and Ellis Island may take much of the day. Avoid automatically fitting another long museum visit into the same morning.

Change your plan · guided conversation

1 · Original

We were planning to visit the museum on Tuesday morning.

2 · Reason

But the weather forecast looks better for a walk.

3 · Alternative

Could we move the museum to Thursday afternoon instead?

4 · Replacement

We could explore the High Line on Tuesday morning.

PART 5 · REAL-LIFE ENGLISH

From booking to the visit

Every situation follows the journey: booking → arriving → presenting tickets → following instructions → solving a problem. Learn the useful phrases before replying.

Questions worth asking · natural spoken English

  • Before booking: “Are there tickets available for Friday?” · “Does the ticket include both islands?”
  • On the way: “Where do we catch the ferry?” · “Is this the right entrance?”
  • At the entrance: “We have tickets for 11. Here they are.”
  • If you need help: “Sorry, could you say that again?” · “Could you check our booking, please?”

À l'oral, privilégiez les questions simples et naturelles. Les expressions techniques figurant sur les billets servent surtout à comprendre les documents.

PART 6 · WRITING & SPEAKING MISSIONS

Real situations, real messages

Before writing, learn who you are addressing, how to write a subject line, how to ask politely and how to close your message.

Who are you writing to?

  • Known professional name: Dear Ms Johnson, / Dear Mr Brown,
  • First-name contact: Hello Alex, (if they introduce themselves that way)
  • No name available: Hello, / Dear Reservations Team, / Dear Visitor Services Team, / Dear Customer Service Team,
  • Friend: Hi Emma, / Hi everyone,

Le registre dépend du destinataire : équipe d'accueil, réservation, relation client ou ami.

Mr, Mrs, Miss, Ms · cultural note

  • Mr + surname: man; does not indicate marital status.
  • Mrs + surname: married woman or someone who chooses this title.
  • Miss + surname: traditionally unmarried woman; often avoided unless requested.
  • Ms + surname: neutral and usually safest professional choice for a woman when her preference is unknown.
  • Mx + surname: gender-neutral title, when preferred.

En cas de doute, utilisez le titre et le nom choisis par la personne, ou « Hello » + prénom connu ; n'inventez pas un titre.

Build a complete email · six small steps

  1. Subject: “Question about museum tickets” / “Request to change our booking” / “Accessibility information for our visit”
  2. Greeting: “Dear Visitor Services Team,” / “Hello,”
  3. Purpose: “I am writing to ask about our visit on 12 May.”
  4. Details: “We have two tickets for 11 a.m., booking number ABC123.”
  5. Questions: “Could you confirm where we should enter?” / “Is it possible to change the time?” / “What are our options?”
  6. Closing: “Thank you for your help. Kind regards,” / “Thank you in advance. Best regards,” / “Many thanks,”

Objet précis → formule d'appel → motif → détails → questions concrètes → remerciement et signature. Ne mélangez pas un courriel professionnel et un message amical.
