02 · GRAMMAR WIN #1

Imperatives: the grammar of directions

A direction normally begins with the base verb. No subject is necessary.

Learn → practise
BASE VERB+DETAIL
Go straight for two blocks.Allez / continuez tout droit sur deux pâtés de maisons.
Turn left at the lights.Tournez à gauche au feu.
Take the second street on the right.Prenez la deuxième rue à droite.
Cross the street.Traversez la rue.
Walk past the bank.Passez devant la banque.
Get off at 59th Street.Descendez à la 59e rue.
Negative direction: Don't + base verb → Don't cross here. Don't take the express train.

USE IT IN NEW YORK

From Fifth Avenue to the café

Build a three-step route using go / turn / walk past / take.

Model · Everyday natural

Go straight for one block, turn right at the lights, and walk past the hotel. The café is on your left.

Model · Polished but usable

Go straight for one block, then turn right at the traffic lights. Walk past the hotel, and you’ll see the café on your left.

03 · GRAMMAR WIN #2

AT, ON, IN + place relationships

These small words are what make directions precise.

Learn → practise

AT = A POINT

at the corner

Use at for a precise point or meeting place.

at the station · at the entrance · at the corner

ON = A STREET / SURFACE

on 42nd Street

Use on with streets and avenues in American English.

on Fifth Avenue · on Broadway

IN = AN AREA

in Manhattan

Use in for cities, neighborhoods and larger areas.

in Midtown · in New York · in Central Park

04 · GRAMMAR WIN #3

Direct questions vs. polite indirect questions

This is a major grammar rule you can reuse everywhere.

Learn → practise

DIRECT QUESTION

Where is the subway entrance?

Question order: question word + verb/auxiliary + subject.

Où est l’entrée du métro ?

POLITE INDIRECT QUESTION

Could you tell me where the subway entrance is?

Important: after the polite opening, return to statement word order.

Pourriez-vous me dire où se trouve l’entrée du métro ?

Common trap ✗ Could you tell me where is the subway entrance? ✓ Could you tell me where the subway entrance is?

05 · GRAMMAR WIN #4

CAN, COULD, SHOULD, MAY — what changes?

Choose the modal according to the job it is doing.

Clear grammar
CAN

Ability / possibility / normal request

Can I take the A train from here?

Est-ce que je peux prendre le A d’ici ?Very normal everyday English.
COULD

Softer / more polite request

Could you show me on the map?

Pourriez-vous me montrer sur la carte ?More softened than can, but still completely natural.
SHOULD

Advice / best choice

Should I take the local or the express?

Est-ce que je devrais prendre l’omnibus ou l’express ?Ask what is recommended.
MAY

Formal permission / possibility

May I leave my bag here?

Puis-je laisser mon sac ici ?Correct and more formal. Less necessary for ordinary street directions.
Rule: modal + subject + base verb → Can I take…? Could you help…? Should we change…? May I leave…?
Never: ✗ Could you to help…?

06 · GRAMMAR WIN #5

Questions across time: now, before and next

You do not need every tense today. You need to recognize which helper builds the question.

Question toolkit
MeaningGrammarNew York exampleFrench
Routine / factdo / does + base verbDoes this train stop at 42nd Street?Est-ce que ce métro s’arrête à la 42e rue ?
Happening nowam / is / are + -ingIs this train going uptown?Ce métro va-t-il vers Uptown ?
Pastdid + base verbDid we miss our stop?Est-ce qu’on a raté notre arrêt ?
Experience / result nowhave / has + past participleHave we passed 42nd Street?Est-ce qu’on a déjà dépassé la 42e rue ?
Futurewill + base verbWill this bus stop near Grand Central?Est-ce que ce bus s’arrêtera près de Grand Central ?
Useful technique: find the time first → choose the helper → keep the main verb simple.

12 · ONE CERTIFICATION BRIDGE

One short task. Nothing new.

Listen → reformulate → ask one clarification question.

8 min
“Walk two blocks along 45th Street, turn left on Sixth Avenue, and the entrance is across from the bank.”

Then reformulate the directions and ask one checking question.

Model · Everyday natural

So I go along 45th Street for two blocks, then turn left on Sixth Avenue. The entrance is across from the bank. Just to make sure, is it on the left side of Sixth Avenue?

Model · Polished but usable

So, if I understood correctly, I should continue along 45th Street for two blocks, turn left on Sixth Avenue, and the entrance will be across from the bank. Could you tell me which side of Sixth Avenue it’s on?
