TODAY'S BIG IDEA

“Natural” ≠ “slang”

Natural American English is often friendly, direct and efficient. It uses short polite openers and softeners without sounding formal.

Neutral everyday Hi, could you help me? Warmer Hi! How are you doing? More formal Good evening. We have a reservation. Too casual for some strangers Hey, what's up?

01 · WARM-UP

Which New York feels most like you?

See the question and hear it. Then compare how the same idea changes in formal, polite everyday, casual and slang-heavy English.

15 min · speaking + register

CENTRAL PARK · BOW BRIDGE

Quiet + beautiful

Question: Would a peaceful morning around Bow Bridge appeal to you? Explain what attracts you, what you would do there, and how long you would want to stay.

Formal

I would be particularly drawn to this part of Central Park because it offers a quieter contrast to the energy of Manhattan. I would probably spend a couple of hours walking, taking photographs and simply enjoying the scenery.

Polite everyday

I'd really like this because it looks peaceful and beautiful. I'd probably walk around, take some photos and stay for a couple of hours without trying to rush through the park.

Casual

I'd love this. I'd just wander around, take a few pictures and enjoy having a quieter morning before heading back into the city.

Slang / very casual

Honestly, this is totally my kind of place. I'd just hang out, walk around and take it easy for a while.

TIMES SQUARE · MIDTOWN

Bright + intense

Question: Do you think you would enjoy Times Square, or would you find it too crowded and touristy? Give a balanced opinion.

Formal

I would certainly want to experience Times Square once because it is so iconic. However, I suspect I would find the crowds and noise rather overwhelming after a short time.

Polite everyday

I'd definitely like to see it, especially at night, but I don't think I'd want to stay there for hours because it looks extremely busy.

Casual

I'd check it out for sure, but after twenty or thirty minutes I'd probably be ready to go somewhere less hectic.

Slang / very casual

I'd totally go once for the lights, but if it's crazy packed, I'd be out pretty fast.

BROOKLYN · SKYLINE AT DUSK

Skyline + atmosphere

Question: What would make a Brooklyn sunset unforgettable for you? Describe the atmosphere, what you would do, and the kind of conversation you might have.

Formal

The combination of the skyline, the river and the evening light would make the experience especially memorable. I would enjoy walking slowly and perhaps asking a local where they would recommend going afterwards.

Polite everyday

I think the view would be amazing at sunset. I'd love to walk by the water, take photos and maybe ask someone for a good place to have dinner nearby.

Casual

I'd just want to walk around, enjoy the view and find somewhere good to eat afterwards. That sounds like a perfect evening to me.

Slang / very casual

That view would be insane. I'd grab some photos, chill by the water and then find a great spot to eat.

Important: slang is mainly for recognition and selective use. Your safest travel default is the polite everyday version.

02 · CULTURAL DECODER

“Hi” is not as casual as it looks on a French translation

Translations such as salut can be misleading. Register depends on situation, tone and relationship—not one dictionary equivalent.

15 min · pragmatics
HI!

In everyday U.S. interactions, “Hi” is normal, warm and often perfectly polite.

A hotel receptionist, server, shop assistant or stranger can all hear “Hi” without it sounding childish or overly familiar. “Hey” is more casual and is also used to get attention, so it can feel less neutral with a stranger.

Key idea: do not translate the word first. Ask: Who am I talking to? What is the situation? How warm or formal do I want to sound?

03 · WHY IT SOUNDS “TOO POLITE”

Friendly + efficient: the logic behind the phrases

American travel/service interactions often combine a quick friendly opener with a clear request. The politeness is real—but usually compact.

10 min · culture + speech
1

Open

Hi / Excuse me

You acknowledge the person before asking.
2

Ask clearly

Could you help me with…?

Direct does not have to mean rude.
3

Give one detail

I'm trying to get to…

Make the request easy to answer.
4

Close warmly

Perfect, thank you so much.

Short, friendly, finished.
The cultural point: Americans often try not to bother other people or impose on their time.

That is different from saying that Americans “don't like to be bothered.” The speaker is often showing consideration: Excuse me, Sorry to bother you, Could I ask you something?, or When you have a moment… signals: “I know I'm taking a little of your time, and I want to be respectful.” You do not need to over-apologize; one short softener is enough.

“Where is the subway?”

→

NATURAL EVERYDAY

“Excuse me, is there a subway entrance around here?”

→

EXTRA SOFT

“Sorry to bother you—do you know where the nearest subway entrance is?”

04 · MODAL LAB

Can, could, should, may — what changes?

These tiny words change meaning, politeness and intention. Learn the rule first, then use them in real New York situations.

18 min · grammar → speaking
Core rule

modal + base verb: can help, could tell, should take, may enter. No to after the modal, and no -s for he/she/it.

Question: modal + subject + base verb → Could you help me? · Negative: can't / couldn't / shouldn't / may not.

CAN

pouvoir · capacité / possibilité / demande directe

Everyday request: “Can you tell me which train I need?”

Meaning: normal, direct and friendly. Not rude when your tone is polite.

🇫🇷 Vous pouvez me dire quel train je dois prendre ?

COULD

pouvoir · demande plus douce / possibilité / capacité passée

Polite request: “Could you tell me where the entrance is?”

Meaning: softer than can. In a polite request, could is not simply past tense.

🇫🇷 Pourriez-vous me dire où se trouve l'entrée ?

SHOULD

devrais / devrait · conseil / recommandation / attente

Advice: “Which station should I get off at?”

Meaning: asks what is advisable or the best choice—not a strong obligation.

🇫🇷 À quelle station devrais-je descendre ?

MAY

pouvoir · permission / possibilité · registre plus formel

Formal permission: “May I leave my coat here?”

Possibility: “It may rain later.”

Meaning: correct and polished, but often more formal than you need in an ordinary travel exchange.

🇫🇷 Puis-je laisser mon manteau ici ? / Il se peut qu'il pleuve.

❌ Could you to tell me?✅ Could you tell me?

11 · LILATE BRIDGE

Interpret, compare, react, reformulate

The travel topic is enjoyable, but the language skills underneath are certification skills.

20 min · exam transfer

TASK A · INTERPRET

Explain a review in your own words

A traveler says an attraction has incredible views but feels crowded and expensive. Explain the message without translating word for word.

Use: overall opinion → positive point → negative point → recommendation.
Model · Level 1

The traveler thinks the view is excellent, but the place can be very crowded and the tickets are expensive. Overall, they still think it is worth visiting.

Model · Level 2

Overall, the traveler is positive about the experience, especially the views, but they also point out two drawbacks: the crowds and the price. Their message is not “don't go”; it is more “go if the view matters enough to you, and choose your time carefully.”

TASK B · COMPARE

Choose between two experiences

Compare a peaceful morning in Central Park with a busy evening in Times Square. Give advantages, disadvantages and your preference.

Model · Level 1

Central Park is calmer and more relaxing, while Times Square is much busier and more exciting. I would prefer Central Park in the morning because I like walking and beautiful scenery, but I would still visit Times Square once at night.

Model · Level 2

The two experiences offer completely different versions of New York. Central Park gives you space, nature and a slower rhythm, whereas Times Square is about spectacle, crowds and sensory overload. Personally, I'd spend more time in Central Park, but I think Times Square is worth seeing once because it captures a very recognizable side of the city.

TASK C · REFORMULATE

Make the request sound more natural

Original: “Where is subway for Brooklyn?”

Model · Level 1

Excuse me, which subway should I take to get to Brooklyn?

Model · Level 2

Excuse me, could you tell me the easiest subway route to Brooklyn from here?

12 · FINAL MISSION

One perfect New York day — but talk like a traveler, not a brochure

Use your real preferences, review language, and the right register for each encounter.

10 min · synthesis

Your plan

  1. Choose one major sight.
  2. Choose one neighborhood / walking moment.
  3. Choose one meal style: sophisticated, casual, or quick.
  4. Prepare one street interaction.
  5. Prepare one opinion based on a review.
Model · Level 1

My perfect day would start in Central Park because I like places that are beautiful but not too busy. After that, I'd go to SUMMIT for the view. The reviews say the views are amazing, although it can be crowded, so I'd try to go at a quieter time. For dinner, I'd choose a nice full-service restaurant. I'd say, “Hi, we have a reservation for 7:30.” If I needed directions, I'd start with “Excuse me” and then ask a clear question.

Model · Level 2

My ideal day would balance the calmer and more energetic sides of New York. I'd begin with a long walk through Central Park, then head to an observation deck later in the day. Recent reviews make SUMMIT sound visually spectacular but also quite busy, so I'd probably choose a time that avoids the biggest crowds. In the evening, I'd go somewhere polished but not overly formal for dinner. I wouldn't try to sound “fancy”; I'd simply adjust the register: “Good evening, we have a reservation for 7:30,” followed by normal, friendly requests. My goal would be to stay in English even when the conversation moves quickly.
