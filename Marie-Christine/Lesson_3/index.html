03

YOUR SIX-STEP SAFETY NET

Welcome → Ask → Listen → Clarify → Respond → Confirm

Une méthode simple pour ne pas rester bloquée.

1

Welcome

Good afternoon. Welcome!

Bonjour. Bienvenue !

2

Ask

How was your journey?

Comment s’est passé votre voyage ?

3

Listen

Listen for names, times, needs and problems.

Écoutez les noms, horaires, besoins et problèmes.

4

Clarify

Sorry, did you say…?

Pardon, avez-vous dit… ?

5

Respond

I’ll help you call the station.

Je vais vous aider à appeler la gare.

6

Confirm

So, we will call now. Is that right?

Donc, nous allons appeler maintenant. C’est bien cela ?

PUT THE SIX STEPS TOGETHER

A complete oral model

Use this text as a safety model, then change one or two details to make it your own.

Utilisez ce texte comme modèle rassurant, puis modifiez un ou deux détails.

You: Good afternoon. Welcome to northern France. It’s lovely to finally meet you. How was your journey?

Visitor: Thank you. The journey was quite long, and unfortunately my suitcase did not arrive with me.

You: I’m sorry to hear that. Did you say that your suitcase is still at the station?

Visitor: Yes. They said it may arrive on the next train.

You: Don’t worry. I’ll help you call the station and check when it will arrive. While we wait, would you like some water or a cup of tea?

Visitor: Some water would be lovely, thank you.

You: Of course. So, we’ll call the station now, and then I’ll show you your room. Is that right?

Visitor: Yes, that’s perfect. Thank you for your help.

★

LILATE STRATEGY

You do not need to understand every word.

Identify the key information, reformulate what you understood and ask for one missing detail. This shows real communication ability.

Vous n’avez pas besoin de comprendre chaque mot. Repérez l’essentiel, reformulez et demandez l’information manquante.

MISSION A · YOU ARE THE HOST

Welcome a visitor to northern France

Accueillir un visiteur dans le nord de la France.

THE SITUATION

An English-speaking friend of your family arrives for a three-day visit.

The visitor is tired after the journey, does not know the area and has a small transport problem.

1

Welcome

Good afternoon! Welcome to northern France. It is lovely to finally meet you.

2

Ask about the journey

How was your journey? Did everything go well?

3

Offer comfort

Would you like to rest, or would you prefer something to drink?

4

Explain the area

We are in northern France. There are several interesting places to visit nearby.

5

Suggest an activity

If the weather is good, we could visit the coast or explore a nearby town.

6

Solve a problem

Let me check the transport information and find the best solution.

LISTENING 1 · ARRIVAL

A guest arrives with a problem

You: Good afternoon! Welcome. How was your journey?

Guest: It was quite long, but I’m happy to be here. I’m afraid my suitcase is still at the station.

You: I’m sorry to hear that. Did the station staff give you a telephone number?

Guest: Yes, they did, but I didn’t understand everything.

You: Don’t worry. I’ll help you call them. First, would you like some tea or coffee?

Guest: Tea would be lovely, thank you.

1. What is the problem?

2. What information does the guest have?

3. What solution do you offer?

4. What would the guest like?

ARRIVAL

LISTENING 2 · VISITING MONTREAL

Plans, weather and clarification

Daughter: Welcome to Montreal! How was your flight?

You: It was comfortable, thank you, but I’m quite tired.

Daughter: Of course. We’re going to have dinner at home tonight. Tomorrow, we’re leaving at a quarter past nine to visit the old town.

You: Sorry, did you say a quarter to nine or a quarter past nine?

Daughter: A quarter past nine — 9:15.

You: Thank you. Could you write it down for me?

Daughter: Of course. The weather may be cold, so bring a warm coat.

1. How do you feel after the flight?

2. What time are you leaving?

3. Why are you leaving?

4. What advice do you receive?

THE ART OF CLARIFYING

Do not guess — protect the conversation

Ne devinez pas : protégez la conversation.

1

Say what happened

I’m sorry, I didn’t catch the last part.

2

Ask for a change

Could you repeat it more slowly, please?

3

Offer what you understood

Did you say the train leaves at 8:15?

4

Confirm the final information

So, we need to arrive at the station before eight. Is that correct?

COLLECT & TRANSMIT

Take a practical message from a visitor

Prendre puis transmettre un message pratique.

AUDIO MESSAGE

Listen twice and note the key details

First listen for the general situation. Then listen again for names, time, place and action.

Hello. This is Daniel Cooper. I am arriving at Lille Europe station on Friday at 4:45 in the afternoon. My original train was cancelled, so I will be about one hour later than planned. Could you please pick me up near the main entrance? I am wearing a blue coat and carrying a red suitcase. My telephone number is 06 48 23 71 90. Thank you.

YOUR NOTES

TRANSMIT THE INFORMATION

Turn your notes into a clear message

A2

Daniel Cooper will arrive at Lille Europe station on Friday at 4:45 p.m. His train was cancelled. He would like us to pick him up near the main entrance.

A2+

Daniel Cooper called to say that his train was cancelled. He will arrive at Lille Europe station on Friday at 4:45 p.m., about one hour later than planned. He would like to be picked up near the main entrance.

B1

Daniel Cooper has informed us that his original train was cancelled. He is now expected at Lille Europe station on Friday at 4:45 p.m., approximately one hour later than initially planned. He has asked to be collected near the main entrance. He will be wearing a blue coat and carrying a red suitcase.

YOUR TASK

Your guest’s train is delayed.

Write a message to confirm that you received the information, explain where you will meet and ask the guest to call if anything changes.

  • Greeting
  • Confirmation
  • Meeting place and time
  • One helpful instruction
  • Polite closing

Hello… / Thank you for your message. / I understand that… / I will meet you… / Please… / See you soon.
