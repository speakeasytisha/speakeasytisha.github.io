THE CONVERSATION FORMULA

Five moves that turn a short answer into a real exchange

Une réponse B1 n'a pas besoin d'être compliquée : elle a surtout besoin d'être développée et reliée à l'autre personne.

1

Answer

Give a direct answer first.

Yes, I do.

2

Reason

Explain why.

…because I enjoy discovering new places.

3

Detail

Add one concrete detail or example.

For example, I like visiting local markets.

4

Reaction

Show interest in the exchange.

That sounds interesting.

5

Ask back

Give the conversation back.

What about you?

SHORT A2 ANSWER

“Yes, I like travelling.”

Correct — but the conversation can stop here.

→

B1 CONVERSATION ANSWER

“Yes, I really enjoy travelling because I like discovering different places and cultures. I especially enjoy walking around a new town and trying local food. What kind of trips do you enjoy?”

03

GRAMMAR · EXPAND WITHOUT LOSING CONTROL

Use connectors to add a reason, detail, example, result or contrast

Le connecteur doit exprimer la relation logique entre vos idées.

REASON

because

I prefer smaller towns because they are usually quieter.

EXAMPLE

for example

I enjoy outdoor activities. For example, I love walking by the sea.

ADD

also / as well

I like museums, and I also enjoy historical places.

RESULT

so

The weather was excellent, so we stayed outside all day.

CONTRAST

but / however

The hotel was comfortable, but it was quite far from the centre.

B1 CONTRAST

although / whereas

Although it rained, we had a very good day.

B1 upgrade rule: one clear connector is better than three connectors used incorrectly. Start with because, so, but, for example; add although, however, whereas when the contrast is clear.

VERB POWER 1 · CHOOSE THE RIGHT VERB

Say / tell / speak / talk · make / do · look / see / watch

Des verbes fréquents qui se traduisent parfois de la même façon en français, mais pas en anglais.

say ↔ tell

SAY

Focus on the words/message.

She said she was tired.

say something · say hello · say that…

TELL

Usually needs a person.

She told me she was tired.

tell someone · tell a story · tell the truth

✓ say something · tell someone something

speak ↔ talk

SPEAK

Languages, formal communication, ability.

Do you speak English?

TALK

Conversation or discussion.

We talked about our holiday.

speak English · speak to someone · talk to/with someone · talk about something

make ↔ do

MAKE

Create, produce, cause.

We need to make a decision.

DO

Task, activity, work.

I need to do some research.

make a plan / mistake / choice · do homework / exercise / work

look ↔ see ↔ watch

LOOK AT

Direct your eyes intentionally.

Look at this photo.

SEE

Notice with your eyes / experience a sight.

I can see the sea.

WATCH

Look at something moving for a period.

We watched a film.

look at · see + object · watch + moving activity

VERB POWER 2 · MOVEMENT, MEMORY & EXCHANGE

Go / come / bring / take · know / meet · remember / remind · borrow / lend

Choisissez le verbe selon le point de vue : vers ici, vers ailleurs, connaître, rencontrer, se souvenir, rappeler, emprunter ou prêter.

go ↔ come · bring ↔ take

GO

Move away / to another place.

We are going to Italy.

TAKE

Carry something from here to there.

Take an umbrella with you.

COME

Move toward the speaker / destination.

Can you come here?

BRING

Carry something toward here.

Please bring your passport.

know ↔ meet

KNOW

Already be familiar with a person/place/fact.

I know her very well.

MEET

See someone for the first time or arrange to see them.

I met her in Boston.

remember ↔ remind

REMEMBER

Keep/bring something in your own memory.

I remember that trip very well.

REMIND

Help another person remember.

Please remind me to call tomorrow.

borrow ↔ lend

BORROW

Receive temporarily.

Can I borrow your charger?

LEND

Give temporarily.

Can you lend me your charger?

TENSES IN REAL CONVERSATION

Choose the tense from the meaning, not from a translation

Vous connaissez déjà ces temps. Ici, l'objectif est de les choisir automatiquement pendant une conversation.

🔁

Present simple

Use: habits, facts, preferences.

+I travel.
−I don't travel.
?Do you travel?

I usually travel with my family.

📍

Present continuous

Use: happening now or arranged future.

+I'm meeting…
−I'm not meeting…
?Are you meeting…?

I'm meeting friends on Friday.

✅

Past simple

Use: finished event at a finished time.

+I went.
−I didn't go.
?Did you go?

I went to Scotland last year.

🌉

Present perfect

Use: life experience / past connected to now.

+I've been.
−I haven't been.
?Have you been?

I've been to Ireland several times.

🧭

Future

Plan: going to · arrangement: present continuous · prediction/decision: will.

PLANI'm going to visit…
ARRANGEDI'm leaving on…
WILLI think I'll…
Conversation shortcut: Habit? → present simple. Finished time? → past simple. Life experience with no finished time? → present perfect. Decided plan? → going to. Fixed arrangement? → present continuous.

REACTIONS & FOLLOW-UP QUESTIONS

Show that you are listening — then keep the exchange alive

Une bonne conversation n'est pas seulement une série de réponses. Il faut aussi réagir et relancer.

Positive reaction

That sounds lovely! That must have been amazing. How nice!

Surprise / interest

Really? That's interesting. I didn't know that.

Empathy

I'm sorry to hear that. That sounds difficult. I understand.

Thinking time

Let me think… Well, actually… That's a good question.
Follow-up question pattern: take one detail from the other person's sentence and ask about it. “I went to Cork.” → “What did you like most about Cork?”

B1 FLUENCY · AVOID REPETITION

Use it, they, this, that, one and ones to sound more natural

Évitez de répéter le même nom dans chaque phrase.

IT

one thing / place / idea

I loved the old town. It was beautiful.

THEY

plural people / things

The restaurants were busy, but they were excellent.

THIS / THAT

refer to an idea or situation

We missed the train. That was stressful.

ONE / ONES

replace a countable noun

I prefer the smaller hotel to the expensive one.

LISTENING · CONVERSATION MOVES

Listen for the answer, reason, reaction and follow-up

N'écoutez pas seulement les faits : repérez aussi comment la conversation continue.

LISTENING A

A conversation about a recent weekend

A: Did you have a good weekend?

B: Yes, I did. We went to the coast because the weather was beautiful. We had lunch near the harbour and then we walked along the beach. It was quite busy, but the atmosphere was lovely.

A: That sounds nice. Did you stay there all day?

B: Almost. We came home in the early evening. What did you do?

LISTENING B

A conversation about future plans

A: Have you planned anything for the autumn?

B: Yes. I'm going to Ireland in September, and I'm travelling to Italy in October. I'm really looking forward to both trips because they will be very different.

A: Really? Which one are you most excited about?

B: That's difficult to say. Ireland may be cooler, whereas Italy will probably be warmer. I think both will be interesting in different ways.

READING · NOTICE HOW THE ANSWER GROWS

Read a natural exchange and identify the building blocks

Repérez les réponses directes, les raisons, les détails, les réactions et les relances.

Emma: Do you prefer city breaks or countryside holidays?

Alex: I usually prefer the countryside because I like quiet places and long walks. For example, last year I stayed in a small village near the mountains, and I really enjoyed the slower pace. However, I also like visiting cities for a few days because there is more to do in the evening.

Emma: I know what you mean. I enjoy both as well. Have you ever combined the two in the same trip?

Alex: Yes, I have. We did that in Scotland, and it worked really well. What kind of holiday do you usually choose?

ORAL PRACTICE STUDIO · 4 MISSIONS

Answer naturally, develop your ideas, then ask back

Chaque mission propose un objectif grammatical, un indice, un modèle A2+/B1 et l'audio du modèle.

ORAL 1

Tell a short anecdote

Prompt: Tell someone about a memorable day or weekend.

past simplefirst / then / finallyreactionquestion back
Start with Last… / A few weeks ago…. Give 2–3 events. Add how you felt. Finish with a question.

A2+: Last weekend, I went to the coast with my family. We had lunch and walked by the sea. The weather was good and I was very happy. What did you do last weekend?

B1: Last weekend, I went to the coast with my family because the weather was beautiful. First, we had lunch near the harbour, and then we walked along the beach for about an hour. It was quite busy, but the atmosphere was lovely. I really enjoyed spending time together. What do you usually like doing at the weekend?

○ not practised
ORAL 2

Give an opinion and justify it

Prompt: Is it better to plan a trip carefully or decide things spontaneously?

present simplebecausehoweverfor example
Choose one side first. Give one reason and one example. Then mention the other side briefly.

A2+: I prefer planning because it is less stressful. I like booking hotels before I travel. However, I also like having some free time.

B1: I generally prefer planning the important parts of a trip because it makes me feel more relaxed. For example, I like booking transport and accommodation in advance. However, I don't plan every hour because I also enjoy discovering things spontaneously. What about you — do you prefer a detailed plan?

○ not practised
ORAL 3

Compare two experiences

Prompt: Compare two places or trips you know.

comparativeswhereaspresent perfect / pastpreference
Compare weather, atmosphere, activities or travel style. Use more…than / quieter than / whereas.

A2+: Scotland is cooler than southern France, and the landscapes are very different. I liked both places, but I prefer Scotland for walking.

B1: I've visited both Scotland and Ireland, and I enjoyed them for different reasons. Scotland felt wilder and more dramatic, whereas Ireland seemed greener and more welcoming in some places. The weather was changeable in both countries, but that didn't stop us enjoying the scenery. Which type of landscape do you prefer?

○ not practised
ORAL 4

Explain a future plan

Prompt: Talk about one upcoming plan and explain what you are looking forward to.

going topresent continuouswillask back
Plan = going to. Fixed arrangement = present continuous. Prediction = will.

A2+: I'm going to travel in September. I'm leaving in the middle of the month. I think the weather will be cool, but I hope it will be sunny.

B1: I'm going to travel in September, and I'm really looking forward to having a change of scenery. I'm leaving in the middle of the month, so the arrangements are already quite clear. I think the weather will probably be cooler than in France, but that should be perfect for walking. Do you have any plans for the autumn?
