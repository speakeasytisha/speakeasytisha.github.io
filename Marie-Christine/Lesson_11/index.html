01

5 MIN · WARM-UP

Two short sentences → one stronger sentence

Combinez deux idées simples sans perdre la phrase principale.

TWO SHORT SENTENCES

Your daughter lives in Boston. She teaches at an international school.

Clear — but separate.

→

ONE B1 SENTENCE

Your daughter, who lives in Boston, teaches at an international school.

The sentence still has a complete main message: Your daughter teaches…

TRY IT ALOUD FIRST

Connect each pair before you open the model.

1.

You visited a place in Ireland. The place had beautiful scenery.

A2+: I visited a place in Ireland. It had beautiful scenery.

B1: I visited a place in Ireland that had beautiful scenery.

2.

You have family in Boston. You enjoy visiting them.

A2+: I have family in Boston. I enjoy visiting them.

B1: I have family in Boston, which is a city I really enjoy visiting.

3.

You use English for family conversations. These conversations matter to you.

A2+: I use English for family conversations. They matter to me.

B1: I want English for family conversations that really matter to me.

02

10 MIN · THE GRAMMAR HUMP

WHO · WHICH · THAT · WHERE — and the main sentence must survive

Le mot relatif ajoute une précision. Il ne remplace pas le verbe principal de la phrase.

PEOPLE

WHO

Use who for a person or people.

Your daughter, who lives in Boston, teaches at a school.

Main clause: Your daughter teaches…

THINGS / IDEAS

WHICH

Use which for a thing, situation or extra idea.

The trip, which lasted a week, was memorable.

Main clause: The trip was memorable.

PEOPLE OR THINGS

THAT

Use that to identify a person or thing in essential information.

I like places that are easy to explore on foot.

Neutral and common in everyday English.

PLACES

WHERE

Use where for a place + what happens there.

Boston is a city where you can walk between many sights.

where = in/at that place

The survival test: temporarily remove the relative clause. If the remaining sentence is not complete, you probably created a fragment. “Your daughter, who lives in Boston.” ✗ → “Your daughter, who lives in Boston, teaches at an international school.” ✓

03

7 MIN · ACCURACY CLINIC

The noun before WHO controls the verb

Le verbe dans la relative s’accorde avec le nom qui précède who/that.

One person → singular verb

SHE / ONE PERSON

Your daughter, who lives in Boston…

A colleague who works with clients…

REMEMBER

he / she / it → verb + s in present simple.

More than one → plural verb

THEY / PEOPLE

Your sons, who live in France…

Clients who need help…

REMEMBER

I / you / we / they → base verb in present simple.

Possessive repair: a place or thing normally uses its, not his/her. Example: Iceland is famous for its landscapes. A plural noun uses their: My children enjoy their visits.

7 MIN · PROFESSIONAL TRANSFER

Use the same grammar to identify, clarify and transmit information

La grammaire personnelle devient une compétence professionnelle : décrire une personne, un document, un lieu ou une demande avec précision.

👤

Identify a person

The client who called this morning needs a confirmation.

📄

Identify a document

Please use the form that is attached to the email.

📍

Clarify a place

The meeting is in the room where we met last week.

🔁

Reformulate

So you need the document that explains the procedure, correct?

COMMUNICATION MISSION

A caller needs information, but the first description is vague.

Situation: You need to identify the correct person and document, reformulate the request, and transmit the information clearly.

IdentifyAdd detailReformulateConfirmTransmit
Use at least one clause with who for the person and one with that/which for the document. Finish by checking: Is that the information you need?

A2+: You are looking for the colleague who works with the customer. You also need the document that explains the next steps. I can send it to you. Is that correct?

B1: So, you are trying to contact the colleague who handles this customer account, and you need the document that explains the next steps. I can forward the document and confirm who is available today. Is that the information you need?

SPEAKING TIMER01:30
PERSONAL

Describe one person who matters to you

Say who the person is, where they live or what they do, and why they are important in your life.

whomain sentenceone reason
Start with: One person who… is… or My …, who …, is…

A2+: One person who is very important to me is a family member who lives abroad. We speak regularly and I enjoy visiting.

B1: One person who is very important to me is a family member who lives abroad. Although we do not see each other every week, we stay close through calls and visits, which makes those conversations especially important to me.

○ not practised
TRAVEL

Describe a place that you remember well

Explain where it is, what you saw or did there, and what made it memorable.

that / whichwherepast tense
Try: I visited a place that… It was somewhere where… The part that I remember most was…

A2+: I visited Ireland, which was very beautiful. I especially liked the places where we could see the countryside.

B1: One trip that I remember very well was my visit to Ireland. I especially enjoyed the areas where we could see the countryside and spend time outside. The part that I remember most was the feeling of discovering somewhere completely different.

○ not practised
PROFESSIONAL

Clarify a person, place and document

Explain which person is involved, where the action happens and which document is needed.

whowherethat / which
Use this order: person → place → document → confirmation question.

A2+: The colleague who works with the customer is in the office where we had the meeting. She needs the document that explains the procedure.

B1: The colleague who is responsible for the customer is in the office where we had the meeting yesterday. She needs the document that explains the procedure, so I am going to send it to her now. Is that the correct document?

○ not practised
