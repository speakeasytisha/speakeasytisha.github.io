WARM-UP · REACT WITH A PLAN

What would you do first?

Que feriez-vous en premier ?

YOUR 3-MOVE ROUTINE

Don’t search for a perfect sentence. Follow a simple plan.

For every problem, do three things: say what is wrong, ask for the information you need, then choose the next action.

Pour chaque problème : expliquez ce qui ne va pas, demandez l’information utile, puis choisissez l’action suivante.

1 SAY THE PROBLEM2 ASK ONE USEFUL QUESTION3 CHOOSE THE NEXT STEP
🧳SAY THE PROBLEM

Your suitcase does not arrive

Start with one calm sentence. Then ask for help.

Excuse me. My suitcase hasn’t arrived. Could you help me, please?

🚆ASK FOR FACTS

Your train is delayed

You may miss your connection. Ask for the delay, platform and next option.

How long is the delay? Which platform do I need? Is there another train?

🏨SHOW EVIDENCE

Your reservation is missing

Explain what you booked and show the information you already have.

I booked a room online, but I can’t see my reservation. I have the confirmation email here.

A2

I have a problem. My suitcase is missing. It is black and medium-sized. Can you help me?

A2+

My suitcase hasn’t arrived. It is a medium-sized black suitcase with a red label. Could you tell me what I need to do?

B1

My suitcase hasn’t arrived on the baggage belt, although the other passengers have collected their luggage. I’d like to report it and find out when it might be delivered.

FULL ORAL MODEL

Lost luggage: a complete interaction

Use this only after you have tried the steps yourself.

You: Excuse me. My suitcase hasn’t arrived.

Staff: I’m sorry to hear that. Which flight were you on?

You: I was on flight 382 from Montreal.

Staff: Can you describe your suitcase?

You: Yes. It’s a medium-sized black suitcase with a red label.

Staff: Thank you. Your reference number is BK-4172.

You: I’m sorry, could you repeat the reference number more slowly, please?

Staff: Of course. B-K, four-one-seven-two. We’ll contact you by text. It may be delivered tomorrow.

You: So, you’ll contact me by text when it arrives, and it may be delivered to my address tomorrow. Is that right?

Staff: Exactly.

You: Perfect. Thank you for your help.

05

GRAMMAR 1 · TELL WHAT HAPPENED

Past simple vs past continuous

Distinguer l’action principale et l’action en cours.

PAST SIMPLE

Finished action

Subject + past form

I missed my connection.

The train arrived late.

Action terminée, événement principal.

PAST CONTINUOUS

Action in progress

was/were + verb-ing

I was waiting when they announced the delay.

We were travelling when the train stopped.

Action qui était en cours à ce moment-là.

TOGETHER

Background + event

I was waiting for my luggage when an employee called my name.

While I was checking in, the computer stopped working.

when introduit souvent l’événement; while l’action en cours.

Remember: do not use was + past verb. Say I was waiting, not “I was waited”. For a completed action, say I waited.

06

GRAMMAR 2 · PAST EVENT OR CURRENT PROBLEM?

Past simple vs present perfect

Choisir selon le lien avec le présent.

PAST SIMPLE

Finished time

Use it with yesterday, last night, at 3 p.m., in 2025.

I lost my phone yesterday.

My train left at 7:10.

PRESENT PERFECT

Result matters now

have/has + past participle

My suitcase hasn’t arrived yet.

I have lost my room key. (= I do not have it now.)

KEY WORDS

yet · already · just

It hasn’t arrived yet.

I’ve already spoken to reception.

The train has just been cancelled.

Ces mots sont très utiles pour parler d’une situation actuelle.

I lost my key last night.Finished time → past simple
I have lost my key.Problem now → present perfect

GRAMMAR 3 · ASK, ADVISE, OBLIGE

Can, could, would, should, have to & must

Demandes polies, conseils et obligations.

CAN

Ability / simple request

Can you help me?

Direct mais poli.

COULD

Politer request

Could you repeat that?

Très utile au LILATE.

WOULD

Polite choice / offer

Would you like me to call a taxi?

SHOULD

Advice

You should speak to the information desk.

HAVE TO

External rule / necessity

You have to complete this form.

MUST

Strong obligation

You must keep your receipt.

Plus fort que “should”.

Important: after a modal, use the base verb: could repeat, should call, must keep. Not “could repeated” or “must to keep”.

GRAMMAR 4 · PLAN THE SOLUTION

First conditional: If + present → will + base verb

Parler d’une situation possible et de sa conséquence.

IF + PRESENT → WILL + BASE VERB

If the train is late, I will take the next one.

If my suitcase doesn’t arrive tonight, I will call the airline tomorrow.

Not:

❌ If the train will be late…

✅ If the train is late…

if = conditionwhen = expected eventunless = if not (B1 bonus)
