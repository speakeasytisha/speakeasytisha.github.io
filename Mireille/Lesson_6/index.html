The rule · 1st, 2nd, 3rd…

Cardinal: one, two, three (quantity). Ordinal: first, second, third (position/date). Most take -th, but 1st/2nd/3rd and spelling changes must be learned. Important exception: 11th, 12th and 13th all take -th (not -st, -nd or -rd). For 21, 22, 23 and 31, use the ending of the last number: twenty-first, twenty-second, twenty-third, thirty-first.

Pour dire une date à l’oral, on emploie généralement un nombre ordinal : le premier, le deuxième, etc. Attention aux irréguliers.

The rule · order and pronunciation

🇬🇧 British English

Written: 05/11/2026 = 5 November 2026 (day / month / year).

Spoken: the fifth of November, twenty twenty-six.

🇺🇸 American English

Written: 11/05/2026 = November 5, 2026 (month / day / year).

Spoken: November fifth, twenty twenty-six.

Important: 04/07 is ambiguous: UK = 4 July; US = April 7. In international communications, write the month in letters, e.g. 7 April 2027 or April 7, 2027.

Preposition: on Monday / on 5 November; in November / in 2027; at 2 p.m. Questions: “What date is the event?” / “When does the exhibition open?” Negative: “The exhibition does not open on Monday.”

UK : jour/mois/année. US : mois/jour/année. À l’international, écrire le mois en toutes lettres évite les confusions.

The rule · digital and spoken time

Digital / 24-hour clock

09:00 → nine o’clock / nine a.m.

14:15 → two fifteen p.m.

17:45 → five forty-five p.m.

12:00 → noon; 00:00 → midnight.

Traditional spoken time

09:15 → quarter past nine.

09:30 → half past nine.

09:45 → quarter to ten.

09:05 → five past nine.

UK: “half nine” is informal British English for 9:30; use half past nine for clarity. US: “nine thirty” is especially common; “quarter after nine” and “quarter till ten” also occur. Both varieties understand digital-style times.

Written: 2:30 p.m. (12-hour), 14:30 (24-hour). a.m. before noon; p.m. after noon. Avoid saying “14 p.m.”

Questions: “What time does the tour start?” / “Does it start at half past two?” Negative: “It doesn't start at three.”

past = après ; to = avant l’heure suivante. 9 h 45 = quarter to ten (un quart avant dix). 14 h 30 = two thirty p.m.

Present Simple for timetables

You already know the Present Simple for questions. Now use it for fixed programmes and scheduled events.

Affirmative: The exhibition opens at ten. / The tours start at two.

Negative: The exhibition does not open on Monday. / The tours do not start at noon.

Questions: Does the workshop start at eleven? / Do the tours finish at four?

Keywords: every Monday, on Friday, at 2 p.m., opens, starts, finishes. Exception: be → “Is the centre open?” (not “Does the centre be open?”).

Contrast: “The talk starts at 3:45” (published schedule); “The talk will start fifteen minutes late” (new change).

On emploie souvent le présent simple pour les horaires officiels, même lorsqu’ils concernent un événement futur. Will sert ici à annoncer un changement.

Preparation · useful phrases

Ask: “Which date would you prefer?” · “Would you like the morning or afternoon tour?” Confirm: “That is Friday, 6 November, at a quarter to four.” Clarify: “Could you confirm the date, please?” Handle ambiguity: “Do you mean 6 May or 5 June?” Transfer: “I will send the details to my colleague.”

D’abord écouter et reformuler ; ensuite confirmer la date et l’heure ; enfin proposer la prochaine étape.

Scenario A · Help a visitor choose

A visitor wants a cultural event on Friday afternoon. Recommend the heritage talk and confirm the start time.

Clear A2 model

On Friday, 6 November, there is a heritage talk at 3:45 p.m. Would you like more information?

Stretch B1 model

We have a heritage talk on Friday, 6 November. It begins at a quarter to four and finishes at half past four. Would you like me to explain the programme?

Scenario B · Avoid a date mistake

A US visitor writes “05/11/2026”. Ask whether they mean May 11 or November 5. Do not guess.

Clear A2 model

Could you confirm the date, please? Do you mean May 11 or November 5?

Stretch B1 model

Just to avoid a misunderstanding, could you confirm whether 05/11 means May 11 in US format or 5 November in UK format? I can then check the programme.

Scenario C · A schedule change

The Thursday tour has been delayed from 2:30 to 2:45 p.m. Apologise, give the new time and offer help.

Clear A2 model

I am sorry. The tour will start at 2:45 p.m., not 2:30. Can I help you?

Stretch B1 model

I apologise for the change. The Thursday English tour will now begin at a quarter to three, fifteen minutes later than planned. Thank you for your patience.

Writing toolkit

Short confirmation: Greeting → event → unambiguous date → time + time zone if needed → polite closing.

Colleague note: Who? Which event? Which date/time? What needs checking?

Useful forms: “I am writing to confirm…” · “The visit is scheduled for…” · “Could you please check…?” · “Thank you for your help.”

Une date avec le mois en lettres est plus sûre pour un correspondant international. Évitez de supposer qu’un format numérique est compris de la même façon.

Write to a visitor

Confirm the English tour on Thursday, 5 November 2026, at 2:30 p.m. Include a polite greeting and closing.

Stretch B1 model

Dear Visitor, I am pleased to confirm that the English guided tour is scheduled for Thursday, 5 November 2026, at 2:30 p.m. It will last approximately 45 minutes. Please let us know if you need any further information. Kind regards.
