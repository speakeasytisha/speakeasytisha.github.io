PROFESSIONAL INTERACTION

Welcome first. Then discover what the visitor needs.

You do not need many sentences. You need a reliable sequence you can reuse in a museum, heritage site, exhibition, cultural centre or event.

L’objectif est d’avoir une petite routine fiable : accueillir → proposer de l’aide → identifier le besoin → répondre ou vérifier.

GRAMMAR THAT DOES A JOB

Three structures that help you guide a visitor.

Learn the rule, see it in context, then use it immediately.

1

There is / There are

Use this to say what exists in a place.

There is + singular
There are + plural

There is a lift near the entrance.
There are two galleries upstairs.

2

Can / can’t

Use this for possibility, permission and services.

can + base verb

You can leave your coat here.
You can’t take photographs in this room.

3

Where / What time / Does

Use question words to get precise information.

Where is…?
What time does…?

Where is the lift?
What time does the tour start?

Recycling last lesson: The tour starts at eleven. → What time does the tour start? One -S, not two.

LISTEN FOR THE NEED

You do not need every word. Find the visitor’s real request.

Listen once for the general idea, then listen again for details. This is a professional skill — and a useful LILATE habit.

LISTENING 1

Your task: listen and identify what the visitor needs.

2. Why does the visitor ask about the lift?

3. What detail needs confirmation?

The LILATE IA evaluates professional communication across four skills. This lesson only practises the first type of professional situation: welcoming, identifying a need and responding clearly.

On reste volontairement sur une seule petite mission. Le but est de transférer ce que vous venez d’apprendre, pas de faire un examen blanc complet.

PROFESSIONAL INTERACTION

An international visitor arrives unexpectedly

Listen to the visitor:

Good afternoon. I only have about forty minutes before my train. I would really like to see the “Stories of the Coast” exhibition, but I also need to know whether I can leave my suitcase somewhere. What would you recommend?

Use this 4-step plan

  1. Welcome the visitor.
  2. Confirm the two needs: limited time + suitcase.
  3. Give one practical solution.
  4. Recommend a simple visit plan.

Useful language: “Of course.” · “There is…” · “You can…” · “I recommend…” · “You have enough time to…”

01:30
Clear A2

Good afternoon. Welcome. Yes, you can leave your suitcase at the information desk. The exhibition is in Gallery 1. You have forty minutes, so I recommend starting with the main room near the entrance.

Stretch

Good afternoon and welcome. You can leave your suitcase at the information desk, so that will not be a problem. As you only have about forty minutes, I recommend starting with Gallery 1 and focusing on the main coastal stories near the entrance. That should give you enough time to enjoy the exhibition before your train.

LILATE alignment

Current LILATE IA guidance describes Part 1 as “Accueil et interaction”: welcome/identify the interlocutor, understand the request, propose an adapted service and interact effectively. Keep this task short and functional. Do not push for memorised sophistication.
