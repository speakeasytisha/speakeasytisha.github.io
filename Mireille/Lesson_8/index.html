LESSON 08 · 01

Make your explanations persuasive

Move from simple motives to evidence, implications and balanced recommendations.

The four-stage professional answer

POSITION → REASON → EXAMPLE → CONSEQUENCE → QUALIFICATION

Position: I would prioritise accessible interpretation. Rationale: This approach responds to different levels of prior knowledge. Illustration: For instance, layered labels allow visitors to choose how much detail they need. Implication: As a result, participation may improve. Qualification: However, we would need to test the resources with actual users.

Explain an investment decision

A cultural centre has €2,000 to improve the visitor experience. Explain where you would invest and how you would evaluate the result.

B1+ developed model

I would improve the exhibition labels first because visitors need clear information. For example, we could offer a shorter version of each explanation. We could ask visitors whether the new labels are easier to understand.

B2/B2+ extended model

Although the proposal would not meet every accessibility need, a small-scale evaluation could reveal which adjustments deliver the greatest benefit before additional funding is committed.

LESSON 08 · 03

Grammar laboratory · Cause, purpose & outcome

Build sophisticated arguments using clauses, noun phrases, infinitives and cause-and-effect verbs.

Choose the structure that matches what follows

1 · BECAUSE / SINCE / AS + subject + finite verb

Since visitor expectations vary, we should offer different formats. Puisque / étant donné que : proposition complète (sujet + verbe).

2 · BECAUSE OF / DUE TO / OWING TO + noun / noun phrase / -ing

The activity was adapted because of the participants’ feedback. / Owing to limited funding, we introduced a pilot. En raison de : groupe nominal ou -ing, PAS une proposition conjuguée.

3 · SO THAT + subject + CAN / COULD / WILL / WOULD + verb

We simplified the introduction so that visitors could engage with the artwork. Afin que : une proposition avec sujet et modal.

4 · IN ORDER TO / SO AS TO + base verb; IN ORDER NOT TO + base verb

We consulted local residents in order to reflect different perspectives. We used plain language in order not to exclude newcomers. Afin de / pour ne pas : infinitif.

5 · LEAD TO / RESULT IN + noun / -ing; RESULT FROM / STEM FROM + noun

Better signage may lead to greater participation. Confusion can result from unclear instructions. Entraîner vs découler de : attention à la direction de la cause.

6 · THEREBY + -ing; AS A RESULT / CONSEQUENTLY + full clause

We invited feedback, thereby strengthening trust. The labels were too technical; consequently, some visitors felt excluded. Ce faisant + -ing ; par conséquent + proposition.

7 · ENABLE / ALLOW + object + TO + verb; PREVENT + object + FROM + -ing

The audio guide enables visitors to explore independently. Clear signs prevent people from getting lost. Permettre à quelqu'un de / empêcher quelqu'un de.

LESSON 08 · 04

Beyond basic connectors · Precision & nuance

Use contrast, evidence, concessions, alternatives and conditions without relying on the same linking words.

Connector toolkit · Grammar + French + professional example

CONCESSION

Although / Even though + clause (bien que) — Although the budget is limited, the pilot is feasible.

Despite / In spite of + noun or -ing (malgré) — Despite working with a small team, we can deliver a meaningful visit.

Nevertheless / Nonetheless + independent clause (néanmoins) — The research is incomplete; nevertheless, the exhibition raises useful questions.

COMPARISON, EVIDENCE AND NUANCE

Whereas + clause (tandis que) — A lecture transmits information, whereas a workshop encourages participation.

Not only … but also … (non seulement… mais aussi…) — The project not only preserves stories but also strengthens local connections.

In light of + noun (au vu de) — In light of visitor feedback, we redesigned the introduction.

To some extent (dans une certaine mesure) — Digital tools can, to some extent, widen access.

RECOMMENDATION AND CONDITIONS

Provided that / As long as + clause (à condition que) — The plan can work provided that staff receive training.

Unless + clause (à moins que / sauf si) — The trial may fail unless we consult residents.

Rather than + noun / -ing (plutôt que) — Rather than replacing guides, we should support them with digital tools.

On balance / Taking everything into account (tout bien considéré) — On balance, the benefits justify a limited trial.

Useful professional frames

One consideration worth highlighting is… / A potential drawback is… / This raises the question of whether… / What this suggests is… / A more sustainable approach might be… / I would recommend this option on the grounds that…

Pronunciation masterclass · /t/, /d/, /ɪd/ and /s/, /z/, /ɪz/

Learn the actual rules behind English word endings, then listen, classify and practise meaningful work-related sentences.

Past -ED: the final SOUND of the base verb decides

/t/ after a voiceless sound (except /t/)

worked /wɜːkt/, developed /dɪˈveləpt/, discussed /dɪˈskʌst/. No extra syllable.

Après un son sourd : /p, k, f, s, ʃ, tʃ, θ/ → /t/.

/d/ after a voiced sound (except /d/)

played /pleɪd/, explained /ɪkˈspleɪnd/, organised /ˈɔːɡənaɪzd/. No extra syllable.

Après une voyelle ou un son sonore → /d/.

/ɪd/ after /t/ or /d/

connected /kəˈnektɪd/, guided /ˈɡaɪdɪd/, created /kriˈeɪtɪd/. One extra syllable.

Après /t/ ou /d/, ajouter une syllabe /ɪd/. On écoute les SONS et non la dernière lettre.

Plural, possessive and third-person -S: the same sound rule

/s/ after a voiceless, non-sibilant sound

maps /mæps/, exhibits /ɪɡˈzɪbɪts/, works /wɜːks/.

/z/ after a voiced, non-sibilant sound

visitors /ˈvɪzɪtəz/, guides /ɡaɪdz/, stories /ˈstɔːriz/, plays /pleɪz/.

/ɪz/ after a sibilant sound: /s, z, ʃ, ʒ, tʃ, dʒ/

places /ˈpleɪsɪz/, exhibitions /ˌeksɪˈbɪʃənz/ (ends /z/, not /ɪz/), bridges /ˈbrɪdʒɪz/, watches /ˈwɒtʃɪz/.

Attention : c'est le dernier son du mot singulier ou du verbe de base qui compte.

Place your fingers lightly on your throat: /z/ and /d/ vibrate; /s/ and /t/ do not. /ɪd/ and /ɪz/ add an extra syllable.

What could your Bachelor enable you to do?

Move beyond introducing the programme: evaluate professional skills, opportunities, limitations and your own long-term goals.

Career-focused reading · From qualification to professional impact

Context: Published information for a Bachelor Européen Médiation Culturelle lists areas including art history, cultural networks, mediation tools, project management and professional expertise. This is an external reference, not confirmation of your exact course. Read the published programme information ↗.

A qualification in cultural mediation can be more than a new academic credential. It may provide a framework for analysing the relationship between cultural institutions, communities and visitors. Through studying artistic practices, cultural policies, project planning and audience engagement, learners may strengthen their ability to design initiatives that are meaningful, inclusive and sustainable.

However, acquiring theoretical knowledge is only one part of professional development. Practical assignments may help learners translate abstract principles into concrete actions: identifying an audience’s needs, evaluating possible formats, coordinating stakeholders and measuring the impact of an activity. These skills are transferable across museums, heritage organisations, cultural associations and community projects, although specific employment opportunities depend on experience, local requirements and the qualification actually obtained.

Crucially, the value of the programme may lie in the learner’s capacity to justify professional choices. Rather than simply organising an event, a cultural mediator should be able to explain whom the event serves, why the format is appropriate, how accessibility has been considered and what evidence would demonstrate success.

LESSON 08 · 08

Live cultural mediation · Three guided missions

Learn a response strategy before each realistic professional situation, then produce a developed answer.

How to explain a difficult artwork without oversimplifying it

The 6-step interpretation method

1 Observe: “What do you notice first?” 2 Describe: factual visual elements without judging. 3 Contextualise: period, technique or cultural context (only if known). 4 Interpret: “One possible interpretation is…” 5 Invite: “How does that relate to your experience?” 6 Check: “Would you like a little more background?”

Distinguer un fait vérifié d'une interprétation. Ne pas inventer d'artiste, de date, d'intention ou de provenance.

Practice artwork (fictional): A contemporary installation contains a broken wooden chair, a projected image of a harbour and recorded voices speaking different languages. The artist’s intention is not supplied. A visitor says, “I don’t understand this. Is it even art?”

Language bank

“That’s a fair question.” / “Rather than giving you a single answer…” / “We can begin with what we can observe…” / “It may suggest…” / “There is room for more than one interpretation.” / “What associations does it bring to mind?”

Mission A · The sceptical visitor

First acknowledge the concern. Then name two visible elements, propose a cautious interpretation, invite the visitor into the conversation and end with a question. Aim: 80–120 seconds.

B1+ developed model

I understand why this artwork seems unusual. We can see a broken chair and images of a harbour. The voices are in different languages. These elements might suggest memories or journeys, but we cannot be certain without more information. What do they make you think of?

B2/B2+ extended model

I can see why the installation might be challenging at first, and that uncertainty can itself be a useful starting point. Rather than searching for an immediate explanation, perhaps we could look at the elements individually: the damaged chair, the harbour projection and the overlapping voices. One possible reading is that they raise questions about belonging, movement or memory, although that remains an interpretation rather than a confirmed statement of the artist’s intention. What interests me is how these different media affect our experience of the work. Do any of the sounds or images connect with something you have experienced?

Mission B · The time-pressed family

A family has only five minutes. Explain the work in 70–90 words, use one question suitable for children, and suggest a way to explore further.

B1+ developed model

This artwork uses a chair, images and voices to tell a story that we can interpret in different ways. We do not need to find one right answer. Can you choose one sound or image that you find interesting? If you have time later, you can listen again and share your ideas.

B2/B2+ extended model

We have just a few minutes, so I suggest approaching this installation like a mystery made of three clues: a chair, a harbour and many voices. Each may create a different feeling, and there is no need to decide immediately what the whole work means. If you could add one object to this installation, what would it be and why? You might discover that your own answer opens up a completely new interpretation.

Mission C · The critical specialist

A specialist asks whether your interpretation is evidence-based. Distinguish observable facts, provisional hypotheses and information that would require research; propose a follow-up.

B1+ developed model

We can confirm the materials we see and hear, but we do not have a statement from the artist. My explanation is therefore only a possible interpretation. I would check the exhibition documentation before making a stronger claim.

B2/B2+ extended model

That is an important distinction. The materials, spatial arrangement and recorded languages can be described directly, whereas any claim about the artist’s precise intentions would require corroborating evidence. I would therefore frame the discussion as an interpretive possibility rather than a definitive reading. To substantiate it, I would consult the artist’s statement, curatorial notes and any relevant documentation, while remaining attentive to alternative perspectives. This would allow us to communicate the work responsibly without closing down visitor dialogue.

Known person
