GRAMMAR YOU CAN SEE & USE

Present Simple or Present Continuous?

Use the Present Simple for routines, roles, facts and regular schedules. Use the Present Continuous for actions happening now, around now, or temporary project activity.

Présent simple = habitudes, fonctions, faits, horaires réguliers. Présent continu = action en cours / situation temporaire autour de maintenant.

Present Simple

Affirmative: I/you/we/they + verb · he/she/it + verb-S
Negative: do not / does not + base verb
Question: Do / Does + subject + base verb?
  • job roles and responsibilities
  • routines and repeated actions
  • facts and permanent situations
  • regular schedules
usuallyevery weekoftennormallyon Mondays

Present Continuous

Affirmative: am / is / are + verb-ing
Negative: am not / isn’t / aren’t + verb-ing
Question: Am / Is / Are + subject + verb-ing?
  • an action happening now
  • a temporary situation
  • a project happening around now
  • a planned near-future arrangement

PROJECT LANGUAGE

Obligation, necessity, possibility and polite requests.

These forms help you organise a project and speak professionally without needing complicated grammar.

Ces structures servent à parler des obligations, des besoins, des possibilités et des demandes polies dans un projet.
FunctionFormUseExample
Obligationhave to + verbA necessary rule or action.We have to confirm the venue.
No necessitydon’t / doesn’t have to + verbIt is optional; it is not necessary.Visitors don’t have to book in advance.
Needneed to + verbA practical necessity.We need to check the budget.
Possibilitycan + base verbSomething is possible / permitted.We can use the courtyard.
Not possiblecan’t + base verbSomething is impossible / not permitted.We can’t move the artwork alone.
Polite requestCould you + base verb…?Ask someone professionally.Could you send the floor plan, please?
IMPORTANT DIFFERENCE
don’t have to

= it is not necessary.

“You don’t have to print the ticket.”

can’t

= it is not possible / not permitted.

“Visitors can’t enter this room today.”

Form rule: after can / can’t / could, use the base verb: can go, could send — never “can to go” or “could sends”.

PROJECT BRIEFCULTURE & HERITAGEVENDÉE

Voices of the Coast — Community Heritage Weekend

The cultural centre is preparing a two-day project called Voices of the Coast. The aim is to connect local maritime heritage with contemporary artistic work. The project is designed for local residents, families and visitors to the region.

The public opening is planned for Saturday morning. On Friday, the team is installing the exhibition and testing the visitor route. Three local artists are taking part. A heritage association is providing historical documents, and the venue team is preparing the reception area and accessibility information.

Your role is to help coordinate communication between the artists, the association and the venue team. You need to confirm arrival times, check the visitor information and make sure each partner knows what they have to prepare. One artist has just explained that she needs a projector for a short video, but the room currently reserved for her does not have one.

Before the next meeting, prepare a short update: explain what is ready, what the team is doing now, and what still needs a solution. Do not promise new spending before the budget has been checked.

PROBLEM-SOLVING WITHOUT PANIC

A problem is not a language failure. It is a communication task.

Use the same four steps whenever something unexpected happens: acknowledge, clarify, propose, confirm.

Face à un imprévu : reconnaître le problème → demander les précisions → proposer une solution → confirmer la suite.
1

Acknowledge

Show you heard the issue.

I’m sorry about that.
2

Clarify

Get the facts.

Can you tell me exactly what happened?
3

Propose

Offer a realistic option.

We can try another room.
4

Confirm

Close the loop.

I’ll confirm this by 3 p.m.
