03 / LANGUAGE TOOLKIT

Ask the right questions

In cultural mediation, questions help you understand visitors' needs.

Grammar · Present Simple questions

Rule: Do + I/you/we/they + base verb? · Does + he/she/it + base verb?

Examples: “Do you need a map?” · “Does the tour start at two?”

Negative: “The tour does not start at two.” · “We do not offer a tour on Mondays.”

Important: After does, use the base verb: “Does the tour start?” (not “starts”). With be, do not add do: “Is the museum open?”

Questions au présent simple : « Do you need… ? » = « Avez-vous besoin de… ? ». « Does the tour start… ? » = « La visite commence-t-elle… ? »

Guided practice

___ you need a ticket?

___ the museum open today?

___ the tour start at 2 p.m.?

Your communication strategy

1. Greet: “Hello, welcome!” → 2. Ask: “How can I help?” → 3. Clarify: “Do you mean the afternoon tour?” → 4. Confirm: “So, you need two tickets?”

04 / LANGUAGE TOOLKIT

Offer help politely

One small change can make your English sound warm and professional.

Grammar · can, could and would like

Can + base verb

Ability or practical offer: “I can show you the gallery.” / “Can I help?”

Could + base verb

Polite request: “Could you repeat that, please?” / “Could I have a map?”

Would like + noun / to + verb

Polite wish: “I would like a ticket.” / “I would like to visit the museum.”

Negative: “I can't change the opening time.” · “I wouldn't like to miss the visit.”

Questions: “Can you show me?” · “Could you tell me the time?” · “Would you like a map?”

Common mistake: “I would like to visit,” not “I would like visit.” After can and could, no to.

« Could you… ? » = « Pourriez-vous… ? ». « Would you like… ? » = « Souhaitez-vous… ? ». « I can… » = « Je peux… ».

Check your understanding

Which is the most polite way to ask someone to repeat?

Welcome. Clarify. Solve.

You are the cultural mediator. Your trainer plays the visitor. Start with the sentence starters, then try without them.

SCENARIO A · WELCOMEGuided → independent

A family from Ireland arrives

Two adults and a 12-year-old child would like an English tour. They do not know where to meet.

Useful phrases: “Welcome to…” · “Would you like…?” · “The tour starts at…” · “Please meet at…”

Clear A2 model

“Hello, welcome! The English tour starts at two. Please meet at the main entrance. Can I help you with anything else?”

Stretch B1 model

“Welcome to the heritage centre! Our English guided tour begins at two and lasts about forty-five minutes. You can meet your guide at the main entrance. Would you like a map while you wait?”

SCENARIO B · CLARIFYGuided → independent

You did not understand the question

A visitor speaks quickly and asks about accessibility. You need them to repeat and explain.

Useful phrases: “Could you repeat that, please?” · “Do you mean…?” · “Let me check.” · “Thank you for waiting.”

Clear A2 model

“Could you repeat that, please? Do you need wheelchair access? Let me check the information.”

Stretch B1 model

“Of course. Just to make sure I understood correctly, you would like to know whether the exhibition is accessible without stairs. Let me check with a colleague so I can give you accurate information.”

Professional habit: do not invent accessibility information. Check with the venue when you are unsure.
SCENARIO C · CHANGEGuided → independent

A guided tour is delayed

The guide has called. The tour will start 15 minutes late. Tell the visitors, apologise and offer a helpful next step.

Useful phrases: “I'm sorry…” · “The tour will start…” · “You can…” · “Thank you for your patience.”

Clear A2 model

“I'm sorry. The tour will start fifteen minutes late. You can wait here or visit the information area. Thank you for your patience.”

Stretch B1 model

“I'm sorry for the delay. Our guide has contacted us, and the tour is now expected to begin fifteen minutes later than planned. In the meantime, you are welcome to explore the information area. Thank you for your understanding.”

07 / LANGUAGE TOOLKIT

Explain what will happen

Use one simple future structure to inform visitors about a change.

Grammar · will + base verb

Meaning: A future event, quick decision, offer or prediction. Form: subject + will + base verb.

Affirmative: “The tour will start at 2:15.”

Negative: “The exhibition will not close early.” / “It won't close early.”

Question: “Will the guide arrive soon?”

Exception to remember: After will, no to and no -s. For a prearranged timetable, the Present Simple is also common: “The museum opens at ten.”

« The tour will start… » = « La visite commencera… ». Will marque ici une information future, pas une habitude.

Practice immediately

The tour will ___ in fifteen minutes.

Choose the correct sentence.

08 / PRACTICE CHECKPOINT

Your heritage communication checkpoint

Try independently first. You can open the support at any time. This exercise is shorter and more guided than the official assessment.

Supportive format: No penalty for asking for help. We measure what you can do now and what to practise next. Your trainer, not this page, evaluates your spoken communication.

Mission 1 · Listen and respond

Say what you know from the programme. For the bag policy, explain that you will check.

Support · sentence starters

“Yes, the English tour…” · “For your bag, let me…”

Clear A2 model

“Yes, there is an English tour at two. For your bag, let me check the rules.”

Stretch B1 model

“Yes, we offer an English guided tour at two this afternoon. Regarding your bag, I'd like to check our policy before giving you an answer.”

Mission 2 · Pass on information

Your colleague needs a short message: a family of three asks about the English tour; one child is 12; they also need to know the bag rules. Write a short note to your colleague.

Support · writing structure

1. Who? 2. What do they need? 3. What information is missing? 4. Polite closing.

Clear A2 model

“Hello, a family of three would like the English tour at 2 p.m. Their child is 12. They also want information about bags. Could you check the rules, please? Thank you.”

Stretch B1 model

“Hello, a family of three, including a 12-year-old child, has asked about today's English guided tour at 2 p.m. They would also like confirmation of the bag policy. Could you please check this and let me know? Many thanks.”

Mission 3 · Small independent oral interview

Answer the three questions aloud. Your trainer can give follow-up questions.

  1. Why are you interested in cultural mediation?
  2. How would you welcome a visitor who speaks little French?
  3. What would you do if you did not know the answer to a visitor's question?

Stretch B1 model

“I am interested in cultural mediation because I enjoy sharing knowledge and making heritage accessible. I would welcome international visitors warmly and adapt my language. If I wasn't sure about something, I would check reliable information rather than guess.”
