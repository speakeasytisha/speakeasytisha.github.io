03 / YOUR CULTURAL ENGLISH STUDIO

Grammar 1 · BE GOING TO

First learn how to express intentions and plans; do not mix it with the next tense yet.

Meaning · When and why?

BE GOING TO = a plan or intention already in your mind

"We are going to organise a heritage workshop." = Nous allons organiser un atelier sur le patrimoine.

✓ Affirmative

I am / you are / she is + going to + base verb

She is going to invite local artists.

✕ Negative

subject + am/is/are not + going to + base verb

We aren’t going to cancel the event.

? Question

Am/Is/Are + subject + going to + base verb?

Are you going to send the invitations?

Short answers: Yes, I am. / No, she isn’t. Keywords: next week, tomorrow, this autumn, our plan, intend to.

Common mistakes: "She going to organise" → "She is going to organise"; "going to organising" → "going to organise".

Pour une intention ou un projet : sujet + am/is/are + going to + verbe à la base. On ne rajoute pas -s, -ing ou to devant le verbe principal.

Stage A · Recognise the form

We ___ going to prepare a brochure.

She is going to ___ the visitors.

Choose the correct negative sentence.

Complete the question: ___ you going to organise a workshop?

Stage B · Produce the form

She ___ going to contact the museum tomorrow.

We are going to ___ a guided tour. (organise)

Clear A2 model

I am going to organise a small exhibition. I am going to invite local artists.

Stretch B1/B1+ model

I am going to organise an exhibition about local coastal heritage. I would like to invite local artists and help international visitors discover the region’s history.

04 / YOUR CULTURAL ENGLISH STUDIO

Grammar 2 · PRESENT CONTINUOUS for the future

Now learn a second future form: activities with a concrete arrangement, often a date, time or another person.

Meaning · The arrangement is made

PRESENT CONTINUOUS = a future arrangement that has been organised

"We are meeting the exhibition designer on Thursday at ten." (A meeting has been arranged.)

✓ Affirmative

subject + am/is/are + verb-ing

I am meeting the curator on Friday.

✕ Negative

subject + am/is/are not + verb-ing

We aren’t meeting on Monday.

? Question

Am/Is/Are + subject + verb-ing?

Are you meeting the guide tomorrow?

Time expressions: tomorrow, on Thursday, next Tuesday at 11 a.m. -ing spelling: make → making (remove final -e); plan → planning (double final n); visit → visiting.

Important: The present continuous can also describe something happening now ("I am speaking now"). Here, a future time expression makes the planned meaning clear.

Pour un rendez-vous ou un arrangement fixé : am/is/are + verbe-ing. Exemple : « Je rencontre la conservatrice vendredi » = « I am meeting the curator on Friday. »

Stage A · Build the structure

We ___ meeting the artist on Tuesday.

The curator is ___ the venue tomorrow.

Choose the correct question.

The team is ___ the workshop next Friday. (plan)

Stage B · Compare a plan and an arrangement

Which sounds like a confirmed meeting?

Clear A2 model

I am meeting the curator on Monday. I am visiting the museum on Friday.

Stretch B1/B1+ model

I am meeting the curator on Monday at 10 a.m. to discuss the exhibition, and we are visiting the venue on Friday to check accessibility.

08 / YOUR CULTURAL ENGLISH STUDIO

Three live cultural mediation missions

Use the phrase bank, listen to the visitor, respond without reading, then reveal the model.

Essential communication toolkit

Open & explain

"Welcome to our heritage centre."

"We are organising…"

"The event takes place on…"

"We are meeting…"

Clarify & resolve

"Could you confirm…?"

"Let me check the arrangements."

"I’m sorry for the change."

"Would another time suit you?"

Pour chaque scénario : saluer → comprendre → reformuler → répondre → confirmer la prochaine étape.

Scenario A · Invite a partner

Situation: You are contacting a local artist to participate in Stories by the Sea. The event takes place on 21 November. Propose the 4 p.m. conversation.

Clear A2 model

We are going to organise a heritage event on 21 November. Would you like to speak at four?

Stretch B1/B1+ model

We are organising Stories by the Sea on Saturday, 21 November. We would be delighted if you could join a conversation with visitors at 4 p.m. Would you be available, and would you like more information?

Scenario B · Confirm a visit

Situation: An international visitor asks about a guided tour. Explain the programme, the meeting point still needs checking, and do not invent missing details.

Clear A2 model

Yes, the English tour starts at 2:30 p.m. It lasts 45 minutes. Let me check the meeting point.

Stretch B1/B1+ model

Certainly. The English guided tour is scheduled for Saturday, 21 November, at 2:30 p.m. and lasts 45 minutes. I will confirm the meeting point with our team rather than give you incorrect information.

Scenario C · A change of arrangement

Situation: The team meeting arranged for Tuesday at 10 a.m. needs to move to 11 a.m. Explain the change and request confirmation.

Clear A2 model

Sorry, the meeting is now at eleven on Tuesday. Is that okay?

Stretch B1/B1+ model

I’m sorry, but we need to move our Tuesday meeting from 10 to 11 a.m. Are you available at the new time? Could you confirm so I can update the schedule?

09 / YOUR CULTURAL ENGLISH STUDIO

Writing invitations and confirmations

Before writing, learn the structure, key phrases, and the difference between a proposal and a confirmed plan.

Writing toolkit

  1. Greeting · Dear Ms Brown, / Hello,
  2. Purpose · I am writing to invite you to… / I am contacting you about…
  3. Details · The event takes place on Saturday, 21 November, at…
  4. Action · Could you please confirm your availability?
  5. Closing · Thank you for your time. / Kind regards,

Useful connectors: because = parce que (reason), also = également (addition), however = cependant (contrast), therefore = par conséquent (result).

Une invitation n’est pas une réservation confirmée. Demandez une réponse avant d’annoncer que la participation est définitive.

Which line is an invitation, not a confirmation?

Which connector gives a reason?

Writing task A · Invite a speaker

Write to a local historian. Mention the fictional event on Saturday, 21 November 2026, at 4 p.m., explain why the historian would be helpful, and ask for availability.

Clear A2 model

Hello, We are going to organise a heritage event on Saturday, 21 November. Would you like to speak at four? Thank you.

Stretch B1/B1+ model

Dear Historian, I am writing to invite you to join our Stories by the Sea event on Saturday, 21 November 2026, at 4 p.m. Your knowledge of local history would help visitors understand our coastal heritage. Would you be available to take part? Kind regards,

Writing task B · Confirm with a colleague

The historian accepts. Write a short internal note confirming the 4 p.m. arrangement and indicating what the team still needs to check (equipment).

Clear A2 model

Hello, the historian is coming on Saturday at four. Could you check the equipment, please?

Stretch B1/B1+ model

Hello team, the historian has confirmed their participation in Stories by the Sea on Saturday, 21 November, at 4 p.m. We are going to check the microphone and seating arrangements before the event. Could someone confirm whether the equipment is available? Many thanks.
