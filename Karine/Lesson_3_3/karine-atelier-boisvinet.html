GRAMMAR · HIGH-VALUE LANGUAGE

Can, could, should & would like

These four forms help you move from a basic sentence to a natural conversation.

CAN + BASE VERB

A real possibility / an offer

We can check the appointment now.

I can show you two options.

can + verb ✓ no “to”

can = pouvoir / possibilité réelle.

COULD + BASE VERB

A softer suggestion / polite request

You could try a lighter style.

Could you arrive ten minutes earlier?

could + verb ✓ softer

could = pourriez / pourrait, plus doux et poli.

SHOULD + BASE VERB

A recommendation

You should use a moisturising conditioner.

You shouldn’t wash it with very hot water.

should + verb ✓ advice

should = devriez / conseil.

WOULD LIKE

A polite preference

I’d like a trim. + noun

I’d like to keep the length. + to + verb

Would you like to change the colour?

would like + noun / to + verb

would like = souhaiter / vouloir de façon polie.

Karine’s shortcut: after can / could / should, use the base verb: could try, not could to try. After would like, use a noun or to + verb. Raccourci : après can/could/should, pas de “to”. Après would like, utilisez un nom ou to + verbe.
MeaningNatural EnglishFrench cue
Possible nowWe can move your appointment.On peut…
Gentle ideaYou could add a few layers.Vous pourriez…
AdviceYou should use a gentler shampoo.Vous devriez…
PreferenceI would like to keep the length.Je voudrais…

GRAMMAR · NEW TOOL

Too & enough: say what needs to change

This structure is perfect for describing a problem and a solution: length, temperature, colour, time, price, volume or comfort.

TOO + ADJECTIVE

More than you want / a problem

The fringe is too short.

The water is too hot.

The colour is too dark for me.

too + adjective

“Trop” + adjectif : too short, too hot, too dark.

ADJECTIVE + ENOUGH

Sufficient / not sufficient

It is long enough to tie back.

The water isn’t warm enough.

The result is natural enough for work.

adjective + enough

“Assez” se place après l’adjectif : warm enough.

1. too goes before an adjective.
2. enough goes after an adjective.
3. Use not … enough for an insufficient result.
4. Add for + person: “too dark for me.”
5. Add to + verb: “long enough to tie back.”

08

SPEAKING

Three real-life missions

Aim for clarity + one follow-up question + one reason. Use the model ladder after you try first.

MISSION 1 · CONSULT

Find out what the client wants

Client: “I want a change, but I’m not sure what would suit me.”

  • Acknowledge.
  • Ask 2 useful questions.
  • Make 1 gentle suggestion.
  • Check the client’s reaction.
Hints

What would you like to change? · Have you ever…? · You could… · Would you like…?

A1 model

I understand. What would you like to change? Do you want it shorter? You could try a small trim.

A2 model

I understand. What would you like to change, the length or the style? Have you ever tried layers? You could add a few soft layers because they give more movement. Would you like to see that option?

B1 stretch model

I understand. Before we decide, could I ask what you like and dislike about your current style? Have you tried anything different recently? If you want a visible but easy change, you could add some face-framing layers. They would create movement without taking off too much length. How does that sound?

MISSION 2 · RECOMMEND

Give practical advice

Client: “My hair feels dry and it is difficult to style in the morning.”

  • Reformulate the problem.
  • Use should once.
  • Use could once.
  • Give a reason.
Hints

So, your hair feels… · You should… · You could also… · because…

A1 model

Your hair is dry. You should use conditioner. You could use a simple styling product too.

A2 model

So, your hair feels dry and it takes too long to style. You should use a moisturising conditioner, and you could also try a lighter leave-in product because it may make styling easier.

B1 stretch model

If your hair feels dry and is difficult to manage, I’d start with a moisturising routine. You should use conditioner regularly, but avoid products that are too heavy. You could also try a lightweight leave-in treatment because it can reduce styling time without making the hair flat.

MISSION 3 · SOLVE

Handle a problem calmly

Client: “I think the fringe is too short, and I’m worried about it.”

  • Show understanding.
  • Use too or enough.
  • Offer a realistic next step.
  • Do not make an impossible promise.
Hints

I understand… · It may feel too… · We can… · You could… · Let’s check…

A1 model

I understand. It feels too short for you. We can look at different ways to style it today.

A2 model

I understand why you’re worried. The fringe feels too short at the moment. We can look at a softer way to style it, and I can show you how to move it to the side while it grows.

B1 stretch model

I understand your concern. If the fringe feels too short, we can’t change the length immediately, but we can make the result feel softer. I could show you two ways to style it to the side and suggest a light product that gives enough hold without making it stiff.
