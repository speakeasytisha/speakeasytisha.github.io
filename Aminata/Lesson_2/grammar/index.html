01 · Grammar mission briefing

Your grammar must support your professional judgement.

You already know what to do in the cabin. This lesson helps you say it accurately, quickly and professionally.

✈

Your goal is control, not perfection

A strong B2 answer is not an answer without any mistakes. It is an answer that stays clear, organised and accurate enough to manage the situation. You will learn to choose the correct time, auxiliary and structure before you speak.

02 · The T.I.M.E. grammar method

03 · Grammar foundations

Be, do and have: the three engines

These verbs create most English sentence structures.

3
BE

Identity, state and continuous forms

I am available. The passenger is worried. We are checking the options.

BE sert à décrire un état, une identité ou à construire le présent continu.

DO

Present and past simple questions

Do you have an allergy? Does she need assistance? Did you request a meal?

DO/DOES/DID construisent les questions et négations au présent et au passé simples.

HAVE

Possession and present perfect

You have a connection. The crew has confirmed the information.

HAVE exprime la possession et sert d’auxiliaire au present perfect.

04 · Present simple

Routines, procedures and professional facts

Use it for what normally happens, what is true and what a role requires.

PS
USUALLY / EVERY FLIGHT / IN GENERAL

The cabin crew checks the equipment before every flight.

Positive

I/You/We/They serve.
He/She/It serves.

Remember the third-person -s.

Negative

I/You/We/They do not serve.
He/She/It does not serve.

After does not, return to the base verb.

Question

Do you serve hot meals?
Does the passenger need help?

Auxiliary + subject + base verb.

05 · Present continuous

Actions happening now and temporary situations

Use be + verb-ing when the action is in progress around the moment of speaking.

NOW
NOW / AT THE MOMENT / CURRENTLY

We are checking the ingredients at the moment.

Structure

I am checking.
You/We/They are checking.
He/She/It is checking.

Spelling

make → making
sit → sitting
lie → lying

State verbs

Usually not continuous: know, understand, need, want, prefer, believe.

Correct: “I understand your concern.”

07 · Present perfect

Past action with a result now

Use have/has + past participle when the exact finished time is not important or the result matters now.

PP

Current result or life experience.

10 · Modals and professional tone

Obligation, permission, possibility and service

A modal changes both meaning and tone.

M

must / have to

Strong obligation or procedure.

Passengers must fasten their seat belts.

can / may

Ability, permission or possibility.

May I see your boarding pass?

could / would

Polite request or preference.

Would you prefer tea or coffee?

might / may

Possibility, not certainty.

The meal may contain traces of nuts.

should

Advice or recommended action.

You should remain seated.

mustn’t / don’t have to

Prohibition versus no obligation.

You mustn’t open it. / You don’t have to move.

11 · Precision grammar

Articles, quantities and prepositions

Small words make professional English sound complete and accurate.

a

a / an

One non-specific singular countable noun.

a passenger · an allergy · an alternative

the

Specific or already identified.

the passenger in seat 4A · the purser

no article

General plural or uncountable idea.

Passengers need information. Safety is essential.

Countable

meal/meals, passenger/passengers, request/requests

many requests · a few options

Uncountable

information, assistance, advice, luggage, water

much information · a little water

Professional fix

an item of luggage · a piece of information · some advice

Place

on board · at the gate · in the cabin · in seat 4A

Time

during the flight · before take-off · after landing · at 3 p.m.

Professional combinations

interested in · responsible for · worried about · available to
