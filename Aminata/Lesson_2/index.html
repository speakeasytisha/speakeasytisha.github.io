02 · Interaction technique

The F.L.I.G.H.T. method

A repeatable process for passenger care, LILATE interaction and professional problem-solving.

F

Before speaking: run a 5-second scan

WHO?

Passenger, colleague, recruiter or supervisor?

WHAT?

Request, complaint, concern, restriction or emergency?

WHEN?

Now, earlier, during booking or later?

RESULT?

What does the person need from you?

F
Focus on key information

Listen for the person, request, seat, timing, restriction, urgency and desired outcome. Write keywords, not complete sentences.

seat 4A · vegetarian meal · nut allergy · wrong tray
L
Locate the real need

The first sentence may not contain the whole problem. Separate the main request from useful background details.

Main need: safe replacement meal. Secondary need: reassurance.
I
Investigate precisely

Ask one question at a time. Start with the information that affects safety or the solution.

“Do you have any other food allergies?”
G
Give a reformulation

Summarise the request in your own words and ask for confirmation.

“So, you requested a vegetarian meal and you need a nut-free alternative. Is that correct?”
H
Help with a realistic solution

Say what you can do, what you need to check and when you will return.

“Let me verify the ingredients and check the available alternatives.”
T
Thank, confirm and close

Confirm the next step and make sure the passenger knows what will happen.

“I’ll return in a few minutes. Thank you for your patience.”
The perfect interaction sequence

Do not jump directly to the solution

A rushed answer often misses important information. A professional answer moves through six controlled stages.

1 Welcome2 Listen3 Clarify4 Reformulate5 Solve6 Confirm
Worked exampleA passenger says: “My meal is wrong and I have an allergy.”

Good afternoon. I’m sorry about the mistake. May I ask which meal you originally requested and what you are allergic to? So, you requested a vegetarian meal and you have a nut allergy. Is that correct? I’ll remove this tray and check the ingredients of the available alternatives before serving anything else. I’ll come back to you as soon as I have confirmed a safe option. Thank you for letting me know.

Professional phrases when you did not understand

“I’m sorry, could you repeat the last part, please?”

“Could you speak a little more slowly, please?”

“When you say ‘special meal’, do you mean a dietary meal?”

“Let me make sure I have understood correctly.”

“Could you clarify whether this is an allergy or a preference?”

Quick check — What should happen first?

A passenger says that the seat is uncomfortable and mentions recent back surgery.

04 · Precise professional questions

Ask the question that unlocks the solution

Correct question structure protects safety, saves time and shows professional control.

?
DO

Routine or fact

Do/Does + subject + base verb?
  • Do you have any allergies?
  • Does this medication need refrigeration?
ARE

Now or current situation

Am/Is/Are + subject + verb-ing?
  • Are you experiencing any pain?
  • Is your child travelling alone?
DID

Finished past event

Did + subject + base verb?
  • Did you request the meal during booking?
  • When did the problem begin?
HAVE

Past connected to now

Have/Has + subject + past participle?
  • Have you informed another crew member?
  • Has the passenger taken any medication?

Premium-service upgrade

Direct questions can be grammatically correct but sound abrupt. Use a polite frame when appropriate.

Basic: What do you want?Professional: May I ask what you would prefer?
Basic: Give me your seat number.Professional: Could you confirm your seat number, please?
